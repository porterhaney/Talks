# OIA Global: Company Profile, History and Ownership (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-09. Every fact below carries the date of its source. "Self-reported" means taken from OIA's own website or press releases. Third-party revenue numbers are estimates, not reported financials.

**Framing correction for the report writer:** The brief calls OIA a "packaging supply chain" company. The sources do not support that. OIA Global is mainly an asset-light freight forwarder and 3PL/4PL: air, ocean and road freight, customs brokerage, contract logistics and project logistics. Packaging design and procurement, plus raw-materials sourcing, sit inside a "Supply Chain Management (SCM)" segment, and that segment is one line among several. Its SCM roots are real: the company launched Creative Packaging Solutions in 2005 and bought PACCESS's materials-sourcing division in 2013. But every acquisition since 2012 has been a freight-forwarding or customs-brokerage business.

## 1. Business lines and how each one makes money

### Takeaway
OIA has two operating segments. The first is **Freight Forwarding** (air, ocean, road, customs brokerage, contract and project logistics), now run by the COO. The second is **Supply Chain Management** (packaging solutions, raw-materials management and sourcing, 4PL orchestration). In October 2025 the COO's role (Freight Forwarding plus Global Business Services) was split from the SCM head's role. OIA does not disclose how revenue or margin splits between segments.

### Cited Findings
- Segment structure as of Oct 14, 2025: Brandon Ruggles was named COO, leading "the Freight Forwarding business segment and Global Business Services". He had been SVP of Supply Chain Management, "overseeing packaging solutions, raw material management, and 4PL orchestration". — [EIN Presswire, Oct 14 2025](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/858153394/oia-global-names-brandon-ruggles-chief-operating-officer)
- Service lines listed on the homepage (accessed Oct 2026): air, ocean and road freight; contract logistics (warehousing and distribution); packaging solutions and packaging design; raw materials management and sourcing; project logistics; 3PL/4PL supply chain orchestration; customs brokerage and foreign trade zones; purchase-order management; supply chain and shipment visibility; carbon reporting. — [oiaglobal.com](https://www.oiaglobal.com/)
- Service details from OIA's llms.txt (self-reported, accessed Oct 2026):
  - Air freight: IATA agent covering 100+ countries.
  - Road freight: network of 3,000+ vetted carriers, covering FTL, LTL, last-mile and cold chain.
  - Contract logistics: WMS, dropshipping, reverse logistics.
  - Customs brokerage: a "control tower network of 6 regional teams".
  - Raw materials management: 775,000+ units per hour of order processing, with a specialization in athletic and footwear materials.
  - Project logistics: heavy-lift and energy work (wind, solar, BESS, oil and gas, data centers).
  - 4PL: one example customer has 20+ ocean carriers, 30+ loading sites and 150+ delivery sites under management.
  - Source: [oiaglobal.com/llms.txt](https://www.oiaglobal.com/llms.txt)
- Packaging solutions (self-reported): about 350 projects a year covering structural and retail packaging design, transport-packaging optimization, fulfillment, color management, production samples and retail POP displays. Case studies cite 5,000+ POP displays and a 9% saving in packaging fiber. — [oiaglobal.com/llms.txt](https://www.oiaglobal.com/llms.txt)
- Packaging/SCM history:
  - 2005: Creative Packaging Solutions (CPS) launched.
  - 2008: footwear distribution launched in Portland.
  - 2013: OIA acquired the Materials Sourcing Division assets of PACCESS LLC.
  - Source: [oiaglobal.com/who-we-are](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/)
- LDI describes OIA as having grown "from a freight forwarder into a full-service supply chain management company" that offers "sourcing, packaging, storage, and shipping of goods". The same page notes that one add-on was "a materials sourcing division bought from a competitor". — [LDI portfolio page](https://lacydiversified.com/portfolio/oia-global/)
- 2011 description: freight forwarding, supply chain management, turnkey contract warehousing, and packaging. — [OIA press release, Aug 31 2011](https://www.oiaglobal.com/?p=394)
- 2020 description: "global logistics, packaging, and sourcing provider". — [Air Freight News, Aug 11 2020](https://airfreight.news/articles/full/oia-global-welcomes-barrie-as-ceo)
- Technology platforms (self-reported):
  - OIA Connect for visibility. It is hosted at oiaconnect.orkestrascs.com, which suggests it is built on Orkestra SCS.
  - TMS and WMS.
  - 3GTMS for booking and quoting.
  - Sources: [oiaglobal.com](https://www.oiaglobal.com/); [llms.txt](https://www.oiaglobal.com/llms.txt)

### Inferences
- Revenue models (inferred from standard industry economics; OIA has not disclosed any of this):
  - Freight forwarding is booked as gross revenue (purchased transportation passed through), with net revenue / gross profit typically in the mid-teens to about 20% of gross.
  - Customs brokerage earns per-entry fees.
  - Contract logistics earns storage and handling fees.
  - Packaging and raw-materials procurement are most likely buy/resell, earning a product margin on goods bought from converters and mills, with design fees on top.
  - 4PL earns management fees.
  - A DCF should model each segment on a net-revenue basis.
- The OIA Connect URL (orkestrascs.com) points to a licensed third-party visibility platform rather than proprietary IP. This is relevant to any "tech premium" argument.
- The raw-materials "athletic and footwear" specialization, the Portland HQ and the Nike case study together suggest the SCM segment depends heavily on the Portland athletic/footwear cluster.

### Gaps
- Revenue, gross profit and EBITDA by segment are not disclosed anywhere I could find.
- The packaging monetization model (resale margin vs. fee) is not stated by OIA.
- I found no Packaging World or trade-press coverage that quantifies the packaging business's size.

## 4. Ownership history and LDI (Lacy)

### Takeaway
Junki Yoshida founded OIA in Portland in 1988 as Oregon International Air Freight. LDI Ltd., LLC (formerly Lacy Diversified Industries, the Lacy family's fourth-generation holding company in Indianapolis) recapitalized OIA and took majority control. The deal was announced Aug 31, 2011, while LDI and OIA date it to 2010. Terms were never disclosed. LDI is a long-hold family office, and I found no public exit signal.

### Cited Findings
- Founding: Oregon International Air Freight was incorporated in Portland in 1988. It took the name and logo "OIA Global Logistics" on July 1, 1999, and shortened the name to "OIA Global" in 2011. — [oiaglobal.com/who-we-are](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/)
- LDI deal announcement, Aug 31, 2011:
  - LDI Ltd., LLC, "a private holding company in Indianapolis focused on distribution and logistics", recapitalized Oregon International Air Freight Co. and OIA Global Logistics-SCM, Inc. and acquired majority control.
  - Founder Junki Yoshida stayed on as Chairman. Charles Hornecker joined as CEO.
  - Yoshida said: "Our partnership with LDI gives us greater resources to grow with our customers."
  - Andre Lacy (LDI Chairman) said LDI's long-term strategy fits OIA's growth vision.
  - No price was disclosed.
  - Source: [OIA, Aug 31 2011](https://www.oiaglobal.com/?p=394)
- Date conflict: the OIA timeline says "2010: LDI Ltd., LLC acquired majority control" ([oiaglobal.com/who-we-are](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/)). LDI's page says "Held since 2010" and "acquired majority ownership from the founders" ([LDI](https://lacydiversified.com/portfolio/oia-global/)). The press release is dated Aug 31, 2011 ([OIA](https://www.oiaglobal.com/?p=394)). Likely explanation: the deal was signed or agreed in late 2010 and announced or closed in 2011. This is unverified.
- LDI says it "augmented OIA's management team with an industry veteran" and that OIA has made five add-ons since 2012, including a materials-sourcing division bought from a competitor. The page has not been updated for the 2017–2026 deals. — [LDI](https://lacydiversified.com/portfolio/oia-global/)
- OIA calls itself "privately owned by LDI, Ltd., a family office with over a century of experience in funding and operating middle-market companies". — [oiaglobal.com/who-we-are](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/)
- LDI background (IBJ, Feb 20 2025):
  - LDI was formerly Lacy Diversified Industries. It is a fourth-generation family holding company whose roots go back to U.S. Corrugated Fiber Box Co., co-founded in 1912 by Howard J. Lacy Sr.
  - It holds six portfolio companies. Three are owned: Polygon Composites Technology (Walkerton, IN), OIA Global (Portland), and Acme Barricades (Jacksonville, FL). Three are minority stakes: Genesis Rail Co., UltiMed Inc., and Sanitary Solutions Inc.
  - Mitch Daniels (former Indiana governor and Purdue president) is LDI's first executive partner and joined in the second half of 2024.
  - LDI plans to grow the portfolio "pretty significantly over the next five years", with a focus on Indiana-based companies.
  - Amelia Patel is chief of staff.
  - Source: [IBJ, Feb 20 2025](https://www.ibj.com/articles/mitch-daniels-joins-indianapolis-based-ldi-ltd-as-executive-partner)
- J.A. Lacy was OIA's board chairman in Aug 2020 ([Air Freight News](https://airfreight.news/articles/full/oia-global-welcomes-barrie-as-ceo)). Aggregators describe J.A. Lacy as LDI's Board Chair and CEO, fourth generation (this is a secondary summary from [Inforcapital](https://inforcapital.com/companies/lacy-diversified/) via search snippet).
- Investment criteria, from secondary or aggregator sources (low confidence):
  - Majority stakes in B2B industrial companies with EBITDA of roughly $3–15M ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/ldis)) or $2–10M+ (Inforcapital).
  - LDI's chief of staff is quoted that they "hold for 10-plus years".
  - Inforcapital claims the portfolio generates more than $1.2B in revenue and employs more than 1,500 people.
  - All of these come from search snippets only and were not verified against primary sources.
- An older Transport Topics piece (on the ACE deal, about 2017) said OIA was one of six companies controlled by Lacy Diversified Industries. — [Transport Topics](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/oia-global-acquires-american-cargo-express)

### Inferences
- LDI has held OIA for about 15–16 years (2010/11 to 2026). That is consistent with a permanent-capital family office, not a fund with an exit deadline.
- OIA is almost certainly LDI's largest holding by revenue. If OIA's self-reported revenue of "$1B+" is accurate, it would be most of the "$1.2B" portfolio revenue that aggregators cite.
- The 2025 IBJ statement about adding Indiana companies, followed by OIA buying Cargo Services in Indianapolis in April 2026, points to some LDI-driven deal sourcing.
- The founder Junki Yoshida is likely the same Portland entrepreneur behind Yoshida's sauce and the Yoshida Group. That is general knowledge and was not verified in these sources.

### Gaps
- Deal terms, valuation and any remaining founder or management equity were never disclosed.
- I did not pull the Oregon or Indiana Secretary of State filings.
- I could not confirm whether LDI is the 100% owner today or whether management holds rollover or incentive equity.
- No LDI exit history was found. I found no documented sales of past portfolio companies.

## 8. Size, financial indicators, certifications and licenses

### Takeaway
OIA self-reports "$1B+" revenue a year. That figure is probably gross revenue, including purchased transportation, and is undated. Third-party estimates range from $359M to $732M. OIA holds the standard US forwarder licenses: CBP customs broker, FMC OTI/NVOCC, IATA/CNS, C-TPAT, AEO and ISO 9001.

### Inferences
- Taking about $1B gross revenue and about 1,200 employees gives roughly $830K gross revenue per employee, which is plausible for a forwarder that books revenue gross. Assuming a typical 15–20% net-revenue margin, net revenue would be roughly $150–200M. This is an unverified analyst estimate, not a disclosed figure.
- Scope 1 and 2 emissions of only about 1,366 MT confirm a very light fixed-asset base: no owned fleet or aircraft, with leased warehouses. That implies low maintenance capex in a DCF.
