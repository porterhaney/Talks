# OIA Global: Public Financial Data Points for a DCF (revenue, headcount, debt, margins), with Reliability Notes

Scope: OIA Global (legal entities Oregon International Air Freight Co. and OIA Global Logistics-SCM, Inc.), Portland, OR. Majority owned by LDI Ltd., LLC (Lacy Diversified Industries), Indianapolis, since August 2010. Research as of 9 October 2026.

Reliability legend used below:
- **CONFIRMED-3P**: a third-party ranking built from company-submitted data (Transport Topics). This is the best public revenue source available, but it is unaudited.
- **COMPANY CLAIM**: OIA or LDI marketing copy or press-release boilerplate. These figures are rounded and promotional.
- **GOVT DATA**: federal records (SBA PPP FOIA data).
- **ESTIMATE**: modeled numbers from data aggregators (ZoomInfo, Owler, Growjo and similar). These are low reliability.

Transport Topics' Top 100 Logistics lists are the only year-by-year public revenue series. They show OIA's gross revenue at **$876M (2020 data), $1,156M (2021), $1,191M (2022), $732M (2023), ~$770M (2024 estimate) and $779M (latest; the profile says "data through Dec 31, 2024", though the list convention implies 2025)**. The 2021–2022 rise and the 2023 drop of about 39% match the freight-rate spike and its unwinding.

### Inferences
- **Best public revenue anchor for a DCF base year: about $780M gross revenue** (TT, latest list). Treat it as roughly 2024–2025 because the year label is ambiguous; see Gaps.
- **Freight-spike distortion is clear.** Gross revenue rose 32% from 2020 to 2021 ($876M to $1,156M) and another 3% in 2022 ($1,191M), then fell 39% in 2023 ($732M). This pattern, a peak in 2021–22 and a collapse in 2023, matches the ocean and air rate cycle. Pre-spike (2020) and post-spike (2023–25) gross revenue are similar, at about $730–880M, so normalized gross revenue sits around $750–850M.
- 2018–2019 data year: OIA was absent from the 2019–2020 TT Top 50 lists, which were ranked by net revenue. That suggests OIA's net revenue was below about $245–267M then, or that OIA did not submit data. It says nothing about gross revenue.
- **The net revenue series is unreliable for modeling.** Net-to-gross ratios jump around: 19% (2020, estimate), 15% (2021), 21% (2022), 50% (2023), 49% (2024, estimate), 26% (latest). A 50% ratio is implausible for a forwarding-heavy business unless definitions changed, for example if packaging or warehousing was booked on a different basis or TT applied a different definition. The **$202M latest net revenue** (26% of gross, not flagged as an estimate) is the most plausible single figure and is consistent with a forwarder that has a meaningful value-added packaging and warehousing mix.
- **The company's $1.0–1.4B claims conflict with TT and with LDI.** LDI's April 2026 summary puts *all* platform companies plus minority investments at "over $1.2 billion" in revenue, so OIA cannot be $1.3–1.4B on a like-for-like basis. The company figures likely reflect peak-cycle 2021–22 revenue (TT $1.16–1.19B), or a broader measure such as freight or spend under management. The "$1 billion" boilerplate predates the spike (2017–2020), while TT showed $876M for 2020, so the company figure appears rounded up.
- A CAGR from public data is only possible over 2020–latest: $876M to $779M, about −2% per year in gross terms. Since 2024, growth has come mainly from acquisitions (BTi Logistics and Sandford Freight Ireland in 2024, JF Moran in 2025, About Freight Worldwide and Cargo Services in 2026; see section 5).

### Gaps
- **Not publicly available:** audited or management financial statements, revenue by segment (forwarding, packaging/sourcing, warehousing, customs), gross profit, EBITDA, net income, and any revenue figure for 2005–2019.
- The Portland Business Journal "largest private companies" list, the Indianapolis Business Journal private-company list and the Armstrong & Associates forwarder lists were not accessible (paywalled) or did not surface OIA, so no figures could be retrieved from them. The JOC Top 25 trans-Pacific NVO lists did not show OIA in the results reviewed. No Inc. 5000 listing was found; OIA's size makes one unlikely.
- The year label on the latest TT figure is ambiguous: the profile says "Data through Dec 31, 2024" even though it appears on the 2026 list.
- Whether the TT figures consolidate all international subsidiaries (UK/EU via Bellville Rodair, Asia, ANZ) or only North America is not stated.

## 2. Lacy Diversified Industries (LDI) consolidated revenue, employees, and OIA's share

### Takeaway
LDI says its platform companies plus minority investments generate "over $1.2 billion" in revenue and employ "1,500+" people (April 2026). On TT's ~$780M, OIA would be about 65% of that revenue and roughly 80% of the headcount. LDI's 2013 revenue was "more than $1 billion", but that predates the 2014 Tucker Rocky/MAG motorcycle merger and included the motorcycle business.

### Cited Findings
- "Our current platform companies and minority investments generate over $1.2 billion in revenue and employ more than 1,500 professionals worldwide." Platform companies: global supply chain solutions provider, Portland, OR (OIA, partnered 2010); composite components manufacturer, Walkerton, IN (2018); highway and traffic safety services, Jacksonville, FL (2023); building products distributor, Indianapolis (2025, Dealers Wholesale). Minority investments: life science research products (2014), drug injection devices (2021), railroad services (2024), repurposed waste tires/plastics (2025). — [LDI Two-Page Summary, April 2026 (PDF)](https://lacydiversified.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LDI-Two-Page-Summary-2026.04.pdf)
- LDI's stated target for new investments is "EBITDA between $3 and $15 million". This is an investment criterion for *new* deals, not an OIA metric. — [LDI Two-Page Summary 2026](https://lacydiversified.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LDI-Two-Page-Summary-2026.04.pdf)
- IBJ, June 5, 2014: a "substantial portion" of "LDI's 2013 revenue of more than $1 billion" came from its motorcycle business. The article also says LDI "also owns 800-employee OIA Global Logistics." An investment firm valued the combined MAG plus Tucker Rocky entity at about $382.5M; that figure is a valuation, not revenue. — [IBJ, 5 Jun 2014](https://www.ibj.com/articles/47979-merger-forms-382m-indy-based-motorcycle-parts-company)

- ZoomInfo lists LDI revenue at $5.8M, evidently only the holding company or family office. — [ZoomInfo LDI](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/ldi-ltd/35686032)
- 2010 deal: LDI Ltd., LLC "recapitalized" Oregon International Air Freight Co. and OIA Global Logistics-SCM, Inc. and "acquired majority control" (Portland, Aug 31, 2010). ### Inferences
- **OIA is LDI's dominant asset.** With about 1,200 of LDI's 1,500+ employees (TT 2026 list) and about $780M of its $1.2B+ revenue, OIA is roughly 80% of headcount and 60–65% of aggregated revenue. The $1.2B appears to include minority investees' revenue on a gross (non-proportionate) basis, so it is not true consolidated revenue.
- LDI's "$1.2B" ceiling is strong evidence against OIA's "$1.3–1.4B" claims as current recurring revenue.
- The IBJ article is the only independent source tying OIA to a specific headcount before 2017 (800 in 2014).
- Ownership note: the TT profile says "Stock: LDI Ltd.", and the 2010 release describes "majority control", so founders or management may hold a minority stake. That matters for equity-value allocation, but no cap-table details are public.

### Gaps
- No IBJ "largest private companies" entry with an LDI revenue figure was retrieved; the list is paywalled.
- LDI's percentage ownership of OIA today, and whether any founder or management equity remains, is not disclosed.
- LDI does not publish consolidated financials.

### Inferences
- PPP first-draw loans were sized at roughly 2.5× average monthly payroll, with per-employee pay capped at $100K (program rule, general knowledge). That implies annual payroll of about $4.6M at JF Moran Co. ($951,660 × 12 / 2.5) and about $2.0M at CSI around 2019–2020. These are tuck-ins of roughly 30–70 people each, small relative to OIA's ~1,200 employees and ~$780M revenue.
- JF Moran's second-draw loan implies, under program rules, that it had 300 or fewer employees and at least a 25% drop in gross receipts in a 2020 quarter versus 2019. That shows the COVID trough for a US customs and forwarding peer.
- Revenue per employee on TT figures: about $650K gross and about $168K net per head (latest, $779M or $202M over 1,200), versus about $880K gross per head at the 2022 peak. This is a useful sanity check for any forecast that pairs headcount with revenue.
- The different headcount figures (1,100+ on the website, 1,200 on TT, 1,300+ on LinkedIn) probably reflect different snapshot dates and whether agents or contractors are counted. About 1,200 is the most defensible current figure.

### Gaps
- No EEO-1, WARN-notice or state payroll data for OIA was found.
- The SBA files for loans under $150K were not searched. That is low relevance, since OIA would have qualified for a larger loan if it had been eligible.
- Headcount by function or geography (US vs. international) is not public.

## 4. Credit facilities, bank loans, UCC filings, debt disclosures

### Takeaway
**No public debt information was found**: no disclosed credit facility, lender, term loan, bond or covenant. The 2010 transaction was a "recapitalization", and PitchBook classifies it as a buyout/LBO, which suggests acquisition debt at that time. Current leverage is unknown.

### Cited Findings
- The 2010 deal is described as a "recapitalization" with LDI acquiring "majority control"; financing was not disclosed. — [LDI release 2010](https://lacydiversified.com/acquisition-and-recapitalization-of-oia-global-logistics/)
- PitchBook lists OIA as "Private Equity-Backed" with latest deal type "Buyout/LBO" and investor LDI (search snippet). — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/58211-29)
- LDI markets a "flexible capital structure with future cash available for growth" and a "build-and-hold" model. — [LDI Two-Page Summary 2026](https://lacydiversified.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LDI-Two-Page-Summary-2026.04.pdf)
- A web search for "OIA Global" with credit facility, line of credit, lender, senior debt or refinancing returned nothing relevant. — [web search, Oct 2026; no source returned]

### Inferences
- Forwarders typically run an asset-based revolver against receivables, because they pay carriers and duties before collecting from customers. A working-capital revolver at OIA is likely but **unconfirmed**. Customs-bond and FMC NVOCC bonding obligations also exist; OIA has held an NVOCC licence since 1994 and a customs broker licence since 1992/93 per [OIA Who We Are](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/), but bond amounts are not material to a DCF.
- The 2024–2026 acquisitions (five deals in about 2.5 years) were funded by some undisclosed mix of cash flow, LDI equity and debt.

### Gaps
- **UCC filings were not retrieved.** The Oregon Secretary of State UCC search needs interactive access. Searching it for debtor "Oregon International Air Freight Co." and "OIA Global Logistics-SCM, Inc." would identify secured lenders and is the recommended next step.
- No debt amounts, interest rates, maturities, covenants, operating-lease obligations or capex figures are public.

## 6. Margin benchmarks (brief; comps covered in depth by another researcher)

### Takeaway
For a forwarder-led 3PL, EBITDA usually runs about 2–6% of *gross* revenue, or roughly 15–30% of *net* revenue (gross profit). Packaging and sourcing management adds a lower-margin distribution-style component. The public sources found here are thin, mostly secondary and directional only.

### Cited Findings
- A Panjiva piece (undated) put Panalpina's EBITDA margin at 2.8% of revenue versus a sector average of 6.1%. — [Panjiva](https://panjiva.com/research/panalpinas-11th-miss-shows-fierce-forwarder-competition/12328)
- Forward Air's Omni (forwarding) segment reported about a 10% EBITDA margin in Q4 2025 (search summary). — [Business Wire, Forward Air FY2025 results](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260223387979/en/Forward-Air-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results)
- RXO bought Coyote for $1.025B, cited as 0.32× gross revenue, 2.2× gross margin and 11.9× EBITDA (vendor blog). That implies EBITDA of about 2.7% of gross revenue and about 18% of net revenue (derived arithmetic). — [Peony blog](https://www.peony.ink/blog/best-logistics-ma-advisors)
- Logistics M&A context: Peakstone puts the median logistics EV/EBITDA at 9.2× (2025). MCF cites 6.5–8.5× EBITDA for €100M–1B mid-market logistics deals (Spring 2024). — [Peakstone Q4 2025 via Axial](https://www.axial.net/forum/industry-report-transportation-logistics-q4-2025-peakstone/); [MCF Spring 2024](https://www.mcfcorpfin.com/wp-content/uploads/MCF-MA-Logistics-Insights-Spring-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative EBITDA range (an inference, not data):** applying 15–30% of the latest TT net revenue ($202M) gives about $30–60M EBITDA. Applying 3–6% of gross ($779M) gives about $23–47M. Together these suggest a plausible normalized EBITDA band of roughly **$25–55M**, with 2021–22 likely well above it because of spot-rate windfalls. Treat this as a sanity range only.
- Because the net revenue series is erratic (15–50% of gross), base margin assumptions on gross revenue, or on the $202M latest net revenue, rather than on the 2023–2024 TT net figures.

### Gaps
- No packaging-procurement or packaging-distribution margin comparables were collected here (for example Veritiv or TricorBraun-type businesses); these are deferred to the comps researcher.
- OIA's actual gross margin, EBITDA and segment mix are not public.
