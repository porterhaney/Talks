# Comparable M&A Transactions and Valuation Multiples for a 2026 Sale of OIA Global

Research date: 9 October 2026. Every multiple carries its date. Labels used throughout:
- **[D]** Disclosed: stated by a party to the deal, its filings, or its advisor.
- **[R]** Reported: from press or a data aggregator, not confirmed by the parties.
- **[E]** Estimated: my own arithmetic from the cited inputs. It is not a published number.

## Q1. Packaging distribution / packaging supply chain management deals, 2018–2026

### Takeaway
Few packaging distribution deals disclose their multiples. The best anchor is Veritiv's purchase of Orora Packaging Solutions (Landsberg) in 2024 at about 9.9x cash EBITDA [D], which the seller described as a premium. At the low end, CD&R took Veritiv private in 2023 at about 5–6x adjusted EBITDA [E, derived from the proxy]. Older platform deals, such as Berlin Packaging in 2014 at about 14x [R], show what scaled, high-growth packaging platforms can command. Capstone's packaging-sector EV/EBITDA fell from 10.3x in FY2025 to 6.5x for 2026 year to date.

### Cited Findings
- On 4 Sep 2024, Orora agreed to sell OPS to Veritiv for A$1.775bn on a cash and debt free basis, about 9.9x FY24A cash EBITDA, which it called a premium to its own trading multiple and to comparable transactions [D] — [Orora ASX announcement](https://ororagroup.com/system/downloads/files/000/000/982/original/2771985.pdf?1725403361=); [Jarden](https://www.jardengroup.com/case-studies/sale-of-orora-packaging-solutions)
- In the 2023 Veritiv/CD&R deal at $170/share, Morgan Stanley's fairness analysis used AV/2023E adj. EBITDA ranges of 4.5x–6.5x and AV/2024E ranges of 4.25x–6.25x. The unaffected Street-case multiples were 4.7x (2023E) and 5.2x (2024E), with net debt of $151m [D] — [Veritiv DEFM14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1599489/000110465923101407/tm2325633-8_defm14a.htm)
- Veritiv's 2022 adjusted EBITDA was $518m, a 7.2% margin. Press widely cited a value of about $2.3bn, but the proxy figure was about $2.6bn aggregate — [MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/featured/featured-blog/cdr-to-buy-packaging-jansan-distributor-veritiv-for-2-3b/); [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1599489/000110465923108254/tm2328129d1_8k.htm)
- Oak Hill paid about 14x EBITDA (~$100m) for Berlin Packaging in 2014 [R] — [Private Equity International](https://www.privateequityinternational.com/oak-hill-capital-acquires-berlin-packaging-for-1-43bn/)
- Capstone Partners put packaging-sector EV/EBITDA at 10.3x for full-year 2025, falling to 6.5x for 2026 year to date. That is nearly three turns below the 2018–2026 average of 9.3x. Deal count through Q2 2026 was down 21.3% year on year. This index covers mostly packaging producers, not distributors — [Packaging Dive, citing Capstone](https://www.packagingdive.com/news/capstone-partners-packaging-acquisitions-muted-2026/826759/); [Capstone Oct 2025 packaging report](https://www.capstonepartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Capstone-Partners_Packaging_MA-Coverage-Report_October-2025.pdf)
- Capstone's warehousing and fulfillment multiples (the nearest adjacent category) averaged 15.5x in 2019–2021 and 12.4x from 2022 to year-to-date 2025. Large deals drive these averages — [Capstone W&F update](https://www.capstonepartners.com/insights/article-warehousing-fulfillment-ma-update/)
- Bunzl management said its acquisition multiples have averaged about 8x operating profit over the past decade, with a target of 6–8x [D] — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bunzl-h2-earnings-call-highlights-112613712.html)
- The 2011 OIA announcement listed its services as freight forwarding, supply chain management, contract warehousing, and packaging and design fulfillment. This is the packaging-plus-logistics mix that sets it apart from pure distributors — [OIA Global news](https://www.oiaglobal.com/?p=394)

### Inferences
- OPS/Landsberg is the closest disclosed transaction comp for OIA's packaging SCM segment. It is an asset-light packaging distributor with design and procurement services. It traded at ~9.9x cash EBITDA, but it was a ~US$1.2bn EV asset with ~US$1bn+ revenue sold to a strategic. A business of OIA's size should expect a 2–4 turn discount to that.
- Taken together, Veritiv (5–6x, 2023), Bunzl's bolt-on discipline (6–8x EBIT, so roughly 5–7x EBITDA), and OPS (9.9x) suggest a range of about **6–9x EBITDA** for packaging distribution and procurement businesses in 2023–2026. A specialty, design-led, sticky-contract business can push the upper end.
- Strategic consolidators are the likely buyers for OIA's packaging side: Veritiv/CD&R, TricorBraun, Berlin, BradyIFS, SupplyOne, Bunzl and similar. All of them are active acquirers in 2025–2026.

### Gaps
- I found no disclosed multiples for TricorBraun (Ares, 2021), Imperial Dade (Bain/Advent), BradyIFS (Kelso/Warburg), Berlin Packaging recapitalizations after 2014 (e.g., Canada Pension Plan Investment Board, 2018), Pratt, Atlas, or the 2025–2026 tuck-ins. Any multiples for these require PitchBook or CapIQ.
- I found no public deals or multiples for outsourced packaging-procurement specialists such as Visual Packaging, Pak2000, Arka, PakFactory, Packlane or Ernest Packaging. Searches returned nothing usable.
- No packaging deal disclosed EV/gross profit.
- Ranpak is a protective-packaging manufacturer, not a distributor (see Q3).

## Q2. Freight forwarding / logistics deals, 2021–2026

### Takeaway
Large forwarders have changed hands at about **0.7–1.0x revenue** and **13–23x EBIT**. On EBITDA, the reported figure is about 7.5x for Schenker (2024E, includes leases). C.H. Robinson's 2026 bid for RXO is 13.2x synergized 2026E EBITDA, but roughly 40x+ unsynergized because RXO's EBITDA is at a cyclical trough. Surveys of mid-market asset-light forwarding put private multiples at about **8–14x for strategics**, while PE pays far less: 8.1x versus 13.7x for strategics in H2 2025. Size discounts are steep.

### Inferences
- Revenue multiples for forwarders that report gross revenue cluster at **0.4–1.0x** for scaled global networks. A mid-market US forwarder with lower margins and no proprietary network would sit well below that, at roughly **0.2–0.5x gross revenue**. EV/revenue is a weak anchor for OIA because freight revenue is largely pass-through. EV/EBITDA or EV/gross profit (net revenue) is the better basis.
- EBITDA in 2025–2026 is at or near a cyclical trough for many forwarders and brokers. Large buyers pay on synergized or normalized EBITDA: CHRW/RXO is 13.2x synergized against ~42x headline. For OIA, the sale process should present normalized and through-cycle EBITDA, not just trough-year LTM figures.
- Strategic buyers are paying 4–5 turns more than PE in 2025 (Proventis: 13.7x vs 8.1x). A sale process that brings in strategics, such as Radiant, mid-cap forwarders or packaging consolidators, matters for value.

### Gaps
- The Raymond James Nov 2025 "Executive Freight Trends" PDF has a "Forwarding and logistics" multiple series, but the fetch returned HTTP 503.

- I did not retrieve Tenney Group, Benchmark International, Ti Insight or Armstrong & Associates freight-forwarder multiple surveys in this pass.
- No forwarder deal I found disclosed EV/gross profit.

## Q4. Private mid-market multiple ranges (2024–2026): size discount, freight downcycle, tariffs

### Takeaway
Private-equity purchases of $10–100M TEV businesses in 2025 averaged about **6.4–8.3x EBITDA** (GF Data). Distribution specifically was at **6.0x in H1 2026** (7.0x year to date in 2025). In logistics, strategics paid about 13.7x and sponsors about 8.1x in H2 2025. For a $5–30M EBITDA asset-light hybrid like OIA in 2026, a central range of about **6.5–9x adjusted EBITDA** looks supportable. A strategic premium, or a differentiated packaging SCM book with contracted revenue, could justify about 10–11x. A trough-EBITDA forwarding-heavy mix could fall to about 5–6x.

### Cited Findings
- GF Data, PE-sponsored deals: average multiples in 2025 year to date (9 months) were 6.4x for $10–25M TEV, 6.8x for $25–50M, and 8.3x for $50–100M. ### Inferences: indicative 2026 multiple framework for OIA (my synthesis, not sourced)

| Scenario | EV/EBITDA (adjusted, normalized) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Downside: PE buyer, forwarding-heavy EBITDA at trough, customer concentration | ~5.0–6.5x | GF Data $10–25M at 6.4x; distribution at 6.0x in H1 2026; PE logistics median 5.4–8.1x |
| Base: PE platform or mid-cap strategic, balanced mix, $10–20M EBITDA | ~6.5–9.0x | GF Data $25–100M at 6.8–8.3x; Bunzl bolt-on discipline; Veritiv 5–6x at the low end and OPS 9.9x at the high end |
| Upside: strategic (packaging consolidator or forwarder) with synergies; packaging SCM shown as contracted, recurring and design-led | ~9–11x+ | OPS 9.9x; Proventis strategic 13.7x and mid cap 11.2x; CHRW/RXO 13.2x synergized |

- **Sum-of-the-parts view:** apply about 7–10x to the packaging SCM EBITDA (contracted, sticky, design and inventory services, comparable to OPS and Berlin-type businesses) and about 5–8x to the forwarding EBITDA (cyclical, commoditized, earn-out-heavy deal structures, as in Radiant's tuck-ins). A blended multiple near 7–8.5x is a reasonable midpoint if the mix is roughly 50/50.
- **Size discount:** moving from ~$250–500M TEV deals (~10x) to $25–100M TEV deals (~7–8x) costs about 2–3 turns (GF Data). Moving from mid cap to large cap in logistics is worth about 2.5 turns (Proventis).
- **Downcycle:** buyers will pay on normalized EBITDA only with evidence. Expect earn-outs or contingent consideration, which Radiant uses routinely, to bridge the gap between trough LTM and normalized EBITDA.
- **Tariffs:** tariffs both depress import volumes and raise demand for customs, compliance and sourcing advice. For OIA, this could support positioning the packaging-sourcing (Asia procurement) capability as tariff and country-of-origin diversification expertise. That is a narrative point; no multiple impact is quantified in the sources.
- **EV/Revenue:** expect about 0.2–0.5x on gross revenue for a mixed business with large freight pass-through. Implied range: Hub Group at 0.54x, Radiant at ~0.48x [E], Veritiv at ~0.35x [E].
- **EV/gross profit:** no source quantified this. Practitioners often value forwarders on gross profit (net revenue). As a cross-check only: at a typical 25–35% EBITDA-to-gross-profit conversion, a 7–9x EBITDA multiple corresponds to about 2–3x gross profit. This is an analytical identity, not a sourced market figure.

## Q5. Public evidence on what Lacy (LDI) paid for OIA in 2011

### Takeaway
I found no public disclosure of the 2011 price or multiple. The only public facts are deal structure and date: LDI recapitalized OIA and took majority control, announced 31 Aug 2011.

### Cited Findings
- LDI, an Indianapolis private holding company chaired by Andre Lacy and parent of FinishMaster and other businesses, recapitalized and acquired majority control of Oregon International Air Freight Co. and OIA Global Logistics-SCM, Inc. (together "OIA"). Founder Junki Yoshida stayed on as chairman and Charles Hornecker became CEO. Announced 31 Aug 2011 — [OIA Global news](https://www.oiaglobal.com/?p=394); [Indianapolis Business Journal](https://ibj.com/?p=20502)
- Follow-on acquisitions under LDI included International Transport Services (Cleveland, Nov 2012), U.S. Worldwide Logistics (Kentucky, Sep 2013) and American Cargo Express (New Jersey), all with undisclosed terms. This shows OIA itself was a buy-and-build platform — [OIA: ITS](https://www.oiaglobal.com/acquisition-international-transport-services); [OIA: USWL](https://www.oiaglobal.com/acquisition-us-worldwide-logistics-inc/); [TT News: ACE](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/oia-global-acquires-american-cargo-express); [Inside Indiana Business](https://www.insideindianabusiness.com/articles/ldi-acquires-kentucky-company)

### Inferences
- LDI bought a "majority control" stake through a recapitalization, so the founder or management likely kept a minority stake after 2011. A 2026 sale may therefore involve minority holders, and any equity bridge should check for this.
- Because the 2011 entry price was never disclosed, a return analysis for LDI cannot be built from public data. Internal LDI records would be needed.

### Gaps
- I found no price, EV, revenue or EBITDA multiple for the 2011 transaction in press, SEC filings (LDI is private) or trade publications.
- No financial terms were found for the 2012–2013 add-on acquisitions either.
