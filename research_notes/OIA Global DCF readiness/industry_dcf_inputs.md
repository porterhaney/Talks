# Industry Outlook and DCF Assumption Benchmarks: OIA Global (asset-light packaging supply chain management + international freight forwarding), 2026 sale

Research date: 9 October 2026. Every figure is dated. Where a figure comes from a secondary or aggregator source it is flagged. "Derived" means I calculated it from cited inputs; it is not a company-reported number.

**Macro context that dominates 2026 (read first):** Three shocks shape every input below. (1) A US–Iran conflict closed or disrupted the Strait of Hormuz from about 28 Feb 2026, which pushed ocean and air rates sharply higher. (2) The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 Feb 2026, and the administration replaced them first with Section 122 and then with Section 301 duties. (3) The Fed hiked rates on 16 Sep 2026 and the 10-year Treasury rose to about 5.3%, its highest level since 2002. Discount rates are therefore materially higher than in 2024–25 valuations.

## 1. Packaging market outlook 2026–2031 and outsourced packaging procurement

### Takeaway
Credible forecasts put global packaging demand growth at about 3.3–3.9% a year to 2030 (Smithers, July 2025). North America is in a similar 2.8–4.3% band, but those regional figures come from lower-quality market-research vendors. Outsourced and service-type packaging (e-commerce packaging, packaging-as-a-service) is forecast to grow faster, at about 9–9.5%, but those figures are weaker still. A base-case organic top-line growth for the packaging SCM segment of about 3–5% nominal is defensible, with share gains on top.

### Cited Findings
- **Global market (Smithers, July 2025):** Smithers' white paper "Impact of Tariffs on Packaging" projects global packaging demand of **$1.52 trillion by 2030**. It gives growth of **3.8% in the baseline scenario, 3.9% in the optimistic scenario and 3.3% in the pessimistic tariff scenario**, and warns growth could slow by up to 0.5 points under pessimistic tariff outcomes. — [Smithers, Jul 2025](https://www.smithers.com/Resources/2025/July/Tariffs-to-Reshape-Packaging-Market). The source does not explicitly label these growth rates as CAGRs.
- **Older Smithers baseline (2020):** the earlier forecast was $1.13T by 2030, up from $860bn in 2020. This is superseded by the 2025 figure above. — [PrintWeek India](https://printweek.in/news/smithers-predicts-packaging-market-value-to-be-usd-113trillion-in-2030-53863)
- **E-commerce packaging (Smithers):** forecast **9% CAGR** to **$116.1bn in 2029**. Digital packaging print is forecast at a 10.9% CAGR to $36.9bn by 2030. — [Smithers via search summary](https://www.smithers.com/Resources/2025/July/Tariffs-to-Reshape-Packaging-Market); [PrintWeek India](https://www.printweek.in/news/digital-packaging-print-to-reach-usd-22-bn-smithers--61311?amp=1)
- **North America total market (LOWER QUALITY):** Towards Packaging puts the 2026 market at about $348bn, rising to about $510bn by 2035, a **4.33% CAGR**. Another page from the same vendor gives a 2025 value of $278.6bn and a **2.8% CAGR**. The vendor contradicts itself. — [Towards Packaging](https://www.towardspackaging.com/insights/north-america-packaging-market-sizing); [Towards Packaging alt page](https://www.towardspackaging.com/outlook/north-america-packaging-market)
- **NA segments (LOWER QUALITY):** flexible packaging **2.78% CAGR** 2026–2034, from $49.67bn in 2025 to $64.05bn in 2034 ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/north-america-flexible-packaging-market)). Food packaging **5.37% CAGR** 2026–2034 ([MarketDataForecast](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/north-america-food-packaging-market)). Plastic packaging **4.38% CAGR** ([MarketDataForecast](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/north-america-plastic-packaging-market)).
- **Packaging-as-a-service / outsourced (LOWER QUALITY):** the global market is valued at **$13.0bn in 2026**, rising to **$24.5bn by 2033 (9.5% CAGR)**. North America holds about 34% share in 2026. — [Persistence Market Research](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/packaging-as-a-service-market.asp)
- **PMMI 2026 State of the Industry (published Aug 2026, 2026–2031 forecasts, paywalled):** the public summary cites tariff-driven trade shifts, material-reduction and EPR pressure, and stronger parts and services demand. It puts US packaging machinery shipments at about **$11.7bn in 2025**. A 6.7% US CAGR appears in the summary, but its period could not be confirmed. — [PMMI](https://www.pmmi.org/report/2026-packaging-state-of-the-industry); [Packworld](https://www.packworld.com/leaders-new/business-drivers-specialty/sustainability/news/22974423/pmmi-pmmis-2026-state-of-the-industry-report-reveals-a-packaging-machinery-market-driven-by-flexibility-automation-and-new-north-american-opportunities)
- **Outsourcing trend (PMMI, 2024 data):** more than two-thirds of brand owners expected to maintain or increase contract packaging and contract manufacturing use, and three-quarters of contract packagers and manufacturers expected growth. — [Packaging Strategies / PMMI](https://www.packagingstrategies.com/articles/105057-pmmi-report-brand-owners-rely-on-contract-packaging-contract-manufacturing)
- **Distributor read-across (Bunzl, FY2025 results, 2 Mar 2026):** group adjusted operating margin fell from 8.3% to **7.6–7.7%**. North America H1 2025 margin was **6.4%** (down 100 bps), with organic declines in North America from a sales and operations reorganisation and weak end markets. Management gave a "stable" 2026 outlook that the market saw as disappointing. — [Bunzl FY2025 RNS (FCA)](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/a71c7bd1-d735-47ea-b519-ce6174c23ff3.html); [MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/operations/earnings/bunzls-2025-1h-saw-organic-declines-in-north-america-europe-offset-by-intl/); [ii.co.uk](https://www.ii.co.uk/analysis-commentary/ii-view-bunzl-offers-disappointing-2026-outlook-ii537600)

### Inferences
- Base-case market growth for the packaging procurement and SCM line is about **3–4% nominal** (Smithers baseline 3.8%; pessimistic tariff case 3.3%). OIA can model above-market growth only with evidence of share gains or wins in outsourced procurement or e-commerce packaging.
- Packaging-procurement revenue that is landed-cost based (product plus freight plus duty) is inflated in 2025–26 by tariffs and freight. Organic volume growth should be separated from price and duty pass-through.
- Bunzl's 6.4–7.7% operating margin is a ceiling reference for a scaled packaging distributor. A sub-scale procurement intermediary would typically earn less (see section 4).

### Gaps
- No Freedonia figure was found, and no primary North American packaging growth rate from Smithers or Freedonia for 2026–2031.
- No market sizing was found specifically for "outsourced packaging procurement / packaging supply chain management" (OIA's niche).
- The full-year 2025 Bunzl North America margin was not retrieved, only H1 2025.

## 2. Ocean and air freight rates 2024–2026 and forwarder gross vs net revenue

### Takeaway
2025 was a soft-rate year: ocean pricing fell and forwarders' ocean gross profit shrank. In 2026 the Hormuz crisis (from about 28 Feb 2026) drove transpacific spot rates up by roughly 4.4x to a peak. On 1 Oct 2026 Far East–US West Coast was $8,346/FEU and East Coast was $11,523/FEU. Xeneta expects a "sizeable correction, not a collapse" over the next three months, against a structural vessel overcapacity backdrop. Model OIA on net revenue (gross profit). Gross revenue is heavily inflated in 2026 and should be normalized downward for 2027 onward.

### Cited Findings
- **Xeneta spot rates, 1 Oct 2026 (USD/FEU):** Far East–USWC **$8,346** (+344.2% vs 28 Feb 2026 pre-Hormuz). Far East–USEC **$11,523** (+334.7%). Far East–N. Europe **$3,726** (+67.9%, peaked around July and falling since). Xeneta says the market has reached its post-Hormuz peak, demand is "not strong", and in three months USEC could be **$6,000–7,000** and USWC **$4,500–5,500**. It calls this a "sizeable correction, not a collapse". The USEC–USWC spread is $3,177, against $772 pre-crisis. — [Xeneta weekly update via AJOT, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.ajot.com/news/xeneta-weekly-ocean-container-shipping-market-update-october-2-2026)
- **Drewry WCI:** composite **$4,526/FEU on 20 Aug 2026** (+4% w/w, third straight weekly rise). Shanghai–LA was $6,802 and Shanghai–NY $9,507 (both +9%). In **late July 2026** the composite was $4,255 and Shanghai–LA was $5,739. — [Hellenic Shipping News / Drewry](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/world-container-index-20-aug/). Conflict: a Spanish-language report dated 4 Aug 2026 gives a composite of **$3,159** (+16% w/w, +81% y/y), which is inconsistent with the late-July $4,255. It may be mis-dated. — [The Logistics World](https://thelogisticsworld.com/actualidad-logistica/world-container-index-aumenta-en-16). The 1 Oct 2026 WCI figures could not be retrieved.
- **Early-2026 contract rates (pre-Hormuz):** Xeneta reported long-term Far East–Med rates down 25% vs end-2025 to **$2,308/FEU** and N. Europe at **$2,010/FEU** on 21 Jan 2026, the lowest since 2023. Both shippers and carriers expected a weaker 2026. — [Xeneta, Jan 2026](https://www.xeneta.com/blog/red-sea-return-what-it-means-for-2026-container-shipping-contract-rates)
- **Red Sea:** the return to Suez is partial and uneven. Asia–Europe monthly offered capacity via Suez fell from 4.1m TEU in 2023 to 292K TEU in 2025. CMA CGM reversed a planned return of three services. Xeneta says a full return "would exacerbate the existing oversupply" and push rates down. — [Xeneta](https://www.xeneta.com/blog/largescale-return-of-container-ships-to-red-sea-in-2026-five-key-considerations-for-shippers); [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1120480)
- **Overcapacity (Sep 2026):** BIMCO says supply pressure is building. Global container volumes rose **5.1% y/y in Jan–Jul 2026**, and Cape rerouting plus sidelined vessels are absorbing capacity for now. — [Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/overcapacity-looms-over-container-shipping-as-supply-pressure-mounts). Schedule reliability fell to **29% in Aug 2026** (Xeneta, same source). Drewry forecast **49 blank sailings** between 24 Aug and 27 Sep 2026, mostly on the eastbound transpacific. — [Cole International](https://blog.coleintl.com/tradenews/49-blank-sailings-expected-across-major-ocean-trade-routes-through-september). A June 2026 report attributed earlier transpacific declines to "growing overcapacity" despite near-record demand. — [India Sea Trade News, 20 Jun 2026](https://indiaseatradenews.com/carriers-may-have-overshot-on-capacity-and-will-need-to-blank-more-sailings/)
- **Hormuz:** as of 30 Sep 2026 Lloyd's List counted 15 containership transits in September, about double August but "negligible". Major lines still avoid the strait. Maersk charges an extra $1,000/container for Hormuz transits. — [Lloyd's List Intelligence, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.lloydslistintelligence.com/resources/blog/strait-of-hormuz-brief-30-september-2026); [Container News](https://container-news.com/maersk-imposes-emergency-freight-rates-amid-hormuz-disruption/)
- **Air freight:** in March 2026, China–US air rates rose 15% in one week to **$6.90/kg**. Gulf carriers (EK, EY, QR) handle about 15% of global air cargo, and forwarders chartered direct Far East–West flights. — [Freightos via AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/freightos-weekly-update-iran-war-pushing-air-rates-up-and-disrupting-ocean). A mid-2026 update had China–North America at **$6.40/kg**, with air pricing about **30% above pre-conflict** levels. — [Air Cargo Week](https://aircargoweek.com/?p=105023). The update is undated, so treat it as approximate.
- **Forwarder P&L evidence for 2025, the soft-rate year:**
  - C.H. Robinson Global Forwarding adjusted gross profit (AGP) was **$741.9M in 2025, -7.6%**. Ocean AGP was $432.5M (-16.8%) "reflecting the significant reduction in market pricing during 2025". Air was $134.7M (+0.3%) and customs $132.8M (**+23.6%**). — [CHRW FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm)
  - Expeditors' 2025 ocean freight and ocean services revenue was $2.81B (-11%), airfreight $3.98B (+9%) and customs brokerage and other $4.27B (+13%). Total revenue was about **$11.07B** (+4.4%). — [EXPD FY2025 10-K/ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746515/000119312526121379/expd_10k_2025_ars_filing.pdf); [StockAnalysis](https://www.stockanalysis.com/stocks/expd/revenue/)
- **Radiant (FY ended 30 Jun 2026):** revenue was $934.4M (+3.5%) but adjusted EBITDA fell to $36.7M from $38.8M. Q4 FY26 revenue rose 18.5% on strength in international airfreight. Management advises modelling "growth conservatively, focusing on gross margin rather than top-line revenue". — [Quiver Quant](https://www.quiverquant.com/news/Radiant+Logistics+Posts+%24934.4+Million+Fiscal+2026+Revenue+and+%2418.8+Million+Net+Income); [Finviz release](https://finviz.com/news/391788/radiant-logistics-announces-results-for-the-fourth-fiscal-quarter-and-year-ended-june-30-2026)

### Inferences
- **Gross vs net revenue:** freight cost is largely passed through. A roughly 4x spike in transpacific spot rates inflates OIA's 2026 gross revenue without a proportional rise in net revenue. The CHRW 2025 data shows the reverse also hurts: when market pricing falls, ocean gross profit per shipment compresses too, at -16.8%. A DCF should therefore:
  1. Forecast on net revenue (gross profit) and volume.
  2. Normalize 2026 gross revenue down for 2027 using the Xeneta three-month correction ranges; pre-crisis levels are roughly $1,900/FEU USWC and $2,650/FEU USEC, back-solved from the +344%/+335% changes (derived).
  3. Treat 2026 forwarding gross profit as above trend, a likely "peak-year" quality-of-earnings adjustment that buyers will normalize.
- The structural backdrop for 2027–2028 is overcapacity: a fleet orderbook overhang, a possible Suez and Hormuz normalization, and weak demand per Xeneta. This argues for mean-reverting freight rates and flat to modestly lower forwarding gross profit per unit in the out-years.

### Gaps
- Drewry WCI and FBX values for 1 Oct 2026 could not be retrieved; Hellenic Shipping News returned 403. A dated Freightos Air Index reading for October 2026 was not found.
- No current, dated status of Hormuz reopening was found. Sources conflict between a "gradual reopening under a US–Iran interim agreement" and traffic "slowed to a trickle".

## 3. 2025–2026 US tariffs on China/Asia imports, customs brokerage demand, nearshoring

### Takeaway
The tariff regime changed three times in 2026:
- **20 Feb:** the Supreme Court held 6–3 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs (*Learning Resources v. Trump*).
- **24 Feb – 24 Jul:** a temporary 10% global surcharge applied under Section 122.
- **From 24 Jul:** Section 301 duties of 10–12.5% apply on about 60 economies. China-origin goods pay 12.5% (secondary sources), with no statutory sunset.

De minimis is suspended for all countries and was codified on 24 Jun 2026. For OIA this means lower but still elevated and volatile China duties, a refund-recovery workstream, and strong customs and compliance demand. Customs is the fastest-growing forwarder line at the public comps (CHRW customs AGP +23.6% in 2025).

### Cited Findings
- **SCOTUS ruling (20 Feb 2026):** *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* held 6–3 that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs. — [Plante Moran](https://plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2026/02/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/ar/insights/2026/2/supreme-court-invalidates-trump-administrations-ieepa-tariffs); [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/us-supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs.html)
- **IEEPA refunds:** these are processed via CBP's CAPE system. Phase 1 launched **20 Apr 2026** and covers about **63% of eligible entries** (unliquidated, or liquidated within the prior 80 days). Reconciliation, drawback, protested, finally liquidated and AD/CVD entries are excluded for now. Full resolution may take years. — [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/update-on-ieepa-tariff-refunds); [Endless Commerce](https://endlesscommerce.com/playbook/supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-refunds)
- **Section 122:** a 10% global surcharge was effective **24 Feb 2026** for 150 days, to **24 Jul 2026**. A rise to 15% was announced, but a revised proclamation had not been issued as of the alert date. 22 State AGs sued in the CIT on 5 Mar 2026. — [Honigman](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3240); [McCarter & English](https://www.mccarter.com/insights/ieepa-tariffs-held-unlawful-section-122-tariffs-commence-trade-deals-likely-to-falter/)
- **Section 301 replacement (24 Jul 2026):** Section 122 lapsed and USTR imposed Section 301 duties of **10% or 12.5%** on goods from about **60 economies** found to have failed to prohibit imports made with forced labor. **China-origin goods pay 12.5%** (up from 10% under Section 122), with no statutory sunset. — [Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/trump-administration-imposes-new-section-301-tariffs-on-60-trading-partners); [AACB](https://www.aacb.com/trade-tariff-news/section-122-is-out-section-301-is-in). Conflict: GingerControl says 80 countries in one version and 60 in another. A Liberty Justice Center legal challenge was filed the week the duties took effect. — [GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/section-122-tariffs-explained). Status: secondary; not confirmed against the USTR or Federal Register notice.
- **De minimis:** the exemption was suspended for China and Hong Kong first, then for all countries as of **29 Aug 2025**. The suspension was continued on 20 Feb 2026. A CBP interim final rule effective **24 Jun 2026** codifies an indefinite suspension for non-postal modes ($800 or less). A companion postal rule has a compliance date of **22 Oct 2026** for shipments with partner government agency data requirements. — [Federal Register, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other); [AACB](https://www.aacb.com/trade-tariff-news/president-trump-keeps-de-minimis-suspension-for-all-countries-readies-imposition-of-section-122-tariffs)
- **Customs brokerage upside:**
  - CHRW customs AGP rose **+23.6% in 2025** ($132.8M vs $107.5M) on "higher duty advance fees [that] reflected elevated global tariff rates in 2025". — [CHRW FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm)
  - Expeditors' customs brokerage and other services revenue rose **+13% in 2025** to $4.27B, its largest line. — [EXPD FY2025 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746515/000119312526121379/expd_10k_2025_ars_filing.pdf)
  - M&A advisors say buyers are acquiring customs capability "as a differentiator". — [Logisyn via search summary of Ti Insight / advisor commentary](https://ti-insight.com/?p=312684)
- **Tariff effect on packaging trade:** Smithers' July 2025 paper projects tariffs "reshaping" the $1.5T packaging market and cutting growth by up to 0.5 points in the pessimistic case. — [Smithers](https://www.smithers.com/Resources/2025/July/Tariffs-to-Reshape-Packaging-Market). PMMI's 2026 report cites "tariff-driven trade shifts" and "new North American opportunities". — [Packworld](https://www.packworld.com/leaders-new/business-drivers-specialty/sustainability/news/22974423/pmmi-pmmis-2026-state-of-the-industry-report-reveals-a-packaging-machinery-market-driven-by-flexibility-automation-and-new-north-american-opportunities)

### Inferences
- For a business that sources packaging in China for US brands, the 2026 China duty stack is lower than at the 2025 IEEPA peak: 12.5% Section 301 "forced labor" on top of any pre-existing Section 301 lists, against the 2025 IEEPA "fentanyl" plus reciprocal layers. It remains elevated and legally unstable, and the new Section 301 has no sunset. Expect continued customer pressure to dual-source (Vietnam, India, Mexico).
  - **Risk:** volume migrating out of OIA's China sourcing base.
  - **Opportunity:** if OIA has, or can build, Vietnam, India or Mexico supplier networks, and if it earns customs, compliance and refund-recovery fees.
- An IEEPA refund recovery service (CAPE filings) is a potential 2026–27 one-off revenue source and a quality-of-earnings item to normalize out.
- Duty advances are a working-capital drag for customs brokers (see section 4). Higher duties mean larger advances and more receivables.

### Gaps
- The status in 2026 of the legacy Section 301 China lists (Lists 1–4A; 7.5%–25%+ and the 2024 four-year review increases) was not verified. It needs USTR confirmation for packaging HTS codes (e.g., Chapter 39 plastics, Chapter 48 paper and board).
- No sourced data was found on 2025–26 shifts in US packaging import share from China to Vietnam, India or Mexico. A USITC DataWeb pull by HTS chapter is recommended.
- No current (post-July 2026) CIT ruling on the Section 122 or Section 301 challenges was found.

## 4. Benchmark financial ratios: asset-light forwarders and packaging distributors

### Takeaway
Asset-light forwarders earn about 16–33% net revenue on gross revenue. EBITDA and operating income run at about 4–10% of gross revenue but about 15–30% of net revenue. Capex and D&A are about 0.5% of revenue, and receivables are about 65 days DSO. Packaging distributors (Bunzl) earn about 7–8% operating margins on revenue. Lower-middle-market forwarders like Radiant earn about 4% EBITDA on gross revenue and about 15% on net revenue, which is the most relevant anchor for OIA's size.

### Cited Findings
**Expeditors (EXPD), FY2025**
- Revenue was **$11.07B**. Operating income was **$1,052.5M** vs $1,041.3M in 2024, and net earnings were $810.3M. — [EXPD FY2025 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746515/000119312526121379/expd_10k_2025_ars_filing.pdf); [StockAnalysis](https://www.stockanalysis.com/stocks/expd/revenue/)
- Net revenue (revenue less directly related costs) was about **$3.67B** in 2025 vs about $3.41B in 2024 (+7%), by segment: air about $1.00B, ocean about $0.79B, customs about $1.88B. This is an aggregator derivation from the 10-K tables and is not company-reported. — [search-derived from EXPD 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746515/000119312526121379/expd_10k_2025_ars_filing.pdf)

### Key risk register (synthesis of sections 2–3; all inferences from cited findings above)
1. **Freight-rate mean reversion.** Xeneta projects a correction from the 1 Oct 2026 peak, and structural overcapacity follows if Suez and Hormuz normalize. This hits gross revenue hardest and also compresses net revenue per unit (CHRW ocean AGP fell 16.8% in 2025).
2. **Tariff and legal volatility.** The Section 301 "forced labor" duties (12.5% for China) face legal challenge. The legacy Section 301 China lists remain. IEEPA refund timing is uncertain. De minimis is gone, which helps formal-entry customs demand.
3. **Nearshoring and China-plus-one.** US brands may shift packaging sourcing to Vietnam, India or Mexico. This is a threat to China-centric sourcing revenue unless OIA's supplier network follows. Supporting data is a gap.
4. **Rate environment.** A 10-year at about 5.3% and a Fed in hiking mode raise WACC and lower buyer leverage capacity.
5. **Geopolitical shipping disruption.** The status of the Strait of Hormuz is unresolved, Red Sea transits remain partial, and schedule reliability was 29% in August 2026.
