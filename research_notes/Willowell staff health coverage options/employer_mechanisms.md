## 1. How a small Vermont employer buys group coverage, the market rules, and 2026/2027 small-group premium levels

### Takeaway
Since January 1, 2026, Vermont's individual and small-group markets are **permanently unmerged** (Act 2 of 2025). Small group covers employers with up to 100 employees. Small employers buy VHC-certified small-group plans **directly from BCBSVT or MVP**, not through a VHC portal. For 2026, BCBSVT small-group **employee-only** premiums run about **$827–$876/mo for bronze, $960–$1,022/mo for silver, $1,189–$1,198/mo for gold, and $1,409/mo for platinum**. That is roughly $9.9K–$16.9K per year per covered employee. The board's "$14K/person" estimate matches a **gold** small-group plan. For 2027, GMCB approved average small-group increases of **+1.2% (BCBSVT) and +6.2% (MVP)**.

### Cited Findings
**Market structure and how to buy**
- Act 2 of 2025 (H.35), signed Feb 19, 2025 and effective Jan 1, 2026, "permanently unmerges the individual and small group health insurance markets, which had been temporarily unmerged on a one- or two-year basis since 2022". Carriers must set individual and small-group premiums separately. The act also raises the "qualified employer" threshold from 50 to 100 employees. — [Act 2 summary (VT Legislature)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT002/ACT002%20Act%20Summary.pdf); [H.35 status](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/H.35); [Vermont Chamber: markets to remain unmerged](https://www.vtchamber.com/?p=22527)
- The Vermont Chamber and MVP backed the split on the grounds that merging after the enhanced PTCs expired would have pushed up small-employer premiums. — [MVP testimony on H.35](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Health%20Care/Bills/H.35/Witness%20Documents/H.35~Jordan%20Estey~MVP%20Written%20Testimony~1-23-2025.pdf); [Vermont Chamber](https://www.vtchamber.com/?p=22527)

### Inferences
- **Cost envelope for Willowell (2026 BCBSVT, employee-only, employer pays 100%)**, for 8 enrollees: bronze about $79K–$84K/yr, silver about $92K–$98K/yr, gold about $114K–$115K/yr. Paying 50% halves these figures. Applying the +1.2% BCBSVT average gives a rough 2027 estimate (e.g., Standard Silver about $993/mo), but plan-level 2027 charts are not yet published.
- Small group is materially cheaper than individual for comparable plans. Gold is about 11% cheaper (Std Gold $1,198 vs $1,345). Silver is about 36% cheaper ($981 vs $1,532 on VHC), because small-group silver carries no CSR "silver loading." So an ICHRA/QSEHRA dollar buys less coverage than a group-plan dollar in Vermont.
- Because the markets are now unmerged, a small group's premiums no longer subsidize the individual pool, and vice versa. Prices diverge by market.
- Minimum participation and minimum employer contribution: neither VHC nor BCBSVT public pages state Vermont figures. Under federal guaranteed-availability rules (45 CFR 147.104), carriers generally must accept small groups that fail participation/contribution rules if they enroll during the Nov 15–Dec 15 window. *This is background knowledge, not verified this session.* Confirm with BCBSVT/MVP small-group underwriting.
- Level-funded plans such as Trustmark's may be priced below community-rated QHPs for a young, healthy group. They are experience-rated and pull small groups out of the community pool. With 6–10 lives, one serious claim can drive renewal spikes, and they are not QHPs, so they do not qualify for 45R.

## 2. Section 45R Small Business Health Care Tax Credit for a tax-exempt employer (2026)

### Takeaway
A 501(c)(3) (a private operating foundation qualifies as a 501(c) organization) can claim a **refundable credit of up to 35% of employer-paid premiums** on Form 990-T. The credit is **capped at federal income tax withheld plus Medicare taxes** and limited to **two consecutive tax years**. Requirements: fewer than 25 FTEs, average wages below about **$68,200 (indexed; the form uses "less than $69,000" after rounding down)**, a uniform contribution of at least 50% of employee-only premium, and coverage in a **SHOP/VHC-certified QHP**. In Vermont that means a VHC-certified small-group QHP bought directly from BCBSVT/MVP, plus VHC's 205ER-TAX eligibility letter. For 8 FTEs averaging $40K in wages, with Willowell paying 50% of BCBSVT Standard Silver, the credit is roughly **$13.6K/yr for 2 years**.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 draft Form 8941 instructions: "For tax years beginning after 2013, the credit is only available for a 2-consecutive-tax-year credit period." It applies to coverage "in a qualified health plan offered through a Small Business Health Options Program (SHOP) Marketplace." For tax-exempts, "the maximum credit is 35% of premiums paid, is limited to the amount of certain payroll taxes paid, and is claimed as a refundable credit on Form 990-T." Eligibility requires being "described in section 501(c)" and exempt under 501(a). — [IRS 2026 Instructions for Form 8941 (draft)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8941--dft.pdf)
- Eligibility: fewer than 25 FTEs ("an employer with exactly 25 FTEs is not, in fact, eligible"). Average annual wages must be "less than $69,000 per FTE." Phase-outs start above 10 FTEs or above $34,000 average wages. Worksheet 6 computes the wage phase-out as (average wages − **$34,100**) ÷ **$34,100**. — [IRS 2026 i8941 draft](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8941--dft.pdf). Secondary sources cite $68,200, $69,000, or $62,000–$65,000 for 2026. The IRS draft governs, and $68,200 = 2 × $34,100. — [OnPay](https://onpay.com/insights/small-business-health-care-tax-credit/); [beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/04/24/small-business-health-care-tax-credit-employer-guide)
- Payroll-tax cap for tax-exempts (2026): federal income tax withheld from employees, plus employee Medicare withheld, plus employer Medicare paid for calendar 2026. — [IRS 2026 i8941 draft](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8941--dft.pdf)
- Premiums counted are limited to what the employer would have paid at the **average small-group premium for the rating area**. The 2026 average premium tables are "not available until November 2026." A stand-alone SHOP dental plan counts as a QHP for the credit. The employer's premium deduction is reduced by the credit (irrelevant for a tax-exempt). A county exception exists for 2026 only for counties with no SHOP QHPs, and only if the employer claimed in 2025. — [IRS 2026 i8941 draft](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8941--dft.pdf)
- IRS Q&A: the employer must pay at least 50% of the premium for employee-only coverage under a qualifying arrangement. — [IRS Small Business Health Care Tax Credit Q&A](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/small-business-health-care-tax-credit-questions-and-answers-who-gets-the-tax-credit)
- VHC tax credit page: the business must "offer employees a Vermont Health Connect-certified qualified health plan," have fewer than 25 FTEs, contribute at least 50% of employee-only premium, submit eligibility application **205ER-TAX** to VHC by mail, and receive an eligibility letter, then claim the credit on its federal return. The page states a **$55,000** wage limit (outdated, no year given), and "VHC itself doesn't play a role in determining tax credits." — [VHC Small Business Tax Credit](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/learn-more/businesses-and-employees/business-tax-credit); [VHC SB FAQ](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/small-business-resources/small-business-faq)
- Statutory base: full credit only with 10 or fewer FTEs and average wages not above $25,000 (indexed by CPI-U). — [IRS Q&A](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/small-business-health-care-tax-credit-questions-and-answers-who-gets-the-tax-credit)

### Inferences
- **Worked example (illustrative; assumes 8 FTEs, average wages $40,000, Willowell pays 50% of employee-only BCBSVT Standard Silver at $980.80/mo, actual premium at or below the VT average small-group premium):**
  - Employer premiums: $490.40 × 12 × 8 = **$47,078**
  - Line 6 × 35% = **$16,477**
  - FTE reduction: none (≤10 FTEs)
  - Wage reduction: ($40,000 − $34,100)/$34,100 = 0.173 → −$2,851
  - **Credit ≈ $13,626/yr** (about 29% of employer spend). It is refundable, but capped at federal income tax withholding plus Medicare. On $320K payroll, Medicare alone is about $9,280 (2.9%), and withholding on modest Vermont nonprofit wages may be low. **The payroll-tax cap could bind.** Model actual 941 data.
  - If Willowell pays 100% of the premium: credit base = $94,157 × 35% = $32,955, less the wage phase-out of about 17% → **about $27.3K/yr**, subject to the payroll-tax cap.
  - If average wages are ≤$34,100, there is no wage reduction. If average wages are ≥$68,200, there is no credit.
- The credit lasts only 2 years. Willowell must budget for full cost from year 3. It reduces net cost but does not change the decision about whether to offer.
- Claiming 45R requires offering a group QHP. That makes the employee-only plan "affordable" for most staff if Willowell pays 50%+ of a $960–$1,020 silver premium only when household income is high. At 50% of Std Silver, the employee pays $490/mo, which is affordable (≤9.96%) only if household income ≥ about $59K. **Staff below that income stay PTC-eligible** if they decline. At 100% employer-paid, the plan is affordable for everyone, so PTC/VPA is lost for all who are offered it, even if they decline.
- FTE counting: workers under 30 hours are counted fractionally, so the "~10 employees" may be fewer than 10 FTEs. Seasonal camp staff working ≤120 days are excluded under 45R rules (not verified this session).

- Whether VHC still processes 205ER-TAX promptly. The page appears dated (it cites the $55K wage limit).
- Exact definition of "seasonal worker" exclusion for camp staff under 45R (not verified).

## 3. QSEHRA: limits, rules, interaction with PTC / Vermont Premium Assistance / Medicaid, and tax treatment

### Takeaway
A QSEHRA (2026 caps **$6,450 self-only / $13,100 family**; 2027 caps **not yet published as of Oct 8, 2026**) is available because Willowell has fewer than 50 FTEs and no group plan. For any employee receiving a PTC, the **monthly QSEHRA allowance reduces the PTC dollar-for-dollar**, or eliminates it if the QSEHRA is "affordable". **For subsidized staff, a QSEHRA mostly substitutes employer dollars for federal dollars.** It can reimburse Medicaid premiums and out-of-pocket costs tax-free for Medicaid enrollees, because Medicaid is MEC. Its real value goes to unsubsidized staff (over 400% FPL, or on a spouse's plan). VHC's own guidance is stricter than the federal rule: it says an HRA plus financial help can't be combined.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 limits: $6,450 self-only ($537.50/mo) and $13,100 family ($1,091.66/mo), up from $6,350/$12,800 in 2025, per Rev. Proc. 2025-32. — [Current Federal Tax Developments on Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/10/9/2026-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-professionals-revenue-procedure-2025-32-analysis); [Take Command QSEHRA Rules 2026](https://www.takecommandhealth.com/blog/qsehra-rules)
- 2027 limits: not yet published. Rev. Proc. 2026-24 set 2027 HSA/EBHRA figures but not QSEHRA. The QSEHRA/FSA figures normally come in the fall inflation Rev. Proc. — [Beneliance on 2027 HSA/EBHRA](https://beneliance.com/2027-hsa-contribution-limit-hra-contribution-increase/); [World Insurance 2027 limits](https://www.worldinsurance.com/eb/alerts/2027-hsa-hdhp-dpcsa-hra-limits)
- Rules (vendor summary): fewer than 50 FTEs; no group health plan; 100% employer-funded (no employee contributions). Must be offered on the same terms to all eligible employees. The employer may exclude employees with under 90 days' service, under age 25, part-time, or seasonal. Allowances may vary by age or family size. Written notice is due **90 days before the plan year** (or by the eligibility date for new hires), with a $50/employee penalty capped at $2,500/yr. Employees must have MEC (Medicaid counts) for tax-free reimbursement. Report the permitted benefit in W-2 Box 12, code FF. Mid-year eligibles get a prorated allowance. Can cover premiums only, or premiums plus out-of-pocket. — [Take Command QSEHRA Rules](https://www.takecommandhealth.com/blog/qsehra-rules)
- 90-day notice timing: for a Jan 1, 2027 start, notice was due Oct 3, 2026. One vendor's reading is that a late notice triggers the penalty rather than invalidating the arrangement. — [myprivatehealthinsurance (secondary)](https://myprivatehealthinsurance.com/qsehra-vs-private-individual-plans-for-micro-businesses-in-2027/)
- PTC interaction: "The QSEHRA allowance will reduce the employee's premium tax credit on a dollar-for-dollar basis." An employee "cannot receive a premium tax credit if the employee is covered under a QSEHRA that provides affordable coverage." — [PeopleKeep](https://www.peoplekeep.com/blog/offering-a-qsehra-and-individual-premium-tax-credits); [Take Command](https://www.takecommandhealth.com/blog/what-happens-to-premium-tax-credits-with-qsehra); [Salusion](https://salusion.com/learning-center/how-do-qsehras-affect-premium-tax-credits/)
- Medicaid: Medicare, Medicaid, Tricare and VA count as MEC, and their premiums "can be reimbursed through QSEHRA tax-free." A state exchange Q&A (Washington, Apple Health) says a Medicaid enrollee with no premium can use QSEHRA for other out-of-pocket medical expenses, and that "utilizing a QSEHRA will not impact an employee's eligibility for Apple Health." — [Take Command help: reimbursable premiums](https://intercom.help/take-command-health/en/articles/2823119-what-types-of-premiums-are-reimbursable-through-qsehra); [WA HBE Cures Act QSEHRA Q&A](https://www.wahbexchange.org/content/dam/materials/collateral/other/cures-act-q-a/HBE_AQT_180521_QSEHRA-Cures-Act-QA.pdf)
- VHC's HRA guidance: if the HRA is affordable, the employee must enroll in a full-price plan without APTC and use the HRA. If it is not affordable and the employee qualifies for APTC, they can use the tax credits "but they must opt out of the HRA." "An employee cannot have an HRA and also receive financial help from Vermont Health Connect." VHC publishes 2026 and 2027 HRA affordability worksheets. A newly offered HRA may trigger a special enrollment period. The page does not distinguish QSEHRA from ICHRA. — [VHC: What if my employer offers coverage but I can't afford it?](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/node/147)
- Vermont Premium Assistance: a state subsidy layered on federal APTC for households ≤300% FPL (33 V.S.A. § 1812), funded via the 1115 waiver. It also includes a state CSR at ≤300% FPL. — [33 V.S.A. § 1812](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/33/018/01812); [HCA testimony 4-28-2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Ways%20and%20Means/Bills/DR%2026-0804/W~Mike%20Fisher~Health%20Care%20Advocate%20Testimony~4-28-2026.pdf); [VHC 2025 income thresholds](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2025-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf)
- A Common Good Vermont guest article (WellNorth Consulting, Dec 2025) notes that a QSEHRA "may reduce an employee's Marketplace tax credit dollar-for-dollar." — [Common Good VT: Beyond the Premium](https://commongoodvt.org/news/beyond-the-premium-strategic-health-benefits-planning-for-non-profits/)

### Inferences
- **Vermont arithmetic:** 2026 VHC silver for a single adult costs about $1,290–$1,600/mo before subsidy (Section 1). The QSEHRA maximum is $537.50/mo. For a single employee earning around $40K, the expected contribution is ≤9.96% of income (about $332/mo). That makes the SLCSP-net-of-QSEHRA well above that threshold, so **a QSEHRA will almost always be "unaffordable" in Vermont for Willowell's wage levels.** The PTC is then reduced by the full monthly allowance. Net effect for a subsidized employee: the employer pays $X and the federal PTC drops by $X. The employee is roughly no better off, apart from minor tax-timing and out-of-pocket reimbursement flexibility. The employer spends real money for near-zero employee gain.
- **VPA:** VPA is tied to APTC eligibility (33 V.S.A. § 1812). If a QSEHRA is affordable (no APTC), VPA is presumably lost too. If unaffordable, how VHC computes VPA after a QSEHRA reduction is unclear (see Gaps). VHC's blanket statement that an HRA and financial help cannot be combined suggests Vermont may treat it more harshly than federal law, at least operationally.
- **Medicaid staff:** QSEHRA reimbursements are excluded from gross income when the employee has MEC, so they should not count as MAGI income for Medicaid. Vermont adult Medicaid generally has no premium and low cost-sharing, so the QSEHRA's practical value to Medicaid staff is small: dental, vision, Rx copays, and Dr. Dynasaur child premiums if any. The MAGI exclusion is inferred from tax treatment and not verified with DVHA.
- **"QSEHRA sized to not exceed what staff lose" doesn't work as framed.** The reduction is dollar-for-dollar against the PTC, so any QSEHRA size is a wash for subsidized staff. It is a pure gain only for staff with no PTC (over 400% FPL, ineligible due to a spouse's affordable offer, or on Medicare/Medicaid using it for out-of-pocket costs). The same-terms rule prevents targeting only those staff, except by age or family-size variation.
- A QSEHRA is not a group health plan, so it does not create an "offer of employer coverage" that blocks Medicaid.

### Gaps
- 2027 QSEHRA limits (expected in the fall 2026 Rev. Proc.; not yet found).
- Vermont-specific treatment of VPA when a QSEHRA reduces APTC. DVHA/VHC should be asked directly; the VHC HRA FAQ does not mention VPA by name.
- Whether an employee can decline a QSEHRA to preserve the full PTC. Under federal rules the PTC reduction applies to the amount "provided"; this was not verified this session.
- Vermont Medicaid's treatment of QSEHRA reimbursements as income (DVHA guidance not found).

## 4. ICHRA: rules, affordability test, PTC interaction, class designs, Vermont vendors

### Takeaway
An ICHRA has **no dollar cap** and lets Willowell set different allowances by class (e.g., full-time vs part-time). Crucially, **an employee offered an unaffordable ICHRA can opt out and keep the full PTC and VPA.** So an ICHRA set at a modest level in Vermont (where silver costs $1,300+) is a **"do-no-harm" design**: subsidized staff keep public subsidies, and unsubsidized staff take the ICHRA. Affordability tests the lowest-cost silver self-only premium minus the monthly ICHRA against **9.96% (2026) / 10.22% (2027)** of household income. Employees buy in Vermont's individual market (VHC or direct), which is now priced separately from small group and is more expensive.

### Cited Findings
- Required contribution percentage: **9.96% for 2026** and **10.22% for 2027** (Rev. Proc. 2026-26), up from 9.02% in 2025. The percentage follows the plan year. — [IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-26](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-26.pdf); [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/law-and-policy/2027-affordability-percentage-for-employer-health-coverage-increases/); [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/7/21/revenue-procedure-2026-26-technical-overview-of-2027-indexing-adjustments-for-premium-tax-credits-and-affordability-standards)
- ICHRA affordability test: the monthly premium for the applicable lowest-cost silver self-only plan, minus the monthly ICHRA amount, compared with the required percentage × monthly household income. — [Thatch](https://thatch.com/blog/ichra-affordability-guide)
- VHC: if the HRA is unaffordable and the employee qualifies for APTC, they can use the APTC but must opt out of the HRA. If it is affordable, they must buy a full-price plan without APTC. — [VHC FAQ node/147](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/node/147)
- Classes (2019 HRA final rules): the ICHRA must be offered on the same terms within a class. Full-time vs part-time, salaried vs hourly, and geography are permitted classes. **Minimum class-size rules (10 employees for employers under 100) apply only to certain classes and only when the employer also offers a traditional group plan to another class.** An employer may give all part-time staff one ICHRA amount and all full-time staff another, with no group plan. The employer defines full-time and part-time. — [Foley summary of final rule](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2019/06/regulations-employers-reimburse-health-insurance/); [HUB compliance bulletin](https://www.hubinternational.com/products/employee-benefits/compliance-bulletins/2019/06/final-rules-impose-requirements-on-employers-reimbursing-for-individual-coverage/); [BASIC slides](https://www.basiconline.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Slides-New-HRA-Final-Regs-10-3-19-.pdf); [Thatch: classes](https://thatch.com/blog/the-11-types-of-ichra-classes)
- Allowances may also vary by age and family size within a class (vendor summaries). ICHRA has no contribution limits and works for employers of any size. — [Take Command](https://www.takecommandhealth.com/blog/qsehra-rules)
- Common Good Vermont's guest article describes a defined-contribution model: a fixed employer dollar amount, with employees choosing an HDHP, a buy-up PPO, or a Marketplace plan via ICHRA. An ICHRA "may let employees take the full federal subsidy if the employer's contribution is considered unaffordable." Its illustrative figure is $300/mo. — [Common Good VT / WellNorth Consulting](https://commongoodvt.org/news/beyond-the-premium-strategic-health-benefits-planning-for-non-profits/)
- Justworks offers ICHRA alongside small-group and large-group options in one comparison, with availability varying by location. — [Justworks help: large group vs small group](https://help.justworks.com/hc/en-us/articles/16971298765467-Large-Group-vs-Open-Market-Health-Insurance)

### Inferences
- **Vermont ICHRA affordability illustration (2027):** at 10.22%, a single employee earning $40K has a threshold of about $341/mo. If the VHC LCSP is about $1,250–$1,300/mo, an ICHRA below about $900–$950/mo is unaffordable for that employee, who can choose ICHRA or PTC. A $300–$500/mo ICHRA would be unaffordable for nearly every Willowell earner, preserving the choice. For an employee earning $80K (no PTC above 400% FPL is about $62.6K single in 2026), the ICHRA is pure gain.
- **Medicaid staff:** an ICHRA requires enrollment in individual health insurance (or Medicare), so Medicaid enrollees would generally not be able to use it. They lose nothing because they simply don't participate. *This is federal-rule background (29 CFR 2590.702-2 integration requirements), not verified this session.*
- **Class design for Willowell:** for example, Class A full-time (≥30 hr) gets $X/mo and Class B part-time 75%-FTE gets a lower amount or nothing. Because Willowell would not offer any group plan, minimum class sizes don't apply. Note: 75% FTE (about 30 hr) may fall on either side of a 30-hour line depending on how Willowell defines classes. Define the classes clearly in the plan document.
- ICHRA notice: generally 90 days before the plan year (background; not verified this session).
- **ICHRA vs QSEHRA for Willowell:** an ICHRA preserves PTC/VPA for anyone who opts out, which a QSEHRA does not, and it has no cap. Its cost is administrative: a vendor at roughly $20–$40 per employee per month is typical nationally (not verified this session).
- No Vermont-headquartered ICHRA administrator was found. National vendors (PeopleKeep, Take Command, Thatch, Justworks) market ICHRA nationwide. Vermont brokers (Section 7) can administer or refer.

## 5. Hybrid and alternative strategies (wage increases, opt-outs, Section 125, HSA, DPC, dental/vision, opt-out cash)

### Takeaway
The lowest-risk "do no harm" options:
- **Unconditional raises.** Taxable and MAGI-raising, but legal. The IRS itself points to them as the compliant alternative to reimbursing premiums.
- **An ICHRA set at an unaffordable level**, so staff can opt out and keep subsidies.
- **Stand-alone dental/vision** (e.g., VACEplus via chamber membership).
- **HSA contributions or direct primary care (DPC)**, now usable with bronze/catastrophic marketplace plans and DPC under OBBBA from 2026.

High-risk options:
- **Paying or reimbursing individual premiums outside a QSEHRA/ICHRA** (an "employer payment plan", $100/day/employee excise tax).
- **Conditional cash tied to buying insurance.**

### Cited Findings
- Employer payment plans: Notice 2013-54 classifies reimbursing or paying individual-market premiums as a group health plan that fails ACA market reforms. The excise tax under §4980D is "$100 per day with respect to each individual." Transition relief for employers under 50 FTE (Notice 2015-17) ended mid-2015. Notice 2015-17 indicates an employer may instead increase compensation if the increase is not conditioned on buying health coverage. — [Bricker Graydon](https://www.brickergraydon.com/benefits-insights/the-clock-runs-out-on-employers-reimbursing-individual-insurance-premiums); [Church Law & Tax: 10 key points](https://www.churchlawandtax.com/irs-developments/10-key-points-on-the-new-health-care-payments-guidance/); [CapinCrouse nonprofit update](https://capincrouse.com/important-update-on-employer-health-insurance-premium-reimbursementpayment-plans)
- Opt-out payments (Notice 2015-87 and July 2016 proposed regs): unconditional opt-out payments count as increasing the employee's cost of group coverage for affordability. Conditional payments under an "eligible opt-out arrangement" (requiring proof of other group coverage) do not. — [Woodruff Sawyer summary of Notice 2015-87](https://woodruffsawyer.com/sites/default/files/wp-content/2016/01/EmployeeBenefits-IRS-Releases-Broad-Range-of-Guidance-in-Notice-2015-87.pdf); [Benefits Law Advisor](https://www.benefitslawadvisor.com/2016/08/articles/4980h-affordability/are-you-down-with-o-o-p-s-opt-out-payments-under-the-affordable-care-act/)
- OBBBA (signed July 4, 2025), effective Jan 1, 2026: individual-market **bronze and catastrophic plans are treated as HSA-eligible HDHPs** (Sec. 71307). **Direct primary care arrangements** no longer disqualify HSA contributions, and DPC fees up to **$150/mo individual / $300/mo family** (indexed) are qualified medical expenses (Sec. 71308). 2026 HSA limits are $4,400 self-only / $8,750 family. IRS Notice 2026-05 gives guidance. — [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/new-rules-for-hsa-eligibility-and-tax-benefits/); [Instead (Mar 2026)](https://instead.com/resources/blog/hsa-eligibility-in-2026-and-what-the-obbb-act-changes); [Winston Taylor](https://www.winstontaylor.com/insights/one-big-beautiful-bill-changes-for-employer-provided-health-and-welfare-benefits-and-fringe-benefits)
- BCBSVT bronze individual and small-group plans include CDHP (HSA-compatible) options, and BCBSVT promotes HSA pairing. — [BCBSVT 2026 individual chart](https://www.bluecrossvt.org/documents/2026-individual-family-premiums-chart); [BCBSVT 2026 small group chart](https://www.bluecrossvt.org/documents/2026-small-group-premiums-chart)
- Dental/vision: VACEplus (a subsidiary of the Vermont Association of Chamber of Commerce Executives) offers Northeast Delta Dental and DeltaVision to members of any local Vermont chamber. It also offers worksite products (Colonial Life via csOne: disability, life, accident, critical illness). Employees working 20+ hr/week are eligible. Enrollment can start any time of year. Premiums are the same whether the employer pays all, some, or none. Dental/vision need only 1 enrollee; worksite products need 3. — [VACE Insurance](https://vaceinsurance.com/); [Addison County Chamber benefits](https://www.addisoncounty.com/benefits); [Addison County Chamber insurance](https://www.addisoncounty.com/insurance)
- The VHC Small Business FAQ notes that other benefits (HSAs, discount programs, stipends) "may not qualify as health insurance" and advises consulting a tax advisor. — [VHC SB FAQ](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/small-business-resources/small-business-faq)

### Inferences
- **Raise wages / unconditional stipend:** this is compliant if it is not conditioned on buying insurance or substantiation. Downsides: it is taxable (income plus FICA, and Willowell pays employer FICA), and it **raises MAGI**, which can reduce PTC/VPA or push a Medicaid-eligible staffer over 138% FPL. Model each staffer's FPL band.
- **Section 125 premium-only plan (POP):** a cafeteria plan **cannot** pay individual-market (exchange) QHP premiums pre-tax under IRC §125(f)(3) (ACA §1515). A POP is useful only alongside a group plan (employee share pre-tax). *Background law, not verified this session.*
- **HSA funding:** Willowell could contribute to staff HSAs (pre-tax, within comparability rules) for staff in HSA-eligible bronze or catastrophic plans, including VHC bronze plans with PTC from 2026. Medicaid enrollees are generally not HSA-eligible. An employer HSA contribution does not reduce PTC, because it is not an HRA. *Inference; confirm with a benefits attorney or CPA.*
- **DPC memberships:** an employer-paid DPC arrangement may itself be a group health plan, which creates ERISA/ACA compliance questions. For a <50 employer offering no other plan, risk is lower, but this needs counsel. No Addison County DPC practice was researched.
- **Medicaid-eligible staff "opting out":** with a group plan, staff can simply decline and stay on Medicaid. With an ICHRA, they decline. With a QSEHRA, they remain "provided" the benefit (but have no PTC to lose).
- **Waiver/opt-out cash:** only relevant if Willowell sponsors a group plan. An unconditional opt-out payment makes the plan less "affordable" for affordability testing, which can help staff keep PTC. Cash conditioned on buying individual coverage is an employer payment plan and risks the $100/day excise tax.

## 8. Cross-mechanism comparison for Willowell (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Given staff on Medicaid, VPA and PTC, the mechanisms that best **avoid making staff worse off** are:
1. **An ICHRA (or defined contribution via ICHRA) set at an "unaffordable" level**, so subsidized staff opt out and keep PTC/VPA while unsubsidized staff get employer dollars.
2. **Unconditional wage increases** (simplest, but they raise MAGI).
3. **Dental/vision only** (VACEplus).

A **small-group QHP** gives the best coverage per dollar (gold at about $14.3K/yr employee-only) and qualifies for a **2-year 35% 45R credit**. But if Willowell pays enough to make it affordable, offered staff lose PTC/VPA. It also costs about $80K–$115K/yr for 8 enrollees at 100% employer-paid. A **QSEHRA** is the weakest fit in Vermont: it reduces PTC dollar-for-dollar, so subsidized staff gain little, and VHC says HRAs and financial help can't be combined.

### Cited Findings
- Employer offer blocks financial help unless the coverage is unaffordable or below minimum value. — [VHC node/147](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/node/147)
- Affordability thresholds: 9.96% (2026), 10.22% (2027). — [IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-26](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-26.pdf)
- QSEHRA: dollar-for-dollar PTC reduction. — [PeopleKeep](https://www.peoplekeep.com/blog/offering-a-qsehra-and-individual-premium-tax-credits)
- 45R: 35% refundable, 2 years, SHOP/VHC-certified QHP, <25 FTE, wage phase-out from $34,100. — [IRS 2026 i8941 draft](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8941--dft.pdf)
