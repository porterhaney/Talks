# Creative compensation: tax and benefits claims checked (Willowell Foundation)

Checked 2026-10-09. Context: Willowell Foundation Inc. (EIN 03-0366363) is classified as a **501(c)(3) private operating foundation that files the 990-PF** ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/030366363)). It has about 10 early-childhood and education staff and no group health plan. The goal is to raise staff's real pay without pushing anyone over Medicaid's 138% FPL test, which is checked against monthly MAGI, and without cutting their PTC or Vermont Premium Assistance (VPA).

This is research, not legal advice. Have a benefits attorney or CPA confirm the plan documents before anything is adopted.

## 1. Retirement deferrals and MAGI: TRUE

- **PTC MAGI** is AGI plus untaxed foreign income, tax-exempt interest and non-taxable Social Security (IRC 36B(d)(2)(B)). **Medicaid MAGI** uses the same method, with exceptions listed in 42 CFR 435.603(e) ([LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/435.603)). Vermont's SPA 25-0002 repeats this method.
- **Pre-tax elective deferrals:**
  - 403(b): excluded from gross income under IRC 402(e)(3) and 403(b)(1), up to the §402(g) limit.
  - SIMPLE IRA: excluded under 402(h)/408(p).
  - Either way, they never reach W-2 Box 1 or AGI.
- **Employer contributions** (match or nonelective) are not income when contributed.
- **Roth deferrals** (§402A) are included in gross income, so they count toward MAGI.
- **2026 limits** ([Notice 2025-67](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/11/notice-2025-67-increased-retirement-plan-contribution-limits-2026.html)):
  - 403(b): $24,500 deferral; $8,000 catch-up at 50+; $11,250 catch-up at ages 60-63.
  - SIMPLE: $17,000, rising to **$18,100 for employers with 25 or fewer employees** under SECURE 2.0 §117. Catch-up $4,000 (50+) or $5,250 (ages 60-63).
- **Catches:**
  - **Employer Roth (SECURE 2.0 §604):** employees may elect to have employer match or nonelective contributions treated as Roth, and those are taxable. Plans can also offer Roth SIMPLE. Tell MAGI-sensitive staff to keep everything pre-tax.
  - **Medicaid verification is monthly and done from pay stubs.** Staff should make sure the eligibility worker uses taxable wages (gross minus pre-tax deferrals), not gross pay.
  - **Plan design:**
    - SIMPLE requires a 3% match or a 2% nonelective contribution for everyone, and the employer cannot run another plan alongside it.
    - 403(b) is open to a 501(c)(3), including a private foundation. Its universal-availability rule (403(b)(12)(A)(ii)) means that if one employee may defer, all generally may.
  - **Lowering MAGI can backfire.** Pushing MAGI below 100% FPL ends PTC eligibility. In Vermont, dropping below 138% FPL moves the person into Medicaid. Model each staffer's numbers.
  - **Upside: Saver's Match.** SECURE 2.0 §103 replaces the Saver's Credit with a federal deposit of up to $1,000 into the retirement account of low earners, starting in tax years after 2026. Low-paid staff who defer get federal money on top.

## 2. §129 dependent care at $7,500 and in-kind preschool tuition: TRUE, but pre-K only

- **Limit:** OBBBA §70404 raised §129(a)(2)(A) to **$7,500 ($3,750 for married filing separately)** for tax years starting after 12/31/2025. It is **not indexed** ([Newfront](https://www.newfront.com/blog/the-obbb-dependent-care-fsa-increase-could-backfire); [HUB](https://www.hubinternational.com/hub-resources/employee-benefits-compliance-bulletins/2025/09/dependent-care-assistance-programs-an-employer-guide)).
- **What counts:** §129 covers "the payment for, or provision of," services that would be employment-related expenses under §21 (§129(e)(1)). So in-kind care counts.
- **Valuing care in Willowell's own facility:** §129(e)(8) values it based on the employee's utilization and the value of the services provided, which in practice means the price charged to the public minus anything the employee pays.
- **Pre-K only:** Under Treas. Reg. 1.21-1(d)(5), nursery school, preschool and similar programs below kindergarten are "care." **Kindergarten and above is education** and does not count, except before- or after-school care and day camps (overnight camps do not count). A pre-K or forest-preschool program fits. K-12 tuition does not.
- **Effect on income:** The excluded amount is outside gross income, outside FICA (§3121(a)(18)) and outside FUTA, so it stays out of both MAGI tests. Report it in W-2 Box 10; any amount over $7,500 goes to Box 1.
- **Catches:**
  - **Written plan** (§129(d)(1)), reasonable notice to employees (§129(d)(6)), and an annual statement (§129(d)(7)).
  - **Nondiscrimination tests:**
    - Eligibility must not favor HCEs (§129(d)(3)).
    - No more than 25% of benefits may go to 5% owners (§129(d)(4)); this does not apply to a nonprofit.
    - **The 55% average-benefits test** (§129(d)(8)). With no HCEs (the 2026 HCE threshold uses 2025 compensation over $160,000), these tests are easy to pass.
  - **Earned income limit** (§129(b)): the exclusion cannot exceed the lower-earning spouse's earned income. A spouse who neither works nor studies generally zeroes it out.
  - **Age limit:** the child must be under 13.
  - **$7,500 is a per-household cap**, shared with any spouse's DCAP.
  - **The exclusion reduces §21 credit-eligible expenses** dollar for dollar.
  - **Private-foundation issue:** a benefit to a board member's child would be self-dealing under §4941, because board members are disqualified persons and are not employees. Officer or executive director compensation, including fringe benefits, must be reasonable (§4941(d)(2)(E)).

## 3. §117(d) at a private operating foundation: FALSE as currently classified

- **The rule:** §117(d)(2) excludes a qualified tuition reduction only for an employee of "an educational organization described in section 170(b)(1)(A)(ii)." That means its primary function is formal instruction, and it normally has a regular faculty, a curriculum and enrolled students (Reg. 1.170A-9(c)(1)). The exclusion covers education below the graduate level for the employee, spouse and dependent children. Nondiscrimination applies (§117(d)(3)) ([26 USC 117](https://www.customsmobile.com/uscode/26/117); [IRS QTR page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/qualified-tuition-reduction)).
- **Why Willowell fails today:** Under §509(a)(1), any organization described in §170(b)(1)(A)(ii) **is not a private foundation**. An entity classified as a private operating foundation filing the 990-PF is therefore, by its IRS classification, not a §170(b)(1)(A)(ii) school.
- **What qualifying would take:** a reclassification request to the IRS (Form 8940) showing the school test is met. That means formal instruction must be the *primary function* of the whole organization, which is hard to show for a land-based or nature program. It could also trigger the §507 private-foundation termination rules, such as the §507(b)(1)(B) 60-month termination, and the school would need a racially nondiscriminatory policy under Rev. Proc. 75-50 with annual Schedule E certification.
- **Other uncertainty:** whether preschool counts as "education" under §117(d) is not settled by authority. Commentators read "below the graduate level" broadly, but fees, aftercare and camps are excluded.
- **Practical answer:** use §129 for pre-K, up to $7,500. Tuition for K and above, beyond §129, would be taxable wages.

## 4. §127 educational assistance: TRUE

- **Limit and status:** $5,250 per year. OBBBA §70412 made qualified student-loan payments (§127(c)(1)(B)) permanent and indexes the $5,250 for inflation **starting in 2027**; it stays flat at $5,250 for 2026 ([Ogletree on IRS FS-2026-10](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/irs-clarifies-tax-free-educational-assistance-cap-to-adjust-with-inflation-beginning-in-2027/)).
- **Effect on income:** excluded from gross income and from FICA (§3121(a)(18)), so it stays out of both MAGI tests.
- **Catches:**
  - **Employee only.** It cannot pay for dependents' education.
  - **Written plan, nondiscrimination and notice rules.** No more than 5% of benefits may go to 5% owners, which does not apply here.
  - **No cash or other taxable benefit may be offered in lieu of it** (§127(b)(4)), so it cannot be a salary-reduction plan.
  - Courses involving sports, games or hobbies are excluded unless job-related.
  - **Student-loan interest paid under §127 cannot also be deducted under §221.**
- **Bonus: §132(d) working-condition fringe.** Job-related education that maintains or improves skills in the current job, such as ECE coursework or a CDA for a current teacher, can be excluded **with no dollar cap** (Reg. 1.132-5(a)(1)(v), 1.162-5). This does not apply if the education qualifies the person for a new trade or business.
- **Student loans:** lower AGI also lowers income-driven repayment amounts. Willowell, as a 501(c)(3), is a qualifying PSLF employer.

## 5. Employer HSA contributions for bronze/HDHP enrollees in the individual market: TRUE, with a comparability trap

- **Bronze plans now qualify.** OBBBA §71307 treats individual-market bronze and catastrophic plans as HDHPs from 1/1/2026 (§223(c)(2)(H)).
  - [Notice 2026-5](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-05.pdf) Q&A-6: plans bought **off-Exchange** count if the same plan is available on an Exchange.
  - Bronze bought through an ICHRA still counts.
  - **SHOP or small-group bronze does not** (Q&A-8).
- **Allowed with no employer HDHP.** An employer may contribute to an employee's HSA even when the HDHP is individually purchased ([Reg. 54.4980G-3 Q&A-7(b) and Example 2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/54.4980G-3)).
- **Tax treatment:** excluded from income under §106(d), and from FICA and income tax withholding (Notice 2004-2 Q&A-19). It stays out of both MAGI tests.
- **PTC is not affected.**
  - An HSA is an account, not a group health plan, and **is not MEC** (T.D. 9660 preamble on §6055 reporting).
  - It is not an HRA, so the HRA affordability rules in Reg. 1.36B-2(c)(5) don't apply.
  - It creates no "offer" of employer coverage.
  - DOL FAB 2004-01 says it is not an ERISA plan if participation is voluntary and the employer doesn't restrict the account.
- **Comparability catch (§4980G; Reg. 54.4980G-3 through -5):**
  - An employer that funds HSAs for employees in an HDHP it does not sponsor must make **comparable contributions to all comparable participating employees** with any HDHP. That means the same dollar amount or the same percentage of deductible, by coverage tier.
  - Full-time (30+ hours) and part-time employees are tested separately.
  - Willowell must make a reasonable good-faith effort to identify eligible employees.
  - The penalty for failing is **35% of all employer HSA contributions**.
  - Two ways to vary amounts: route contributions through a §125 cafeteria plan, where §125 nondiscrimination applies instead of comparability; or give non-HCEs larger contributions (Reg. 54.4980G-6).
- **Medicaid catch:** an HSA-eligible individual must have no disqualifying non-HDHP coverage (§223(c)(1)(A)(ii)). **Medicaid enrollment disqualifies** (consensus reading; Medicaid is not among the permitted coverages in §223(c)(1)(B)/(c)(3)). Medicaid enrollees are therefore not "eligible individuals" and fall outside the comparability pool. A spouse's or child's Medicaid does not disqualify the employee.
- **Employee-funded option:** a §125 plan whose only benefit is HSA salary reduction is allowed, and it cuts MAGI and FICA dollar for dollar.

## 6. Direct primary care (DPC): OBBBA facts TRUE; "employer-paid DPC is harmless" is FALSE or high-risk

- **What OBBBA §71308 did:**
  - A DPC service arrangement no longer disqualifies HSA eligibility (§223(c)(1)(E)) if total fees are **≤ $150/month individual or ≤ $300/month family**, indexed after 2026.
  - DPC fees are qualified medical expenses payable from an HSA (§223(d)(2)(C)).
  - The arrangement must be primary care only, from primary care practitioners, for a fixed periodic fee only. It excludes general-anesthesia procedures, prescription drugs other than vaccines, and non-ambulatory labs. Annual billing is allowed, for example $1,800 per year.
  - Fees above the cap are still QMEs reimbursable from an HSA, but they disqualify contributions (Notice 2026-5 Q&A-13, -20).
- **Employer-paid DPC is a group health plan.**
  - Treasury: "If the direct primary care arrangement fee is paid by an employer, that payment arrangement would be a group health plan" ([REG-109755-19 preamble, 85 FR 35398](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-06-10/html/2020-12213.htm)).
  - Notice 2026-5 Q&A-18 treats employer-paid DPC fees, including through §125, as **§106-excluded employer health coverage**. Those fees cannot also be reimbursed from the HSA.
- **PTC risk:**
  - A self-insured group health plan is an "eligible employer-sponsored plan" (Reg. 1.5000A-2(c)(1)(ii)) and **is MEC unless it consists solely of excepted benefits** (Reg. 1.5000A-2(g)).
  - DPC is not an excepted benefit. The on-site-clinic exception doesn't fit an off-site membership practice.
  - Under §36B(c)(2)(C)(iii), an employee **enrolled** in an eligible employer-sponsored plan cannot get PTC for that month, **regardless of affordability or minimum value**. Merely *offering* a DPC-only plan would not block PTC, because it fails minimum value.
  - Result: **an employer-paid DPC membership plausibly zeroes out PTC and VPA for enrollees.** No IRS guidance squarely addresses DPC; this is the same logic the IRS applied to stand-alone HRAs in Notice 2013-54.
- **Market-reform risk:** a DPC-only group health plan cannot meet PHSA 2711 or 2713, because it doesn't cover all USPSTF preventive services. That triggers the **§4980D excise tax of $100/day per affected person**. The §4980D(d) small-employer exception covers only insured coverage.
  - One vendor claims the 21st Century Cures Act fixes this. That is wrong: Cures §18001 created QSEHRAs, and a QSEHRA reduces PTC.
- **ERISA:** the plan would need a plan document and an SPD.
- **No Vermont DPC statute was found** (no "not insurance" safe harbor identified).
- **Safer route:** have Willowell fund the HSA (item 5), and let staff on bronze plans pay the DPC fee themselves from the HSA. Alternatively, pay a taxable wage increase (item 10).

## 7. Excepted benefits and PTC: TRUE, with a tax catch on indemnity plans

- **Why PTC is unaffected:** MEC excludes coverage that consists solely of excepted benefits (§5000A(f)(3); [Reg. 1.5000A-2(g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.5000A-2)). Enrolling in, or being offered, stand-alone excepted benefits therefore doesn't block PTC.
- **Conditions to qualify as excepted:**
  - **Limited-scope dental and vision** (26 CFR 54.9831-1(c)(3)): offered under a separate policy, or not an integral part of a medical plan, so participants can decline it.
  - **Hospital or fixed indemnity** (54.9831-1(c)(4)): pays a fixed dollar amount per day or period, regardless of expenses; is not coordinated with other coverage; and is offered under a separate policy.
  - The 2024 group fixed-indemnity notice requirement was reportedly vacated in *ManhattanLife v. HHS* (E.D. Tex.); verify.
- **Tax catch on indemnity payouts:** if hospital-indemnity premiums are employer-paid or paid pre-tax through §125, the fixed payouts are **taxable income to the extent they exceed unreimbursed medical expenses** (CCA 201703013, as narrowed by CCA 201719025) ([CFTD summary](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2017/2/2/fixed-indemnity-medical-plan-payments-are-taxable-as-wages-when-received-if-premiums-were-not-paid-from-after-tax-funds)).
  - That income lands in the month paid, which matters for the monthly Medicaid test.
  - **Fix:** have employees pay indemnity premiums **after tax**, or impute the premium as wages.
- **Dental and vision** reimburse actual expenses, so the §105(b) exclusion applies and they have no MAGI effect.

## 8. Medicaid lump-sum rule and Vermont: PARTLY TRUE, with a predictability catch

- **Federal rule:** "An amount received as a lump sum is counted as income only in the month received" ([42 CFR 435.603(e)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/435.603)). This dates from the original 2012 rule (77 FR 17206); 81 FR 86456 (2016) amended other parts of the section.
  - Applicants are tested on current monthly income (§435.603(h)(1)).
  - For enrollees, states choose between monthly income and projected annual income (§435.603(h)(2)).
  - **States may prorate "reasonably predictable" future income** shown by a contract or "a clear history of predictable fluctuations" (§435.603(h)(3)).
- **Vermont:**
  - HBEE Part 5 §28.03 repeats the lump-sum rule verbatim ([2018 adopted Part 5](https://humanservices.vermont.gov/sites/ahsnew/files/hbee-part-five-adopted-rule-18-063.pdf)).
  - **SPA VT-25-0002** (approved 5/16/2025, effective 2/1/2025) moved current beneficiaries from projected annual income to **current monthly income**. It also **checks "Yes" to considering reasonably predictable changes in income** ([SPA](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/spa/downloads/VT-25-0002.pdf); [GCR 24-147](https://humanservices.vermont.gov/sites/ahsnew/files/documents/24-147-P-GCR-MAGI-methodologies-SPA.pdf)). HBEE Part 5 was amended in 2025 to match; the final text was not retrieved.
- **Catches:**
  1. **A recurring, promised annual bonus is the textbook "predictable" income Vermont may prorate.** A truly discretionary, non-contractual, one-time payment is safer as a "lump sum."
  2. **States differ on whether a bonus is a "lump sum" or simply wages for that month.** Either way it hits only that month, but in that month the person can be over 138% FPL.
  3. **OBBBA:**
     - **Six-month redeterminations** for expansion adults (§71107), starting with renewals after 12/31/2026.
     - **Work/community-engagement requirements**, 80 hours/month (§71119), effective by 1/1/2027; CMS issued an interim final rule on 6/3/2026. Staff working 80+ hours satisfy it.
     - More frequent checks mean more chances for a bonus month to be seen.
  4. **Marketplace side:** PTC uses **annual** household MAGI for the tax year (§36B(d)(2)). The whole bonus counts, and APTC is reconciled on Form 8962. Many staff sit in the PTC band, so a $3K bonus raises their annual MAGI by $3K.

## 9. Charity paying an enrollee's net premium: PARTLY TRUE

- **What the regulation requires:** [45 CFR 156.1250](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/156.1250) requires individual-market QHP issuers to accept payments **only** from Ryan White programs, tribes and tribal organizations, and state, federal or local government programs. Issuers **may refuse** other third parties.
- **CMS guidance:** the 2/7/2014 FAQ said the concerns in the 11/4/2013 FAQ do not apply to **private not-for-profit foundations** that use **financial-need criteria (not health status)** and pay for the **full policy year**. This is non-binding.
- **Vermont:** **no Vermont statute or DFR bulletin requiring carriers to accept charity payments was found** (Idaho has one; Vermont does not appear to). BCBSVT and MVP bill individual and VHC enrollees directly. **Get written confirmation from each carrier** before launching a fund.
- **PTC is unaffected.** The credit depends on the benchmark premium and income (§36B), not on who pays the enrollee's share. Need-based charitable aid is a gift (§102), not income, so it doesn't raise MAGI.
- **Big catch:** if Willowell funds, controls or earmarks the money for its own employees, it is **compensation** (taxable). It is also an **employer payment plan**, which is a group health plan that fails market reforms (Notice 2013-54; [Notice 2015-17 Q&A-5](https://www.swlaw.com/blogs/employee-benefits/2015/03/11/irs-issues-more-guidance-on-employers-that-pay-for-individual-health-insurance-policies-for-employees-gives-limited-relief-to-small-employers/)), and it plausibly counts as employer coverage.
  - The fund must be independent, open to a charitable class broader than Willowell staff (for example, all Addison County early educators), need-based, and free of employer control.
  - A private foundation granting to its own employees also raises §4941 and §4945 concerns.

## 10. Taxable "health stipend": TRUE

- **Allowed.** Raising pay is not an employer payment plan or group health plan if the payment is **not conditioned on buying coverage** and Willowell doesn't **endorse a particular policy, form or issuer**. Giving staff Exchange and PTC information is fine (Notice 2015-17 Q&A-4; Notice 2013-54; DOL Tech. Rel. 2013-03).
- **What makes it a plan:** dedicating the money to premiums, requiring proof of coverage, or reimbursing premiums turns it into an employer payment plan, **even if it is taxed** (Q&A-5).
- **Effects:**
  - Full taxable wages: federal and Vermont income tax, FICA (Willowell pays 7.65%), and Vermont UI wages.
  - **Counts in monthly Medicaid MAGI and annual PTC MAGI**, so it reduces PTC and VPA on the slope and can cross 138% FPL.
  - Calling it a "health stipend" is a labeling risk. Call it a wage supplement.
- **Private-foundation note:** pay to officers or the executive director must remain reasonable (§4941).

## Bottom line for design (MAGI-neutral or MAGI-reducing levers, best first)

1. **§129 pre-K care in-kind, up to $7,500 per household.**
2. **§127 / §132(d) education and student-loan help.**
3. **Employer HSA contributions** for bronze enrollees (comparable to all eligible staff), with DPC paid by staff from the HSA.
4. **403(b) employer contributions plus pre-tax deferrals.**
5. **Employee-paid or employer-paid dental and vision** as excepted benefits. Make hospital indemnity after-tax only.
6. Taxable wages or bonuses last. Model each staffer's monthly FPL position.

**Avoid:**
- Employer-paid DPC.
- Paying or earmarking individual premiums.
- An employer-controlled premium fund.
- §117(d) unless Willowell is reclassified.
