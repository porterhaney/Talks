# Peer Vermont ECE Centers: Employer-Paid Health Insurance and How It Is Funded

Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: Burlington/Chittenden County and Addison County early childhood programs. Most job-posting data comes from aggregators (Idealist, Indeed, ZipRecruiter, NAAEE, BetterTeam), and some of it is undated or expired. The research tools returned search-engine summaries for many pages. Items marked "(search snippet)" were not opened and read in full.

**Main finding up front:** I found **no public source (job posting, center website, news story or 990) showing a Vermont ECE center that pays 90% of staff health premiums.** Many Burlington-area centers advertise "health insurance" (Burlington Children's Space, Heartworks, Robin's Nest, Champlain Valley Head Start, the YMCA, First Roots–Wild Roots), but none of them publish the employer's share of the premium. One older Burlington Children's Space posting (2022) reportedly advertised **"free MVP Health Insurance"** (100% employer-paid), but I could not open that posting to confirm it. The board member's "up to 90%" figure is plausible for some of these employers, but the evidence would have to come from direct inquiry or offer letters, not from public listings.

## 1. Which specific centers advertise employer-paid health insurance, and at what %?

### Takeaway
Health insurance is advertised by most mid-size and large Chittenden County nonprofits and by Head Start, but **none of them publish a premium percentage.** In Addison County, the largest center (Otter Creek Child Center, about 34 positions after expansion) still did **not** offer health insurance as of Sept 2025, according to VTDigger. A small rural center (Crockett Academy, Williston) was quoted about $800/month per employee from MVP and called it infeasible.

### Inferences
- The "up to 90%" figure most plausibly describes a few Burlington nonprofits that have unusual funding, scale or legacy (for example BCS, which once advertised "free" coverage, or Head Start/CVOEO and YMCA-type parent organizations). I could not find it in any public posting. Burlington-area employers rarely publish the employer share. The board member's source is probably word of mouth or an offer letter.
- The plan language in the current BCS posting ("BCBSVT/MVP… HSA accumulates monthly") points to a model common among small VT employers that try to pay a high % of premium: buy a **lower-premium high-deductible plan on Vermont Health Connect's small-group market, pay most or all of the employee-only premium, and seed an HSA.** This keeps the employer's dollar cost far below the gold-plan cost. This is an inference and should be confirmed with BCS.
- "% of premium" usually refers to **employee-only** coverage. A center paying 90% of a single HDHP premium may pay much less toward family tiers.

### Gaps
- No source states an employer premium % for any of BCS, Heartworks, Robin's Nest, CVHS, YMCA, Lund, King Street, Sara Holbrook, Trinity, First Roots–Wild Roots, Mary Johnson or Bristol Family Center. Direct calls or emails to the EDs or HR are needed.
- No search results were found for Sara Holbrook Community Center benefits, "Children's Garden," UVM Campus Children's School teacher benefits, or Middlebury College Children's Center. That center may not exist as a separate entity; Otter Creek appears to be the college-affiliated center.
- VTAEYC job board and Front Porch Forum postings were not directly searchable.
- Bristol Family Center's benefits line on its 990 was not shown on CauseIQ. A full 990 PDF (Part IX, lines 7–9) would reveal its spending on pension and other employee benefits.

## 2. Funding models behind benefits

### Takeaway
The programs that can offer health insurance usually have at least one structural advantage beyond tuition: (a) a federal or state grant stream that treats fringe as an allowable cost (Head Start, Parent Child Centers); (b) a large parent organization or institutional sponsor (CVOEO, YMCA, UVM Health Network, Middlebury College); (c) scale (roughly 25+ staff, Heartworks' multi-site chain); or (d) Act 76's higher CCFAP reimbursement rates, which have greatly increased program revenue since Jan 2024 and which some programs explicitly used to start health insurance. None of these is a dedicated health-insurance subsidy; benefits come out of general operating revenue.

### Cited Findings

**Act 76 / CCFAP (state subsidy rates), 2024–2025**
- Act 76 (2023) puts about **$125M/yr** into child care, funded largely by a **0.44% payroll tax** (employers may pass up to a quarter to workers). It expanded CCFAP eligibility to families earning up to **575% of FPL**, paid at higher weekly rates — [Seven Days](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/education-news/vermonts-bold-investment-in-childcare-is-largely-paying-off/); [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2025/09/29/otter-creek-child-center-to-expand-amid-overwhelming-child-care-demand-in-addison-county/)
- Higher reimbursement rates started in **January 2024**. ABC & LOL Child Care (Cabot/St. Johnsbury/Peacham; **65 staff**, 200+ children) used Act 76 readiness payments and the higher rates to raise wages and **"begin offering health insurance to program employees for the first time" as of March 1, 2024.** The employer share was not stated — [Vermont Business Magazine, Mar 2024](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2024/march/18/cabot-celebrates-new-child-care-program-made-possible-act-76)
- An open letter from 15 program directors (May 2024) said programs are "increasing compensation for our staff, and in some instances, offering health insurance and other employee benefits for the first time." This is an opinion piece, not reporting — [VTDigger op-ed](https://vtdigger.org/2024/05/23/child-care-program-directors-and-owners-act-76-is-leading-to-newfound-stability-for-child-care-programs-and-families/); [Waterbury Roundabout](https://www.waterburyroundabout.org/opinion-archive/ilg6wabaxxxpb0sr6pkfohwe3iroal)
- LGK: Act 76 funds let Tammie's Early Care & Education (a Thetford family home program) "purchase health insurance" — [LGK](https://letsgrowkids.org/child-care-investing-in-vermont-future-progress-act-76)
- A Building Bright Futures provider quote says subsidy changes "helped allow us to offer health and dental insurance" (search snippet) — [BBF](https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/vermonts-child-care-update-how-new-policies-are-supporting-families-and-child-care-providers/)
- **Otter Creek:** increased subsidies "added over $100,000 to the center's budget," which funded significant raises. The share of families receiving state aid rose from **44% to over 60%** — [Middlebury Campus](https://www.middleburycampus.com/article/2025/10/otter-creek-child-care-center-to-reopen-after-months-of-renovation-aiding-child-care-crisis)
- Seven Days (Nov 2025): nearly 4 in 5 owners and directors reported higher wages in 2024, but the article gives no statistic on health insurance uptake — [Seven Days](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/education-news/vermonts-bold-investment-in-childcare-is-largely-paying-off/)

**Institutional / employer sponsor**
- **Middlebury College → Otter Creek:** more than **$360,000/yr through United Way of Addison County**, mostly directed to Otter Creek, for over 10 years. Capital gifts total over $5M, plus a $6M anonymous alumni gift for community initiatives that partly funded the $11–12M renovation. Leahy congressionally directed spending added more than $3.5M via LGK. At least half of enrolled children belong to college faculty or staff. **Even with this sponsor, health insurance was not yet offered in Sept 2025** — [Middlebury Campus](https://www.middleburycampus.com/article/2025/10/otter-creek-child-care-center-to-reopen-after-months-of-renovation-aiding-child-care-crisis); [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2025/09/29/otter-creek-child-center-to-expand-amid-overwhelming-child-care-demand-in-addison-county/)
- **UVM Health Network** operates its own employee-dependent early learning center (2025) as a retention benefit. Its staff are probably covered by the hospital system's plan, but this is unconfirmed — [UVM Health newsroom](https://www.uvmhealth.org/newsroom/childcare-options-to-expand-for-uvm-health-network-employees-with-opening-of-early-learning-center)

**Federal Head Start (CVOEO's Champlain Valley Head Start)**
- The 2024 Head Start rule (§1302.90(f), effective **Aug 1, 2028**) requires programs, for full-time staff (**30+ hrs/wk**), to "**provide or facilitate access to high-quality affordable health care coverage**." The preamble says programs may either "(1) provide and contribute to employer-sponsored health care coverage, or (2) educate, connect, and facilitate the enrollment of employees in … the Marketplace … or Medicaid," and it **does not set an employer % or require parity with public schools.** Agencies with **≤200 funded slots are exempt** — [Federal Register, Aug 21, 2024 (2024-18279)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-21/pdf/2024-18279.pdf)
- A **May 12, 2026 proposed rule** ("Restoring Flexibility To Support Head Start Program Access") would remove the §1302.90(e) wage and (f) benefit requirements. Whether it has been finalized is unknown — [Federal Register 2026-09383](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09383/restoring-flexibility-to-support-head-start-program-access)
- Head Start benefits are paid from the federal grant (fringe is an allowable budget line) and sit within CVOEO's agency-wide plan. This is an inference from the grant structure. The CVHS site says the program is a "Best Places to Work in Vermont" honoree — [CVHS](https://champlainvalleyheadstart.org/)

**State grant-funded Parent Child Centers (e.g., Addison County PCC)**
- A 2023 PCC Network funding-gap analysis found: "**State contributes $11,738 [per employee toward health insurance], PCCN on average $8,528. 2 out of 15 Parent Child Centers can't afford to contribute towards health insurance.**" It put the network-wide health benefit gap at about **$620,375**. Retirement contributions are 17% (state) vs 3% (PCC) — [Amy Johnson, PCCN Funding Gap, 2-9-2023](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/House%20Human%20Services/Parent%20Child%20Centers/W~Amy%20Johnson~Parent%20Child%20Center%20Network%20Funding%20Gap~2-9-2023.pdf)
- In 2024 the PCCN asked for **+$721,945** in base grant funding specifically for health insurance cost increases — [Bailey testimony 2-20-2024](https://legislature.Vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/House%20Appropriations/FY%202025%20Budget/Public%20Hearings/W~Donna%20Bailey~Parent%20Child%20Center%20Network~2-20-2024.pdf)

**City of Burlington Early Learning Initiative (ELI)**
- ELI was launched FY2018 with **$500,000/yr from PILOT funds**. Capacity grants of about $785k were awarded 2018–2022 for infant/toddler capital work and stabilizing slots, including **$150,000 to Sara Holbrook Community Center and $130,000 to the Greater Burlington YMCA** for capital campaigns. It also runs the First Steps training program (with a $1,000 participant bonus) and First Steps family scholarships. **I found no ELI money for wages or health benefits** (search snippets) — [Burlington ELI](https://www.burlingtonvt.gov/earlylearning); [VTDigger 2017](https://vtdigger.org/2017/05/19/burlington-announces-plans-500000-low-income-child-care/); [Harvard Zaentz: First Steps](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/first-steps-training-program)

**Cost benchmark**
- About **$800/month per employee** (MVP quote to a Williston center, 2025) — [Seven Days](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/education-news/vermonts-bold-investment-in-childcare-is-largely-paying-off/). The State of Vermont pays 80% of premiums for its own employees, which serves as a public-sector benchmark — [VT Careers](https://careers.vermont.gov/content/What-We-Offer/?locale=en_US)

### Inferences
- **Willowell math (rough, my estimate):** at about $800/mo, covering 10 staff at 90% would cost roughly 10 × $800 × 12 × 0.9 ≈ **$86,000/yr**. An HDHP employee-only plan with an employer-seeded HSA could plausibly cost much less. This needs a broker quote.
- The peers most likely to pay 90% are Head Start (federal fringe), YMCA/Lund/CVOEO (parent organization's large-group plan) and hospital or college-sponsored centers. Stand-alone small nonprofits like Bristol Family Center and Otter Creek (before its expansion) mostly have not offered health insurance even after Act 76.
- **For Willowell, the most relevant lever is Act 76 CCFAP revenue.** BCS's program revenue tripled from 2021 to 2024. If Wren's Nest is not enrolled in CCFAP, or serves few subsidy-eligible families, it is missing the main revenue source that let peer centers add benefits. This is an inference; Willowell's CCFAP status was not researched here.

### Gaps
- No public source breaks down how any specific Burlington center funds its premium contribution (for example, a "health insurance line funded by X" statement).
- Lund and CVOEO 990 benefit lines were not pulled; they would show agency-wide benefits as a % of salaries.
- No Vermont shared-services or pooled-purchasing health plan for ECE programs was found. The Head Start rule preamble notes commenters asked for a "collective into which small programs could buy," but none exists federally — [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-21/pdf/2024-18279.pdf). The 2025–2026 VT biennium (S.206 ECE licensure bill) shows no health-pool provision in what I found — [VTAEYC biennium recap](https://www.vtaeyc.org/2026/06/the-2025-2026-legislative-biennium-two-years-of-child-care-investment-and-progres/)

## 5. Patterns: what makes 90% employer-paid feasible?

### Takeaway
Based on the evidence, the most reliable predictors of meaningful employer-paid health insurance in VT ECE are: (1) a **parent organization or sponsor with a large-group plan** (CVOEO/Head Start, YMCA, UVM Health Network); (2) **a dedicated public grant stream with fringe built in** (Head Start; the state PCC grant averaging $8,528/employee); (3) **scale of roughly 25–65 staff with diversified revenue** (ABC & LOL at 65 staff added insurance after Act 76; Heartworks multi-site); and (4) **high CCFAP/Act 76 revenue share**. Stand-alone programs with fewer than 30 staff (Bristol Family Center with 28 employees, Otter Creek before its expansion, Crockett Academy) generally still do not offer health insurance as of 2025.

### Cited Findings
- PCCs on state grants average **$8,528/employee** toward health insurance, and 2 of 15 contribute nothing — [PCCN Funding Gap 2023](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/House%20Human%20Services/Parent%20Child%20Centers/W~Amy%20Johnson~Parent%20Child%20Center%20Network%20Funding%20Gap~2-9-2023.pdf)
- Head Start federal rule threshold: 30+ hrs = full-time, with an exemption for ≤200 slots — [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-21/pdf/2024-18279.pdf)
- 65-staff ABC & LOL added insurance in 2024 — [VBM](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2024/march/18/cabot-celebrates-new-child-care-program-made-possible-act-76)
- Otter Creek, even with Middlebury College's $360k/yr, had not yet added health insurance as of 9/2025 — [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2025/09/29/otter-creek-child-center-to-expand-amid-overwhelming-child-care-demand-in-addison-county/); [Middlebury Campus](https://www.middleburycampus.com/article/2025/10/otter-creek-child-care-center-to-reopen-after-months-of-renovation-aiding-child-care-crisis)
- BCS (about $1.3M revenue, about $936k payroll in FY2024) advertises BCBS/MVP plus an HSA — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/222533646); [Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit-job/7685b3cc83d04768a7885d22b129aa60-preschool-teacher-500-sign-on-bonus-burlington-childrens-space-burlington)

### Inferences
- **What Willowell may be "missing"** compared with Burlington peers: (a) CCFAP/Act 76 subsidy revenue at scale, since BCS's program revenue tripled 2021–2024; (b) a parent organization's group plan; (c) a lower-cost HDHP plus HSA design that lets a small employer advertise a high employer % at a moderate dollar cost; and (d) possibly, existing AmeriCorps member health coverage for stipend roles.
- The 30-hour full-time line (Head Start, ACA) is the common eligibility cut-off. Willowell staff at 75–100% FTE would mostly qualify under such a rule.
- A 10-person employer is in Vermont's small-group market (Vermont Health Connect, BCBSVT or MVP). There is no ECE-specific pool. Peers that buy through a parent organization escape small-group pricing.

### Gaps
- No data on the actual employer % at any peer. **Recommended next step:** have a board member or the ED email 4–5 peer EDs (BCS, Robin's Nest, CVHS/CVOEO HR, Bristol Family Center, Mary Johnson) asking for (1) employer % for single and family tiers, (2) plan type (HDHP/HSA or gold), (3) the hours threshold, and (4) which revenue line funds it.
