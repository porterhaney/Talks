# Creative and non-obvious ways to fund or provide health security for Willowell staff

Researched Oct 9, 2026. Context: Willowell Foundation runs Wren's Nest Forest Preschool (Bristol VT; licensed center; 5 STARS; accepts CCFAP; Act 166 UPK partner) plus middle and high school programs. It has about 10 staff and no health benefits.

Companion files in this folder: `cliff_mechanics.md` (Medicaid/PTC cliffs), `employer_mechanisms.md` (QSEHRA/ICHRA/small group), `grants_philanthropy.md`, `peer_centers.md`, `vt_ece_state_programs.md`.

Confidence tags: [V] = verified from a primary or official source fetched today. [S] = from secondary or search-summary sources. [?] = uncertain or inferred.

---

## TL;DR: ranked by likely dollar impact for Willowell

1. **Enroll every eligible family in CCFAP. This is probably the largest untapped revenue.** The state pays a flat **$439/wk for a full-time preschooler (26-50 hrs) at a licensed center, whatever the program's tuition.** The Addison County median full-time preschool tuition is **$300/wk**. The program receives up to the state rate as family share plus state payment, and eligibility runs to **575% FPL**, about $190K for a family of 4. So almost every CCFAP family is worth more to Wren's Nest than a private-pay family, and the family usually pays less. This could plausibly produce **$3K-$7K more per enrolled child per school year**, money that could fund an ICHRA/QSEHRA. See §1 for the UPK and hours traps.
2. **Fiddlehead Family Health Care, a direct primary care (DPC) practice in Bristol.** It is a few blocks from Willowell and costs about **$75-$100/mo per adult**. It works as a low-cost add-on paired with marketplace bronze + HSA. Note: it reports being "almost full," with a waitlist.
3. **Advocacy: model a Vermont version of Connecticut's 2027 ECE health subsidy or Michigan's Nurture Benefits.** Pitch it through the VTAEYC + LGK Action Network agenda and the BBF Addison Regional Council. Nothing is pre-filed in Vermont for 2027 yet.
4. **A pooled employer or PEO is legal in Vermont but unproven for health coverage.** It needs a DFR inquiry. A three-share plan or community benefit fund would be a multi-year build, not a 2027 fix.
5. **Retirement:** Vermont Saves defaults to a **Roth** IRA, which does *not* lower MAGI. Steering staff to its **Traditional** option, or to a SIMPLE IRA/403(b), lowers MAGI and can raise premium tax credits and the 2027+ Saver's Match.

## 1. CCFAP revenue maximization

### Facts
- **Income eligibility goes to 575% FPL, with no family share below 175% FPL.** These are the official guidelines effective **Mar 22, 2026** [V]: https://dcf.vermont.gov/benefits/ccfap/income-guidelines
  - The weekly family share is $0 at or below 175% FPL. It then rises $25 per 25-point FPL band, from $50 at 200% to **$425 at 575%**.
  - Household of 4: 175% = $4,813/mo (~$57.8K/yr); 400% = $11,000/mo ($132K) with a $250/wk share; 575% = $15,813/mo (~$190K) with a $425/wk share.
  - Household of 3 or fewer: 575% = $13,091/mo (~$157K).
  - The family share is **per family**, not per child. With multiple providers, the youngest child's provider is paid first [V, Understanding Payments PDF below].
  - Families also need a qualifying **service need** (work, school or training, job search, health, etc.). Authorized hours follow that need [S].
- **2026 state rates, licensed center, weekly** (schedule "Effective July 13, 2025," still the live file on Oct 9, 2026) [V]: https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Policies%20Procedures%20Guidance/CDD-Guidance-CCFAP-Capped-Rates.pdf
  - Preschool (3 to under 6): **full-time (26-50 hrs) $439**, **part-time (1-25 hrs) $240**, extended care (over 50 hrs) $597.
  - Infant: $495 / $271. Toddler: $465 / $255. School-age: $371 / $204.
  - [?] I found no July 2026 rate change. The FY27 budget was still under debate in spring 2026.
- **How payment works** [V] (CCFAP "Understanding Payments," rev. 2024-25): https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/CCFAP/CCFAP-Understanding-Payments.pdf
  - "Payment will be based on the state rate, **regardless of the provider's established rates**."
  - Providers "do **not** have to raise your rates to receive the maximum amount the State pays." In the "Jane Smith" example, a $250 tuition provider still receives the state rate minus the family share, plus the family share.
  - If tuition is **above** the state rate, the remainder is the family's responsibility.
  - Providers **may waive or collect** the family share.
- **The UPK interaction is the key trap** [V] (Act 76 FAQ Q8): https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/Act76/ACT-76-FAQs.pdf
  - "If a child is enrolled in an Act 166 program, their 10 hours of PreK are **deducted from the CCFAP certificate**."
  - UPK pays **$4,106/child for 2026-27**, covering 10 hrs/wk for 35 weeks (about $117/wk) [V]: https://education.vermont.gov/node/5219
- **Private tuition benchmark** (DCF 2024 Market Rate Survey, Addison County, licensed preschool) [V]: https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/Reports/CC-MRS/CC-MRS-Report-2024.pdf
  - Full-time: average **$278.70**, median **$300**, 75th percentile $325, versus the state rate **$439**.
  - Part-time: average $167, median $178, versus $240.
  - Statewide licensed full-time preschool median is $325.
- **Tuition cap:** annual tuition increases are capped at **5.4%** for July 2026 to June 2027. The cap limits raising private tuition, but **not** CCFAP receipts [S, see `vt_ece_state_programs.md`].

### What this means for Wren's Nest (8:00-2:30 = 6.5 h/day)
Treat these figures as illustrative. Verify Wren's Nest's actual days per week, tuition and UPK invoicing.

| Scenario (per child, per week) | Private-pay family | CCFAP family (any income ≤575% FPL) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 days, 32.5 h, **no UPK** | tuition (Addison median ~$300) | **$439** (family share + state) |
| 5 days, 32.5 h, **with UPK** (certificate drops to 22.5 h, part-time) | UPK ~$117 + family pays rest of tuition | UPK ~$117 + **$240** = ~$357 |
| 5 days + **extended care to 4:30** (42.5 h, minus 10 UPK = 32.5 h, full-time) | UPK + tuition + extended fee | UPK ~$117 + **$439** = ~$556 |
| 4 days (26 h, no UPK) | 4-day tuition | **$439** (26 h is exactly the full-time floor) [?] confirm hours counting |

- Over a ~36-week school year, a CCFAP family generates about **$2K-$5K more per child** than a median-tuition private family. Extended-care users with UPK generate more.
- **Ten converted families would bring in about $20K-$50K/yr.** That is roughly enough to fund QSEHRA/ICHRA allowances of $400-$500/mo for 4-8 staff.
- **Family-side example:** a 4-person family at 300% FPL (~$99K) pays a **$150/wk** share, less than the $300 median tuition. The state adds $289. **Both sides gain.**
- **Policy-sensitive design choices:**
  - Offering **extended care** and making sure full-day authorized hours are on the certificate is the single biggest lever. Confirm with CDD how certificate hours are set: they come from the family's need, not just the program's schedule.
  - Make sure Willowell **collects** the family share rather than waiving it, except as deliberate scholarship.
  - Look at **staff's own children** enrolled at Wren's Nest. Staff are likely CCFAP-eligible.
- **Risks:**
  - CCFAP is a growing, payroll-tax-funded entitlement. The caseload is up about 63%, and DCF requested more for FY27. [S] FY27 DCF budget: https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Human%20Services/Budget/FY2027%20Budget/Witness%20Documents/AHS/W~Sandi%20Hoffman~Department%20for%20Children%20and%20Families%20FY2027%20Budget%20Presentation~2-5-2026.pdf
  - Future rate freezes or eligibility trims are possible. Don't lock recurring benefit costs entirely to CCFAP.
- **Administrative path:** use the CCFAP application at https://dcf.vermont.gov/benefits/ccfap. Addison County's Community Child Care Support Agency (CCSA) can assist; [?] I did not confirm which agency serves Addison.

## 2. Three-share / multi-share community coverage

- **Muskegon "Access Health" (MI, since 1999).** Its current product is the "C3 Community Health Plan" [V]: https://www.accesshealth.org/c3-community-health/
  - It is a 501(c)(3) "coverage" plan, not licensed insurance. It pays local providers directly.
  - Eligibility: uninsured, not eligible for any other coverage *including Medicaid*, household income **133-350% FPL**, and living or working in Greater Muskegon. Members pay 30%.
  - Benefits: $0 deductible, copays, an out-of-pocket max, plus primary, specialty, hospital, ER, imaging, lab, Rx and DME.
  - Historic split: 30% employer, 30% employee, about 40% community (originally DSH/Medicaid funds). EBRI 2005: https://www.ebri.org/content/the-muskegon-access-health-three-share-plan-a-case-history-3509
  - **Post-ACA status:** it survives at small scale. Crain's reported ~$72/mo member premiums after a state appropriation, and GuideStar lists about 306 members. It lost about $2M in Medicaid-linked funding in 2018, then got a ~$1.2M state appropriation to reopen enrollment.
  - In July 2025 it asked the Michigan House for $1.3M to sustain and replicate the model [S]: https://www.crainsgrandrapids.com/news/health-care/state-funding-will-enable-muskegon-health-plan-to-enroll-new-members/ ; https://www.house.mi.gov/hfa/PDF/LEO/LEO_Subcmte_Testimony_AccessHealth_BuildingHealthandEconSecurity_7-29-25.pdf
- **Federal bill H.R. 8585, the Community Multi-Share Coverage Program Act of 2026** (Moolenaar/Huizenga, introduced Apr 29, 2026) [S]: https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119hr8585ih
  - It would fund 3-5 HHS grants for nonprofit multi-share programs covering small-business workers between the Medicaid limit and 400% FPL.
  - Authorization: $4.8M in FY26, $7.2M in FY27, $12M/yr in FY28-29.
  - It was referred to committee with no action seen. Earlier versions in 2019 and 2022 died.
- **Other legacy three-share programs:** Pueblo CO (hospital-funded third), Duval County FL (closed 2008), plus Minnesota and Illinois sites. Most shrank or closed once the ACA marketplace and Medicaid expansion arrived [S]: https://kffhealthnews.org/news/novel-health-plans-try-to-help-uninsured
- **MI Tri-Share Child Care** is a *child care* cost-share, not health. Employer, employee and state each pay 1/3 of child care.
  - It has 10 regional hubs covering 59 of 83 counties. Region 3's hub is "Child Care Back Office" (Mar 2026) [V]: https://www.michigan.gov/mileap/press-releases/2026/03/19/mi-tri-share-expands-with-region-3-facilitator-hub
  - Copies exist in NC, KY, IN, OH, WV, ND, MO and **CT**. CT's New London County pilot used $1.8M in federal relief [S]: https://ctmirror.org/2026/01/23/tri-share-pilot-new-london-ct/
  - A federal Tri-Share Child Care Pilot Act (H.R. 6312) is in committee.
- **Vermont / New England:**
  - **No three-share health plan exists or is proposed in Vermont** [S, no evidence found].
  - Nearest precedents: Vermont's **Catamount Health** with employer-assessment premium assistance (2007-2013, ended with the ACA), and **Maine's DirigoChoice** (2005-2013, employer + employee + state sliding subsidy; ~700 small firms at peak; ended for lack of funding) [S]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirigo_Health
- **Why it's hard in Vermont now:**
  - Vermont's merged individual/small-group market and the generous PTCs (through 2025) cover most of the same population.
  - A non-insurance "coverage plan" like C3 would probably draw Vermont DFR scrutiny as unlicensed insurance [?].
  - The realistic local variant is a **community-funded premium subsidy layered on marketplace plans**, i.e. a §3 fund.
- **Related Vermont tool, Medicaid HIPP (Health Insurance Premium Payment).** DVHA reimburses employer or COBRA premiums for Medicaid members when that is cost-effective, mostly for high-cost conditions [V]: https://dvha.vermont.gov/node/1171
  - It would only matter if Willowell ever offered a group plan. It could cover the premium for a Medicaid-enrolled staff member or child with high medical costs.

## 3. Community or multi-program early-educator benefit funds

**No US example found of a community foundation or United Way paying health premiums for early educators across many programs.** Most real examples are **state-funded**, sometimes with a foundation layered on top.

- **Michigan: Nurture Benefits + Kresge.** [V/S] https://bridgemi.com/children-families/michigan-program-aims-expand-benefits-child-care-workers/ ; https://kresge.org/news-views/faq-how-to-access-the-mileap-kresge-pilot-program-supporting-child-care-providers/ ; https://www.michigan.gov/mileap/press-releases/2026/02/27/mileap-kresge-foundation-partner-early-childhood-educators-neighborhood-based-pilot
  - **Structure:** the Small Business Association of Michigan (SBAM) pools child care employers into a "managed risk pool" with 3 BCBSM tiers plus dental, vision, life and 401(k).
  - **Subsidy:** the state pays "the majority" of the base plan.
  - **Eligibility:** licensed and license-exempt providers working 30+ hrs, with a 60-day wait for new hires.
  - **Funding:** a $4.4M, 2-year pilot from a $33M federal PDG B-5 renewal. Enrollment opened Nov 15, 2025.
  - **Kresge (a private foundation)** layers extra supports in Detroit ZIP 48221 (pilot announced Feb 27, 2026).
  - **Takeaway:** the money goes to the *risk pool / employer plan*, not to individuals.
  - [?] It may not translate to Vermont, where association health plans are effectively barred (see `employer_mechanisms.md` §6).
- **Connecticut: a 2027 ECE health insurance subsidy** (Vermont Public / CT Public, Jul 17, 2026) [V]: https://www.vermontpublic.org/2026-07-17/ct-early-childhood-educators-are-eligible-for-a-new-health-insurance-subsidy-in-2027
  - **Amount:** **$1,000-$1,200/yr per educator** ($1,200 at 175-250% FPL, $1,100 at 250-400%, $1,000 above 400%).
  - **Eligibility:** educators at licensed centers or homes on the CT registry who are not eligible for Medicaid or Covered CT and buy a QHP through Access Health CT. It works with individual, small-group **or ICHRA** coverage.
  - **Funding:** **$10M from CT's Early Childhood Education Endowment**, a state fund holding $300M plus a $411M surplus deposit.
  - **This is the best New England model to copy in Vermont advocacy.**
- **D.C. HealthCare4ChildCare / Pay Equity Fund:** free DC Health Link premiums for ECE staff, via SHOP or individual plans. It is publicly funded by a dedicated tax [S]: https://www.newamerica.org/insights/dc-compensation-equity/
- **California CCPU Health Care Fund:** a union-bargained trust funded with **$100M/yr from the state**. It is open to union and non-union subsidized home providers. Its optional reimbursement program pays premiums, copays and deductibles via a debit card, up to plan-based caps [V]: https://www.ccpuhealth.org/about-the-program/
- **Washington State:** a free Cascade Care silver plan for child care workers under 300% FPL ran 2022-2023 and was **ended Dec 31, 2023** [S]: https://dcyf.wa.gov/news/no-cost-and-low-cost-health-care-premiums-reach-thousands
- **Minnesota:** a PDG-funded $55/mo benefits stipend through a vendor (Optima) covering dental, vision, telehealth and life. **It does not cover medical premiums.** It has a waitlist [S]: https://dcyf.mn.gov/news/new-program-offers-benefits-support-early-childhood-educators
- **Alaska (thread):** **no premium program found.** It runs Anchorage-funded wellness grants ($150-$1,350 for training) and a $2.3M operating stimulus [S]: https://www.akbizmag.com/industry/nonprofits/thread-awarded-2-3m-contract-for-anchorage-childcare-stimulus
- **Colorado:** no local premium fund found. Pitkin County's Kids First (a local sales tax) funded teacher stipends of $6K full-time / $4K part-time through 2025, which is pay rather than health coverage [S]: https://aspen.gov/DocumentCenter/View/14975/Kids-First-2024-Annual-Report-and-Provider-Survey
- **Maine:** **LD 1115 (2025)** would let MaineAEYC members buy into the **State employee group health plan**. It passed both chambers in 2024 but died on Veto Day; [?] its 2025 outcome is unconfirmed [S]: https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/getTestimonyDoc.asp?id=191921
  - **This is an interesting Vermont advocacy idea: a VTAEYC or ECE buy-in to the State/VEHI plan.**
- **How a Vermont community fund could be structured** [? needs tax counsel]:
  - **Host:** a public charity such as the Vermont Community Foundation or United Way of Addison County.
  - **Recipients:** a fund serving a **charitable class** (e.g., "low/moderate-income early educators working in regulated Addison County programs"), paying **needs-based grants to individuals** or paying insurers directly.
  - **Eligibility criteria** should be objective, set by a committee, and **not controlled by any one employer**.
  - **Pitfalls:**
    - (a) If Willowell itself funds grants for its own staff, the fund looks like an employer payment plan (ACA market-reform penalties) or taxable wages.
    - (b) Grants tied to employment are likely taxable income to the recipient. Needs-based charitable gifts can be excluded, but this is murky.
    - (c) Premium payments reduce the PTC only if made through an HRA, but cash grants may push up MAGI if treated as wages.
  - **Simplest legally clean variant:** a CT-style flat subsidy run by the **state** (exempt under the general welfare exclusion) or an independent multi-employer charity.

## 4. Direct primary care (DPC) near Addison/Chittenden

  - **Capacity:** the practice website says "almost full ... call to be placed on our waitlist" (802-453-4696): https://www.fiddleheadmd.com/
  - It is featured by Seven Days (Apr 1, 2026), along with Blue Spruce (Newport/St. Johnsbury/**Williston**, ~$100-$175/mo adult), Integrative Osteopathic Family Medicine (S. Burlington), Red Clover Pediatrics (N. Bennington) and LoPresti (Manchester): https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/healthcare/some-vermont-doctors-embrace-the-new-direct-primary-care-model/
- **Champlain Medical DPC (S. Burlington)** has **employer DPC partnerships** with ECI and Marshall Tire Group and an "Employer Portal." Prices are not published (802-448-9370) [V]: https://champlainmedical.com/direct-primary-care
- **Mint Health (Waterbury)** charges $20-$90/mo and still bills insurance [S]: https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-04-21/primary-care-providers-concierge-membership-fees
- **Blue Spruce (Williston)** lists ~$100 (0-26), $150 (27-59) and $175 (60+) on its site [S]: https://bluespruce.care/
- **Federal tax treatment (OBBBA, 2026+):** DPC no longer disqualifies HSA contributions, and DPC fees up to **$150/mo single / $300 family** are HSA-payable. Bronze and catastrophic marketplace plans are now HSA-eligible. See `employer_mechanisms.md` §5.
- **Vermont law:** **no Vermont DPC "not insurance" statute found** [?]. That is unlike NH RSA 329:1-e, NC and TX. S.197 (Act 173 of 2026, primary care reform) does not address DPC [S].
  - An employer-paid DPC membership may itself be an ERISA group health plan. QSEHRA/ICHRA reimbursement of DPC fees is a cleaner route. Needs counsel.
- **Cost example:** Willowell paying Fiddlehead memberships for 10 staff aged 26-55 would be about **$750-$1,000/mo, or $9K-$12K/yr**. That is a meaningful "health security" floor (unlimited primary care, many labs, chronic care) but **not** catastrophic coverage. Pair it with marketplace bronze + HSA.

## 5. Shared-employer / back-office / PEO models

- **Vermont PEO legality** [V]: 21 V.S.A. ch. 12 (Employee Leasing), https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/fullchapter/21/012
  - PEOs must be **licensed by the Commissioner of Labor**: $100 application + $1,000 license fee, $100K net worth, annual renewal.
  - §1039(b): health benefits may be provided **only** through (1) an insured policy under **8 V.S.A. ch. 107** from a DFR-authorized insurer, or (2) an ERISA-qualified **single-employer** plan.
  - Benefits must be comparable within each client company.
  - **PEOs are legal**, but whether a PEO can put a Vermont small employer's workers into an out-of-state **large-group** master policy, avoiding Vermont's merged, community-rated small-group market under 33 V.S.A. §1811, is **not settled by any DFR guidance I could find** [?].
  - Anecdotal: some Vermont clients expect to be pushed back to the exchange [S]: https://www.staffmarket.com/peos-by-state/Vermont/clients
  - **Action:** ask 2-3 PEOs (Justworks, TriNet, Insperity, Rippling PEO) in writing what medical carrier and plan type they use for Vermont worksite employees, and email DFR Insurance Division for its position.
  - DFR's TPA page lists PEOs among entities treated as "insurers" for TPA-licensing purposes: https://dfr.vermont.gov/third-party-administrator
- **Shared services alliances** (Opportunities Exchange, Early Learning Ventures CO, Sound Child Care Solutions WA, NJ's 16 alliances, Austin) [S]:
  - They centralize payroll, billing, purchasing and HR.
  - "Affording staff benefits like health insurance and retirement" is a stated goal, but **I found no alliance that actually runs a pooled employer health plan.**
  - OppEx recommends using the ACA marketplace (HDHP + HSA) and pooled retirement instead.
  - Sources: https://www.oppex.org/issue-briefs ; https://www.oppex.org/shared-services/business-leadership-initiatives-1
- **The working model is Michigan Nurture Benefits** (§3): a state-subsidized SBAM risk pool. It depends on Michigan allowing association-style pooling.
- **The "single employer across sites" route is real but heavy.** Mergers or affiliations under one EIN (e.g., Sound Child Care Solutions' consortium, which grew to ~10 sites) give access to one small-group or large-group plan. Head Start grantees (e.g., CVOEO Champlain Valley Head Start) offer benefits this way [S].
  - **Vermont option [?]:** an affiliation or management agreement with a larger Addison nonprofit that already has a group plan (e.g., Addison County Parent/Child Center, a Head Start delegate, or a school district for UPK). It would need a licensed leasing arrangement or a true merger.
- **Vermont shared-services infrastructure exists, but not for benefits.** First Children's Finance VT runs sharedservicesvt.org and free state-funded business coaching. It has supported 59% of regulated programs since 2023 [S]: https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Human%20Services/Early%20Childhood/Pre-K/W~Erin%20Roche~Testimony%20on%20behalf%20of%20First%20Children's%20Finance~3-18-2026.pdf
  - FCF is the natural convener of an Addison benefits-pool feasibility study.

## 6. Vermont 2027 legislative landscape and coalitions

- **Pre-filed 2027 bills on ECE health benefits: none found** as of Oct 9, 2026 [S]. Pre-filing happens Nov-Dec, and the session opens Jan 2027.
  - The last concrete proposal was Let's Grow Kids' 2023 S.56 testimony: categorical eligibility for a no- or low-cost silver VHC plan, modeled on Washington. It was not enacted.
- **S.206 (ECE individual licensure via OPR)** passed the Senate in 2026 and is **held in House Human Services for 2027**. Licensure is often framed as the precondition for compensation and benefits policy [S]: https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/celebrating-progress-on-2026-policy-recommendations/
- **Building Bright Futures (BBF)** is "formulating Policy Recommendations for the 2027 legislative session," usually finalized in November.
  - The 2026 recommendations focused on sustaining Act 76, background checks and pre-K. **No explicit health-benefits item.**
  - The Strategic Plan *does* call to "increase compensation and benefits" and "ensure access to health insurance." https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/data-page/policy-recommendations/
- **Coalitions Willowell could join:**
  - **VTAEYC + Let's Grow Kids Action Network (LGKAN, a 501(c)(4) successor; LGK itself closed end-2025).** Their joint 2026 agenda covered protecting Act 76 funding, the ECE workforce, background checks and family access: https://vermontbiz.com/node/241237 ; https://vtdigger.org/2025/11/19/after-a-decade-vermonts-child-care-juggernaut-passes-the-torch
    - The VTAEYC annual conference is **Oct 21-23, 2026, in Killington**, a good venue to raise ECE health coverage.
  - **Vermont Early Childhood Advocacy Alliance (VECAA)**, a program of Vermont Community Loan Fund. Its 21-member steering committee sets the annual agenda. **2027 agenda applications closed Sept 17**, but organizations can still join as members: https://vecaa.org/legislative-agenda/ ; https://vecaa.org/get-involved/join/
  - **BBF Addison Regional Council** for local voice and data: https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/
  - **Vermont Steering Committee for Comprehensive Primary Health Care** for universal primary care. S.197 / Act 173 of 2026 builds toward it [S]: https://sites.google.com/view/end-of-session-report/health-care
- **Pitch to bring:**
  - (a) A CT-style flat ECE premium subsidy, which could be funded from the Child Care Special Fund (the Act 76 payroll tax).
  - (b) A Maine LD 1115-style ECE buy-in to the State employee plan.
  - (c) Restoring a VHC ECE categorical subsidy.
  - (d) Letting the CCFAP rate include an explicit benefits component, since the First Children's Finance cost-of-care model already has "aspirational" wage scenarios.

## 7. Vermont Saves vs 403(b) / SIMPLE IRA (and MAGI)

- **Vermont Saves** [V]: https://www.vermonttreasurer.gov/economic-empowerment-division/vermont-saves ; https://vtsaves.vermont.gov/savers/program-details
  - **Who must register:** employers with **2 or more W-2 employees** and no qualified plan. Per the official page, this was expanded from 5+ under the Feb 2026 rules.
  - **Penalties:** $20 per covered employee through Sept 30, 2026, then **$75 per employee per year from Oct 1, 2026**. **Willowell must register or certify an exemption now** unless it already has a qualified plan.
  - **Default account: an individual Roth IRA**, with a Traditional IRA option.
  - **Contributions:** default 5% of pay, auto-escalating 1%/yr to 8%. A cap increase to 10% was proposed in 2025 [S].
  - **Cost:** free for employers. Savers pay about **0.32%/yr** (asset-based) **plus a $26/yr account fee** per the 2024 auto-enroll notice; the statute caps the dollar fee at $30 [S].
  - **Scale:** about 5,830 savers and 1,375 employers by spring 2026.
- **Effect on MAGI:**
  - **Roth contributions do not reduce AGI or MAGI**, so they do nothing for PTC, Medicaid or Dr. Dynasaur eligibility.
  - A **Traditional IRA** contribution (through Vermont Saves' Traditional option, or on one's own) is an **above-the-line deduction**. Vermont Saves isn't an employer plan, so the "active participant" phase-out shouldn't apply [?]. It **lowers MAGI** for PTC and for Medicaid (Schedule 1 deductions count) [?].
  - Pre-tax **403(b) or SIMPLE IRA** deferrals lower Box 1 wages directly and therefore MAGI, and they are handled automatically through payroll.
- **2026 limits** [V]: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500
  - IRA: $7,500 (+$1,100 catch-up).
  - 403(b): $24,500 (+$8,000 catch-up; $11,250 at ages 60-63).
  - SIMPLE: $17,000, or **$18,100 for employers with 25 or fewer employees**: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-sponsor/simple-ira-plan
  - A SIMPLE requires a 3% match or 2% nonelective contribution.
  - A 501(c)(3) can sponsor a 403(b) with **no employer contribution** and low cost; non-ERISA status needs care.
- **Saver's Match starts with 2027 contributions** [S]: https://www.psca.org/news/psca-news/2026/8/savers-match-guidance-coming-ahead-of-2027-launch/ ; https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-begin-implementing-executive-order-14403-by-announcing-intent-to-issue-proposed-regulations-on-savers-match-which-will-benefit-millions-of-low-and-moderate-income-taxpayers
  - It pays 50% of the first $2,000 per person (max **$1,000**), deposited in 2028.
  - It is **AGI-tested**, so pre-tax contributions help staff qualify.
  - IRA contributions qualify, but [?] whether the Vermont Saves IRA will be an eligible receiving vehicle (TrumpIRA.gov list) is unknown.
- **Recommendation:**
  - For staff near a cliff (Medicaid 138%, Dr. Dynasaur 312% for kids, PTC bands, CCFAP bands, which use **gross** income [?]), steer contributions **pre-tax**. Use the Traditional option in Vermont Saves (free to Willowell), or a 403(b) if Willowell wants payroll-level pre-tax treatment and an optional match.
  - **CCFAP uses gross monthly income**, so pre-tax retirement deferrals may **not** lower CCFAP family share [? verify with CDD].
