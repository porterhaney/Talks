# Vermont State Programs, Funding Streams & Policy Initiatives for Early Educator Health Coverage / Benefits (as of Oct 8, 2026)

Context: Willowell Foundation / Wren's Nest Forest Preschool (licensed center-based program, 5-STARS, Bristol VT, accepts CCFAP, prequalified Act 166 UPK partner), ~10 staff at 75-100% FTE, no health benefits. Question: is there a state option for non-public-school educators?

**Bottom line up front:** As of October 2026 Vermont has **no** state health plan, buy-in, premium subsidy, or Medicaid categorical-eligibility pathway specifically for child care / early childhood educators. Proposals exist (Let's Grow Kids 2023) but were never enacted, and the 2026 session passed nothing on educator health benefits. The realistic state money is **indirect**: higher Act 76 CCFAP rates, the Act 166 UPK tuition, small annual QCIP payments, and individual-level workforce supports (loan repayment, scholarships, bonuses). These are flexible revenue a program *could* put toward benefits, but none is earmarked for benefits. DC (HealthCare4ChildCare) is the strongest live precedent; Washington's version expired at the end of 2023.

## 1. Act 76 (2023): CCFAP rates, payroll tax, rate setting, and whether rates build in compensation/benefits; follow-on legislation 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Act 76 raised CCFAP rates sharply (licensed centers +35%), decoupled them from STARS, and funds them with a new payroll tax (the Child Care Contribution, about $99.6M collected through Nov 2025). But the rates are **not** formally built on a compensation/benefits standard. A First Children's Finance cost-of-care model (Jan 2025, updated early 2026) models current versus "aspirational" wages to inform future rate setting. No Act 76 provision or follow-on bill in 2024-2026 creates a health benefit, wage scale, or benefits stipend for early educators.

### Cited Findings
**Rates and structure**
- CCFAP capped rates moved to new, higher base rates that no longer depend on a program's STARS level (Act 76 Secs. 8a and 9). The state stopped using provider rate agreements and automatically pays the state rate for families receiving CCFAP. Providers can now set tuition at their discretion and no longer have to charge CCFAP and private-pay families the same rate to get the maximum CCFAP payment. — [DCF Act 76 FAQs](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/Act76/ACT-76-FAQs.pdf); [CCFAP Rate Increase Per Act 76](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/Act76/CCFAP-Rate-Increase-Per-Act-76.pdf)
- Licensed center rates rose 35% above July 2023 levels starting December 2023. Example from the schedule: a full-time infant rate at a licensed center was $471/week after the July 2023 change. — [CCFAP Rate Increase Per Act 76](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/Act76/CCFAP-Rate-Increase-Per-Act-76.pdf); see also [Act 76 CCFAP Rates Jan 2024 (AOE)](https://education.vermont.gov/sites/aoe/files/documents/Act-76-CCFAP-Rates-Jan-2024.pdf)
- Infant and toddler CCFAP rates were increased again effective July 13, 2025. — [CDD blog: CCFAP infant and toddler rate changes](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd-blog/ccfap-infant-and-toddler-rate-changes)
- Family eligibility now reaches 575% FPL. — [DCF Act 76 FAQs](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/Act76/ACT-76-FAQs.pdf)
- Families pay a weekly family share of $0 to $425 depending on size and income; the state pays the provider the CCFAP state rate minus that share. — [DCF tuition/rates search results; CDD tuition notice](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Policies%20Procedures%20Guidance/CDD-Notice-Tuition-Rates-2025-26.pdf) (summarized via search, not opened directly)
- **Tuition increase cap:** regulated programs may not raise annual tuition more than **5.4%** for July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 (formula: 1.5 x the most recent Employment Cost Index change for Educational Services). The prior year's cap was 5.85%. — [CDD blog: child care tuition increase limit](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd-blog/child-care-tuition-increase-limit-0); [Shelburne News](https://www.vtcng.com/shelburnenews/news/local_news/child-care-tuition-cap-law-checks-rate-hike/article_b5f4c161-e56a-4487-98d5-7ffd547411fd.html)
- Providers in BBF listening sessions said they understand the tuition cap protects families, but they worry about its effect on future wage growth. Several program leaders said they are close to the point where they cannot raise wages any further. — [BBF Report on Act 76 Monitoring, Jan 20, 2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)

**Payroll tax (Child Care Contribution, CCC)**
- CCC revenue totaled about **$99.6M through Nov 30, 2025**: FY2025 $59.4M; FY2026 Q1-Q2 (partial) $40.2M. State economists estimate ongoing collections will run about **$11M per year lower** than originally projected because the tax base was smaller than assumed. — [BBF Act 76 Monitoring Report 2026, pp. 14-16](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- About 99% of the roughly **$172M** budgeted for CCFAP in SFY2026 was projected to be spent, leaving about $1.8M (1%). BBF says any discussion of repurposing a surplus is premature. — [BBF 2026 report, p. 16](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- Nearly 12,000 children in almost 10,000 families are determined eligible for CCFAP, about 50% more families than before Act 76. Implementation was staged from July 2023 to January 2026. — [BBF 2026 report, p. 3](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- A third-party site states the payroll tax rate as 0.22% for employees and gives a 50% employer figure; this was NOT verified against an official source. — [trisharechildcare.com (unverified)](https://trisharechildcare.com/states/vermont)

**Rate setting and compensation**
- CDD commissioned a Cost of Care Model from First Children's Finance with Act 76 administrative funds. The initial version came out January 2025, with an update due early 2026. It uses two wage scenarios, current and aspirational; for example, a center lead teacher at **$47,237 versus $76,372**. Its findings: the true cost of high-quality care exceeds tuition, infant care is consistently underfunded, and **moving preschool enrollment to K-12 settings creates system vulnerability**. The models "provide critical context for CCFAP rate setting, workforce investments." — [BBF 2026 report, pp. 13-14, 30](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- Under Act 76 Sec. 23, the Vermont Dept. of Labor submitted a Child Care Provider Wage Memo on Dec 31, 2025: the 2024 average Vermont child care worker wage was **$18.93/hr** (median about $18/hr), the second-highest median in the region. Only CA, MA, WA, and DC have higher averages. — [BBF 2026 report, p. 14](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- Providers report that Act 76 revenue made "wage increases, benefit improvements" possible. A Child Trends 2025 study quotes a provider: "[Changes to CCFAP] helped allow us to offer health and dental insurance and allowed us to increase staff wages." — [BBF 2026 report, pp. 3, 31](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)

**Follow-on legislation, 2024-2026**
- **S.206 (2026), the Early Childhood Education Profession Bill:** it would create individual licensure through the Office of Professional Regulation for educators in non-public-school regulated programs (more than 8,000 educators). The fiscal note shows $262,000 from the General Fund in FY2027 for OPR. It passed the Senate on 4/22/2026, but House Human Services **held it for 2027**, so it did not pass. The bill concerns licensure, not health benefits. — [BBF: What the 2026 Session Means](https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/what-the-2026-session-means-for-early-childhood-in-vermont/); [Bill status S.206](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/S.206); [Fiscal note](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Health%20and%20Welfare/Bills/S.206/Witness%20Testimony/S.206~Ted%20Barnett~Fiscal%20Note~2-18-2026.pdf); [VTAEYC testimony](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Health%20and%20Welfare/Bills/S.206/Witness%20Testimony/S.206~Sharron%20Harrington~Vermont%20Association%20for%20the%20Education%20of%20Young%20Children~1-28-2026.pdf)
- A VTDigger opinion piece (Apr 7, 2026) argued S.206 would worsen staffing by adding licensing burden. — [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2026/04/07/vermont-wants-to-fix-child-care-this-bill-will-make-it-worse/)
- BBF's 2026 session recap lists no CCFAP amendment, compensation standard, wage scale, benefits stipend, or health-benefit study for early educators. — [BBF: What the 2026 Session Means](https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/what-the-2026-session-means-for-early-childhood-in-vermont/)

### Inferences
- Wren's Nest benefits from Act 76 because of higher CCFAP rates for subsidized children and because CCFAP payments no longer depend on STARS. However, nothing in the CCFAP rate is earmarked for benefits. Any health benefit must come out of general operating margin, which the 5.4% tuition cap limits on the private-pay side.
- The cost-of-care model and its "aspirational wage" scenario could become the vehicle for future benefit-inclusive rate setting. It is worth asking CDD and First Children's Finance whether the 2026 update includes a benefits line, such as an employer health contribution.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the July 2026 CCFAP state rate schedule (the full-time preschool weekly rate for licensed centers). Check DCF's "CCFAP State Rates" PDF.
- The exact CCC payroll tax rate and employer/employee split were not verified from an official source in this research. My recollection from Act 76 is 0.44% of wages, with the employer paying at least 75%, but this is unverified; check Vermont Dept. of Taxes.
- I could not open the First Children's Finance cost-model report to see whether it models a health-benefit cost line.
- I could not find the contents of the Act 76-required "Joint Report on Elevating the Status of Early Childhood Education" (AOE and AHS, late 2025). The AOE press release describes it only as concerning agency governance and parity. — [AOE press release](https://education.vermont.gov/press-release/vermont-agency-education-delivers-progress-update-and-key-recommendations-ahead-2026)

## 2. Is there any Vermont state health plan, buy-in, or subsidy for child care / early childhood workers? Proposals, studies, pilots

### Takeaway
No. Vermont has no occupational health pathway for early educators. The main proposal was Let's Grow Kids' 2023 recommendation that early educators be **categorically eligible for a no- or low-cost silver plan through Vermont Health Connect**, modeled on Washington. It was not enacted in Act 76, and no later bill carried it. About **67% of Vermont early educators reportedly rely on Medicaid**, compared with 20% of Vermont adults aged 19-64. BBF's 2026 monitoring report names limited access to health insurance as a top threat to workforce sustainability.

### Cited Findings
- Let's Grow Kids (Feb 2023, S.56 testimony) recommended that "early childhood educators should be categorically eligible for a no- or low-cost silver-level health insurance plan through Vermont Health Connect beginning in 2024," and cited Washington's model statutory language. The same package proposed categorical CCFAP eligibility, regardless of income, for child care staff. — [LGK suggested changes to S.56, 2/24/2023](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/Senate%20Health%20and%20Welfare/Bills/S.56/Witness%20Documents/S.56~Sarah%20Kenney~Suggested%20Changes%20from%20Let's%20Grow%20Kids~2-24-2023.pdf)
- Act 76 as passed (H.217) did not include a health insurance provision for educators. I found no subsequent enacted provision. — [H.217 as passed](https://ljfo.vermont.gov/assets/Publications/2023-2024-As-Passed-the-General-Assembly/d048419212/GENERAL-370513-v2-2023_H_217_Child_care_and_unemployment-PASSED-GA.pdf); [BBF 2026 session recap](https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/what-the-2026-session-means-for-early-childhood-in-vermont/)
- Vermont Health Connect's eligibility rules have no occupational or categorical exception for early educators; financial help depends on income only. — [Vermont Health Connect eligibility tables](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/compare-plans/eligibility-tables)
- BBF 2026: "many educators said that benefits, especially health insurance, remain out of reach… approximately **67% of Vermont's early childhood educators rely on Medicaid** for health coverage compared to 20% of Vermont adults 19-64." BBF notes the added risk created by federal Medicaid policy uncertainty. Workforce sustainability "remains fragile, especially given burnout, long onboarding timelines, and limited access to benefits such as health insurance." — [BBF Act 76 Monitoring Report 2026, pp. 4, 25](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- Let's Grow Kids' website says the organization **sunsets in 2025**, which removes the main advocacy champion for the health-insurance proposal. — [LGK, via search summary](https://letsgrowkids.org/child-care-investing-in-vermont-future-progress-act-76) (sunset noted in search result; verify)
- An LGK-hosted blog post (2024) quotes a center director saying their 2024 goal was to "finally offer our amazing staff health insurance benefits" because of new Act 76 funding. — [LGK: Smires & Brink](https://letsgrowkids.org/newsroom/smires-and-brink-our-child-care-new-years-resolution-for-2024)

### Inferences
- For Willowell staff today, the "state option" is the generic one: Vermont Health Connect with federal and state premium assistance based on income, or Medicaid/Dr. Dynasaur for lower-income staff. Nothing is specific to child care employment. (Another researcher presumably covers Vermont Health Connect mechanics and ICHRA/QSEHRA.)
- The 67% Medicaid statistic suggests many Willowell-like staff are already covered through Medicaid. An employer plan could even disrupt that coverage, so a staff census on current coverage is a sensible first step.

### Gaps
- The original source of the 67% figure is not identified in the BBF report beyond "national research." It may be a CSCCE or Urban Institute estimate; treat it as approximate.
- I found no Vermont-specific compensation and benefits study for early educators dated 2024-2026, beyond the VDOL wage memo and the FCF cost model.

## 3. Universal pre-K (Act 166) funding for private prequalified programs and Act 73 (2025) changes

### Takeaway
The statewide UPK tuition rate paid to prequalified private partners is **$4,106 per child for 2026-27** (10 hrs/week, 35 weeks), up from $3,982 in 2025-26 and $3,884 in 2024-25. It is indexed annually by the NEEP price index. Act 73 (2025) shifted pre-K into the new education funding formula (pre-K weight of -0.54) and ordered a JFO review. The 2026 education transformation bill (**H.955**) affirmed the mixed-delivery system but **deferred key pre-K funding decisions to 2027**, with a cost-of-care analysis and funding recommendations due before the 2027 session.

### Cited Findings
- 2026/27 statewide UPK tuition rate is **$4,106**; 2025/26 was $3,982; 2024/25 was $3,884. — [AOE UPK Funding](https://education.vermont.gov/student-support/early-education/universal-prekindergarten-act-166/funding); [JFO Review of Early Care & Learning System, Feb 2026](https://ljfo.vermont.gov/assets/Subjects/Early-Care-Education/Final-JFO-2025_Early_Care_and_Learning_Report.pdf)
- Districts pay the statewide rate to prequalified programs, private or public, for 10 hrs/week over 35 weeks. Additional hours are billed by the program to families or CCFAP. — [AOE UPK Funding](https://education.vermont.gov/student-support/early-education/universal-prekindergarten-act-166/funding); [AOE UPK Handbook](https://education.vermont.gov/sites/aoe/files/edu-aoe-universal-prekindergarten-program-handbook.pdf)
- Under Act 73, pre-K students carry a weight of -0.54 in the funding formula. JFO notes a district would receive a base amount of $6,915 (FY2025 dollars) per enrolled UPK student under those **contingent** provisions. Act 73 also includes intent language to set an appropriate pre-K weight and ensure costs fall on the right funding source. — [JFO report per Act 73](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Human%20Services/Early%20Childhood/Pre-K/W~Emily%20Byrne~Early%20Care%20and%20Learning%20System%20in%20Vermont;%20JFO%20report%20per%20Act%2073~2-24-2026.pdf); [JFO Feb 2026 review](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Ways%20and%20Means/PreK/W~Emily%20Byrne~Review%20of%20Vermont%E2%80%99s%20Early%20Care%20and%20Learning%20System%20Report~2-12-2026.pdf)
- AOE presented a UPK proposal to House Human Services on Mar 19, 2026 (draft 26-0802). — [AOE UPK presentation](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Human%20Services/Bills/26-0802/Witness%20Documents/W~Zoie%20Saunders~Universal%20Prekindergarten%20Presentation~3-19-2026.pdf)
- **H.955 (2026)** includes pre-K: it affirms mixed delivery, defers key pre-K funding decisions to 2027, and makes BBF a joint pre-K monitor with AOE and DCF (initial report by Dec 1, 2026; annual reports from Jan 2027). A cost-of-care analysis and funding-structure recommendations are due before the 2027 session. BBF did not state whether the Governor signed it. — [BBF: What the 2026 Session Means](https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/what-the-2026-session-means-for-early-childhood-in-vermont/)
- **S.214** (Essex County pre-K access) passed the Legislature. — [BBF 2026 recap](https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/what-the-2026-session-means-for-early-childhood-in-vermont/)
- Act 76 includes legislative intent to move toward full-day pre-K for 4-year-olds. The FCF cost model warns that moving preschool enrollment into K-12 settings "creates system vulnerability" for community programs. — [BBF 2026 report, pp. 12, 30](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- At $4,106 per UPK child, 20 UPK-eligible children would bring in roughly $82k a year. That is a meaningful but fixed revenue line, and it is at political risk: if 2027 decisions favor school-based or full-day pre-K, private partners like Wren's Nest could lose 4-year-olds. Willowell should watch the H.955-mandated BBF/AOE/DCF reports (Dec 2026) and the 2027 session.
- A higher UPK rate based on a cost-of-care model that includes benefits is a plausible advocacy ask for 2027.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether H.955 was signed, or its act number.
- I did not find the exact Act 73 pre-K weight implementation timeline after 2026.

## 4. Workforce programs (individual-level supports) and business supports; shared services

### Takeaway
Vermont's early educator workforce supports go to **individuals**: student loan repayment (SLRAP, up to $4,000/yr), T.E.A.C.H. scholarships, tuition and fee grants, apprenticeship, and professional recognition bonuses, all mostly administered by VTAEYC with CDD/federal CCDF funds. None pays for health insurance, but they raise total compensation at no cost to Willowell. I found **no Vermont shared-services alliance offering pooled health insurance**.

### Cited Findings
- **SLRAP:** up to **$4,000/yr**, paid to the individual, for full-time educators with an early-childhood degree (or a degree leading to a Level IIIB+ career ladder certificate) earned within the last 5 years. Requires a 12-month commitment to a regulated Vermont program and a salary cap. Administered by VTAEYC. — [CDD staff funding page](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/funding/staff); [VTAEYC SLRAP](https://www.vtaeyc.org/slrap/); [CDD blog](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd-blog/cdd-and-vtaeyc-offer-student-loan-repayment-assistance)
- Act 76 raised the SLRAP annual salary cap from **$50,000 to $60,000** and **eliminated the program's scheduled repeal**. CDD continues to fund it with federal child care workforce development funds. — [BBF 2026 report, pp. 13-14](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf). Note: the Act 45 report (Oct 2025) says the newer Act 45 scholarship and loan programs were scheduled for repeal July 1, 2026, so their current status is unclear. — [Act 45 Report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Economic%20Development/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Department%20for%20Children%20and%20Families~Act%2045%20Report,%20Scholarships%20and%20Student%20Loan%20Repayment~10-6-2025.pdf)
- A CDD post mentions a one-time **supplemental $4,000** for SLRAP applicants who apply by July 15 (year unclear). — [CDD blog: July 15 deadlines](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd-blog/july-15-deadlines-recognition-bonuses-student-loan-assistance-and-teach)
- **T.E.A.C.H. Early Childhood Vermont** scholarships are for educators working at least 30 hrs/week in regulated programs. FY25 participation was 135, and average participant wage rose from $17.62 (FY23) to $22.68 (FY25). Other supports: the **ECE Apprenticeship** (with VT Dept. of Labor), College Tuition Assistance Grants, CDA and GED fee grants, and the Education Gap Grant. — [CDD staff funding page](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/funding/staff); [VTAEYC scholarships](https://www.vtaeyc.org/scholarships-grants-bonuses/); [BBF 2026 data dashboard p. 9](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- **Early Childhood Professional Recognition Bonuses** go to staff at regulated programs who earn certain certificates, credentials, or licensure; amounts are set by a CDD bonus chart. Career ladder certificates issued rose from 365 (FY24) to 456 (FY25). — [CDD staff funding page](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/funding/staff); [Expanded bonus post](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd-blog/expanded-professional-recognition-bonus-opportunities-and-student-loan-repayment); [BBF 2026 report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- VTAEYC Annual Conference: Oct 21-23, 2026, Killington. — [CDD staff funding page](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/funding/staff)
- **Shared services:** Shared Services VT (launched 2014 by Vermont Birth to Three and CCA for Social Good) is a subscription resource and discount platform. I found no evidence that it pools health insurance. — [Chester Telegraph 2014](https://www.chestertelegraph.org/2014/09/08/new-website-to-link-vermont-childcare-providers-with-resources/); [CCA Global](https://www.ccaglobalpartners.com/News/2014/September/Early-Childhood-Education-Providers-in-VT-Receive-Resource-Platform-Developed-by-CCA-For-Social-Good)
- First Children's Finance works with CDD on cost modeling and business supports. — [BBF 2026 report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Willowell could add these to its "total compensation" story right away. Steering eligible staff to SLRAP, T.E.A.C.H., and recognition bonuses costs nothing, and each award could be several thousand dollars per person.
- A pooled-insurance shared-services model would be new in Vermont; Willowell could raise it with VTAEYC or First Children's Finance.

### Gaps
- Current (FY27) recognition bonus amounts, and whether the Act 45 programs were extended past July 1, 2026, were not confirmed.
- I found no Vermont "retention bonus" program for 2026 beyond the recognition bonuses.

## 5. Can STARS-rated programs get quality or capacity grants usable for benefits?

### Takeaway
Yes, but the money is small. The annual **Quality and Capacity Incentive Payment (QCIP)**, created by Act 76, pays a one-time per-child amount ($80/child for centers with 1-59 children) plus bonuses, including **+40% for 5-STARS**. Allowable uses are flexible ("strengthen quality, expand access, promote inclusion"), but CDD does not say whether wages or benefits qualify. The **2026 window closed Sept 14, 2026**. Statewide, QCIP distributed $2.7M to 802 programs in 2025, or about $3,400 per program on average.

### Cited Findings
- QCIP 2026: applications ran **Aug 14 to Sept 14, 2026**, with payments starting late September 2026, one application per license. Eligibility: an active license, open on the application date, planning to stay open through June 2027, and accepting (or able to accept) CCFAP and charging tuition, or a Head Start recipient. — [DCF QCIP page](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/QCIP)
- Base payment per child: FCCH $100; **licensed center (1-59 children) $80**; center (60+) $75; afterschool $55. Bonuses as a percentage of base: STARS 1:5%, 2:10%, 3:20%, 4:30%, **5:40%**; specialized care 30%; infants/toddlers 10-20%; open at least 45 hrs/week 10%; year-round 10%. The page is internally inconsistent about one-time versus per-month payment. Recipients must complete a use-of-funds survey and return unspent funds if they close. — [DCF QCIP page](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/QCIP)
- 2025: $2.7M went to 802 programs; the funds are "flexible funding that programs can use to strengthen quality, expand infant and toddler slots, support extended-hour care, and increase inclusion." — [BBF 2026 report, p. 12](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
- STARS bonuses paid in FY25 numbered 359 (106 at 5 STARS). — [BBF 2026 report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Illustrative calculation: a 5-STAR center with about 25 children gets $80 x 1.4 = $112/child, plus possible 10% bonuses, so roughly **$2,800 to $3,400 a year**. That is far short of funding health insurance for 10 staff, which typically costs tens of thousands of dollars or more. If Wren's Nest did not apply by Sept 14, 2026, check with CDD (AHS.DCFCDDQCIPAnnual@vermont.gov).

## 6. Other states' precedents Vermont advocates point to

### Takeaway
Vermont advocates (LGK, 2023) explicitly cited **Washington**. That program, $0 Cascade Care Silver premiums for licensed child care workers under 300% FPL, was temporary and **ended Dec 31, 2023**. The most robust live model is **DC's HealthCare4ChildCare**, funded by the Pay Equity Fund ($70M/yr). Other relevant models are wage and benefit pass-throughs (Massachusetts C3, Minnesota Great Start) and categorical child care subsidy eligibility for child care workers (Kentucky, Arkansas, Iowa, and a Rhode Island pilot). None of these makes child care workers categorically eligible for **Medicaid**. The "regardless of income" policies in KY, RI, and elsewhere concern **child care subsidies** for workers' own children, not health coverage.

### Cited Findings
**Washington**
- Under the 2021 Fair Start for Kids Act ($1.1B), the state paid full premiums for Cascade Care Silver plans for employees of licensed child care facilities under 300% FPL (enrollees still owed copays and deductibles). — [Economic Opportunity Institute](https://opportunityinstitute.org/blog/post/coming-this-fall-brand-new-program-will-help-an-estimated-40000-child-care-workers-afford-health-insurance/); [Zaentz Navigator](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/health-care-coverage-for-child-care-workers); [WA DCYF news](https://dcyf.wa.gov/news/no-cost-and-low-cost-health-care-premiums-reach-thousands)
- **It ended Dec 31, 2023.** Workers were pointed to the general Cascade Care Savings program (up to 250% FPL) instead. — [WA DCYF wind-down guidance](https://webdevpoc.dcyf.wa.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/WindDown-Guidance.pdf); [WA HBE Cascade Care Savings](https://www.wahbexchange.org/about-the-exchange/initiatives/cascade-care/cascade-care-savings/)

**District of Columbia**
- The Early Childhood Educator Pay Equity Fund (2022) funds **HealthCare4ChildCare**. If a licensed facility opts in, its employees who are DC residents get **free premiums for themselves and dependents** through DC Health Link; non-residents get reduced premiums. More than 2,000 DC residents in child care had coverage as of April 2025. The fund is $70M per year, and FY2026 was kept at $70M despite proposed cuts. — [Zaentz Navigator: HealthCare4ChildCare](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/healthcare4childcare/); [Under 3 DC](https://under3dc.org/our-agenda/pay-equity-fund/); [OSSE FAQ](https://osse.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/osse/publication/attachments/Pay%20Equity%20Fund%20Educator%20FAQ%20English.pdf); [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/d-c-experimented-with-giving-child-care-workers-big-raises-the-project-may-not-last/)

**Massachusetts C3**
- C3 operational grants were funded at $475M in FY25 and $115M + $360M in FY26. Allowable uses include personnel costs, **benefits**, stipends, and recruitment and retention supports. Centers must spend 50% on workforce in FY26. Workforce made up 70% of FY25 C3 spending. EEC guidance does not specifically name health insurance. — [Mass.gov C3](https://www.mass.gov/service-details/commonwealth-cares-for-children-child-care-stabilization-grants); [C3 Aug 2026 data brief](https://www.mass.gov/doc/commonwealth-cares-for-children-c3-august-2026-data-brief/download)

**Minnesota Great Start Compensation Support Payments**
- Licensed centers must use the payments for increased **compensation and benefits**, premium pay, or associated employer taxes. Family child care homes have more flexibility. Funds must be spent within 6 months, with documentation required. — [MN DCYF program page](https://dcyf.mn.gov/programs-directory/great-start-compensation-support-payment-program); [Minn. Stat. 142D.21](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/?id=142D.21); [MN DHS presentation 2024](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/health/20240214/Health_20240214_DHS-Update-On-Implementation-Of-Great-Start-Compensation-Payments-And-CCAP-Rate-Increases-Powerpoint.pdf)

**New Mexico**
- Universal no-cost child care began Nov 1, 2025. Providers get additional funding if they pay entry-level staff at least $16-$19/hr depending on quality level, which is a **wage** floor; I found no state health insurance benefit for workers. — [NM ECECD Universal Child Care](https://www.nmececd.org/universal/); [Universal Child Care 101 for Providers](https://www.nmececd.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Universal-Child-Care-101-for-Providers_English.pdf); [CNN Dec 2025](https://www.cnn.com/2025/12/13/business/universal-child-care-new-mexico)

**Categorical child care subsidy for child care workers (not health insurance)**
- **Kentucky (2022):** employees of licensed centers or certified homes working at least 20 hrs/week are eligible for the child care subsidy regardless of household income. The state said it would continue the program with federal and state funds after federal relief money expired. **Arkansas** (Jan 2024) has a similar policy. **Iowa** made its pilot permanent. **Rhode Island** ran a $4M CCDBG pilot from Aug 2023 that raised the income cap to 300% FPL for child care workers. — [New America](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/in-effort-to-entice-child-care-staff-more-states-follow-kentuckys-lead/); [Stateline 2024](https://stateline.org/2024/06/17/for-child-care-workers-state-aid-for-their-own-kids-care-is-life-changing)
- LGK proposed this same categorical CCFAP eligibility for Vermont in 2023; it was not enacted. — [LGK 2023 S.56 suggestions](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/Senate%20Health%20and%20Welfare/Bills/S.56/Witness%20Documents/S.56~Sarah%20Kenney~Suggested%20Changes%20from%20Let's%20Grow%20Kids~2-24-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- If Vermont advocates revive a health-coverage ask, the likely templates are (a) Washington-style **state-paid Vermont Health Connect premiums** for regulated-program staff, which would be administratively simple because Vermont already runs its own exchange, or (b) a DC-style employer opt-in. Either would need a funding source; the CCC payroll tax is fully committed to CCFAP (about 99% utilization) and is running about $11M per year below projections.
- The premise that "child care workers qualify for Medicaid regardless of income" in some state appears to be a **misconception**. KY and RI-style policies are about child care subsidies, not Medicaid.

### Gaps
- Current status of Rhode Island's pilot after 2024 and Kentucky's after 2025 was not confirmed.
- Enrollment and cost outcomes for Washington's program (2021-2023) were not retrieved.
- Massachusetts EEC guidance explicitly naming health premiums, and Minnesota FAQ 4.9, were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- SLRAP is funded with federal child care workforce development funds. — [BBF 2026 report, p. 14](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)
