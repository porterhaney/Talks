## 1. Vermont funders: who fits, grant sizes, deadlines, and whether they fund operating costs, compensation, or benefits

### Takeaway
I found **no Vermont foundation with a program that explicitly funds early-educator health insurance or benefits.** The realistic Vermont sources are small. They are either unrestricted or general-operating money that Willowell could legally spend on benefits, or relationships that could grow into multi-year operating support. The best near-term fits are:
- the Walter Cerf Community Fund (Addison County focus, small grants can be general operating)
- the A.D. Henderson Foundation (Vermont early childhood, offers general operating and multi-year support "when appropriate")
- United Way of Addison County's 3-year unrestricted Community Impact Funding (but its focus-area rotation doesn't fit until a later year)
- Porter Medical Center's Community Health Investment program (opens January 2027, health-equity framing)

The largest Vermont money stream for early-educator pay is public, not philanthropic: Act 76 / CCFAP rates.

### Inferences

## 2. National funders of early-educator compensation and benefits

### Takeaway
National early-educator compensation philanthropy funds **state and local systems, policy, and pilots run through intermediaries**, not individual small programs. I found no national funder that takes unsolicited requests from a single program to pay staff benefits. A Vermont program would reach this money only indirectly, through statewide intermediaries such as Building Bright Futures, Let's Grow Kids, or VTAEYC.

### Cited Findings
- **Early Educator Investment Collaborative**, created in 2017. Members: Ballmer Group, Bezos Family Foundation, Buffett Early Childhood Fund, Packard Foundation, Foundation for Child Development, Heising-Simons Foundation, and Stranahan Foundation; Gates is a partner funder.
  - It made $9M in grants to state and local partnerships in Colorado, Louisiana, and DC. The grants funded dedicated revenue streams and salary pilot demonstrations.
  - One grantee is expanding health insurance for early educators through data, education, and outreach.
  - [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/early-educator-investment-collaborative-makes-9-million-in-grants-to-address-unjust-compensation-for-early-childhood-education-workforce-301988571.html)
- **Kresge Foundation + Michigan MiLEAP (Feb 2026):** a neighborhood pilot in Detroit's 48221 zip code. The state gives access to an employer-sponsored benefits package, "Nurture Benefits," covering health, vision, dental, life insurance, and retirement for child care providers. Kresge supplements it. — [Michigan MiLEAP press release](https://www.michigan.gov/mileap/press-releases/2026/02/27/mileap-kresge-foundation-partner-early-childhood-educators-neighborhood-based-pilot)
- **Washington DC Early Childhood Educator Pay Equity Fund:** funded by a tax on high earners. It also subsidizes health premiums through HealthCare4ChildCare, open to DC-resident child care staff and their dependents since 2023. Its evaluation is funded by the Bezos Family Foundation, the Klingenstein Fund, DC Action, and the Early Care & Education Funders Collaborative. — [Results4America case study](https://catalog.results4america.org/case-studies/early-childhood-educator-pay-equity-fund-washington-d-c); [Mathematica study](https://www.mathematica.org/projects/washington-dc-early-childhood-educator-pay-equity-fund-impact-and-cost-effectiveness-study)
- **Illinois issue brief on health insurance for the early childhood workforce:** notes the substantial ACA (Affordable Care Act) public funding available for coverage. Several programs use it to lower staff premiums rather than paying for coverage outright. — [IDEC issue brief (2024)](https://idec.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idec/events/documents/quality-workforce/2024/2024-issue-brief-healthins-article.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- The national models (DC, Michigan) are **public subsidy plus philanthropy at the state level.** For Willowell, the useful moves are:
  1. Use these models as precedent when talking to Vermont donors and legislators.
  2. Tell Building Bright Futures, Let's Grow Kids, and VTAEYC that Willowell would join a Vermont ECE benefits pilot if one is created.
- The ACA-subsidy point suggests that grant dollars may go further if paired with a structure that lets staff use premium tax credits. Plan design belongs to the insurance research workstream, not this one.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether W.K. Kellogg, Alliance for Early Success, Heising-Simons, Buffett, or Gary Community Ventures currently fund anything in Vermont. Search results showed no Vermont grants or pilots.

## 3. Endowment-for-benefits models, naming, and multi-year commitments

### Takeaway
I found few documented precedents of nonprofits endowing staff health premiums. The closest is Clarkson University's endowed medical-assistance fund for uninsured costs. The math is simple, though: at a 5% draw, every $14K/yr of coverage needs about $280K of endowment. Covering the $60–140K/yr range needs about $1.2M–$2.8M.

### Cited Findings
- **Clarkson University, Howard Lechler '48 Endowed Medical Assistance Fund** (est. 2000): helps regular faculty and staff with extraordinary medical expenses that insurance doesn't cover. It distributes under the university's endowment spending rule, and undistributed income returns to principal. — [Clarkson fund guidelines](https://www.clarkson.edu/sites/default/files/2023-06/Howard-Lechler-Fund-Guidelines.pdf)
- Cornell's "endowed" staff benefits refers to an employee category paid from the endowed side of the university, not a dedicated benefits endowment. It is **not a model.** — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/290211)
- **Constraint:** VCF's Walter Cerf Fund excludes endowments, and Lintilhac excludes unsolicited endowment campaigns. Institutional funders generally won't seed a benefits endowment. — [VCF Cerf](https://vermontcf.org/funds/the-walter-cerf-community-fund/); [Lintilhac](https://lintilhacfoundation.org/how-to-apply/)
- **Multi-year unrestricted precedent:** United Way of Addison County's CIF awards are 3-year unrestricted grants. — [UWAC](https://unitedwayaddisoncounty.org/for-non-profits)

### Inferences
These are arithmetic, not sourced.
- Endowment sizing at a 5% draw:
  - 1 FTE (about $14K/yr): about $280K
  - 4.6 FTE ($64.5K/yr): about $1.29M
  - 10 FTE ($140K/yr): about $2.8M
- At a more conservative 4% draw: $1.6M for $64.5K/yr, and $3.5M for $140K/yr.
- Premiums rising roughly 5–10%/yr will outpace a fixed 4–5% draw. **A benefits endowment needs either growth gifts or a hybrid model:** an endowment for the base, plus annual giving or CCFAP revenue for premium growth.
- **Bridge structure:** fund benefits with 3-year bridge pledges while the endowment builds. For example, recruit 5–10 donors to pledge $5K–$15K/yr for 3 years, giving $50–100K/yr. Separately raise a named endowment. A "per-educator" naming level (about $280K to endow one educator's coverage permanently) gives donors a concrete unit.
- **Campaign fit:** keep the benefits ask distinct from the $1M loan-payoff/reserve campaign, or add it as a clearly labeled "Educator Well-Being" component. Mixing in a recurring-cost purpose without an endowment risks a funding cliff in year 4.

### Gaps
- I found no documented case of a small independent school or early childhood center endowing staff health premiums specifically. No public examples surfaced in search.
- I found no published donor-practice data on multi-year compensation pledges for early childhood programs.

## 4. Private operating foundation (POF) status: constraints on receiving grants

### Takeaway
Willowell files a 990-PF as a private operating foundation. That status has three consequences:
- **Individual donors are fine:** POFs get public-charity deduction limits (60% of AGI for cash).
- **Most donor-advised fund (DAF) grants are fine:** POFs are 170(b)(1)(A) organizations, so DAF sponsors can grant to them without expenditure responsibility.
- **Grants from other private foundations are harder.** A private foundation granting to a POF must exercise **expenditure responsibility**, unless Willowell qualifies as an "exempt operating foundation." Such grants still count toward the funder's payout. Some funders limit their grants to public charities and will decline.

Willowell's finances may also support **reclassification as a public charity**, either as a school or under the 509(a)(2) support test. That would remove this friction and is worth a CPA review.

### Cited Findings
- **Willowell's numbers** (FYE June 2025 990-PF, EIN 03-0366363, Bristol VT):
  - Revenue: $723,006; expenses: $706,497
  - Contributions: $220,097 (30.4% of revenue)
  - Charitable disbursements: $679,539
  - Net assets: $1,470,526; total assets: $1,844,354; liabilities: $373,828
  - [ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/030366363)
- **IRC §4945(d)(4):** a private foundation grant is a taxable expenditure unless the grantee is:
  - a 509(a)(1) or (2) organization,
  - a qualifying 509(a)(3) organization, or
  - "an exempt operating foundation (as defined in section 4940(d)(2))",
  - or unless the foundation exercises expenditure responsibility.
  - [26 USC 4945 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4945)
- **§4940(d)(2), "exempt operating foundation":** requires all of these:
  - it is an operating foundation;
  - it has been **publicly supported for at least 10 taxable years** (it met 170(b)(1)(A)(vi) or 509(a)(2));
  - at least 75% of its board are not disqualified individuals, and the board is broadly representative of the public;
  - no officer is a disqualified individual.
  - [26 USC 4940 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4940)
- **§4942(g)(1)(A):** grants to a private foundation **that is an operating foundation** count as qualifying distributions for the grantor, unless the grantee is controlled by the grantor or its disqualified persons. — [26 USC 4942 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4942)
- **Expenditure responsibility** means a pre-grant inquiry, a written agreement (no lobbying, return of unused funds), regular grantee reports, and reporting on the grantor's 990-PF. The grantee keeps records for at least 4 years. — [Council on Foundations: Understanding Private Operating Foundations](https://cof.org/node/1160); [IRS: Expenditure Responsibility](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/private-foundations/grants-by-private-foundations-expenditure-responsibility)
- **DAFs:** the Pension Protection Act lets DAFs grant to all 170(b)(1)(A) organizations without expenditure responsibility, with an exception for certain supporting organizations. Operating foundations are among the 170(b)(1)(A) organizations. The Council recommends against DAF grants to *non-operating* private foundations. — [Council on Foundations: Taxable Distributions for DAFs](https://www.cof.org/content/taxable-distributions-donor-advised-funds)
- **Donor deductions:** the IRS deductibility code "POF" carries a 50% limit (60% for cash), the same as public charities. Ordinary private foundations ("PF") are generally 30%. — [IRS deductibility status codes](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search-deductibility-status-codes)
- Whether the 60% cash limit continues after 2025 should be checked against current law. A secondary source says it was scheduled to sunset after 12/31/2025. — [PG Calc](https://blog.pgcalc.com/new-deduction-limit-on-cash-gifts)
- **Path to public charity status:** §507(b)(1)(B) lets a private foundation terminate its status by meeting 509(a)(1), (2), or (3) for a continuous 60-month period. It must notify the IRS beforehand, now on Form 8940 filed through Pay.gov. A school seeking 170(b)(1)(A)(ii) status names that classification; Form 8940 Schedule M covers schools. — [IRS: Termination of PF status](https://www.irs.gov/node/801); [IRS Form 8940 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8940); [RSM on Pay.gov mandate](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2023/irs-mandates-electronic-form-8940-filing-on-pay-gov.html)
- An unverified secondary source says an organization that always met the public support test may use Form 8940 to correct its classification without the 60-month process. — [Charitable Allies](https://charitableallies.org/private-foundation-to-public-charity/)

### Inferences
- **Practical effect on fundraising:**
  - **Individuals and DAFs (including VCF donor-advised funds): no meaningful barrier.**
  - **Private foundations (Henderson, Hoehl, Lintilhac, family foundations):** they *can* give, but must do expenditure responsibility paperwork. Some small family foundations are restricted to 509(a)(1)/(2) grantees by policy and will decline.
  - **Community foundations like VCF and United Way** are public charities, so §4945 does not apply to them. They may still have internal policies, which should be confirmed.
- **Strategic flag (CPA/attorney review recommended):**
  - Willowell runs Pond Brook middle school and the Walden Year high school program with regular faculty, curriculum, and enrolled students. It may qualify as a **170(b)(1)(A)(ii) school**. Alternatively, tuition and program revenue plus broad contributions may pass the **509(a)(2)** support test. Contributions were already 30% of revenue, and the rest is likely tuition and program fees.
  - Reclassifying as a public charity would remove foundation-to-foundation friction and the 1.39% excise tax on net investment income. It would also make Willowell eligible for funders that restrict grants to public charities.
  - **This matters most once a benefits endowment adds investment income.**
  - The "exempt operating foundation" route requires 10 years of public support and board independence, so it is less likely to be quick.
- Grant applications should state Willowell's POF status up front, with its IRS determination letter and Publication 78 code, so private foundation funders can plan expenditure responsibility rather than discover it late.
