# Vermont Health-Coverage Eligibility Cliffs for ECE Workers Earning ~$30K–$60K (as of Oct 2026; plan years 2026 and 2027)

Research date: 2026-10-08. Most dollar thresholds come from Vermont Health Connect (VHC) and DVHA primary documents, which were downloaded and parsed directly. Key primary documents:
- VHC 2026 QHP subsidy thresholds PDF: https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf
- VHC 2027 QHP subsidy thresholds PDF: https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2027-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf
- DVHA/VHC 2026 Medicaid (MCA) FPL chart, effective April 1, 2026: https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-MCA-PIL-FPL-Disregard-Chart.pdf
- VHC 2026 applicable percentages (federal plus Vermont Premium Assistance): https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/Applicable-Percentages-2026.pdf
- VHC 2026 plan designs and final rates: https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026_PlanDesigns_FinalRates.pdf
- VHC 2026 enhanced silver (CSR) plan designs: https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026_CSR_Plan_Designs.pdf

**Bottom line for the board.** For a single educator earning $30K–$60K, the "free healthcare" is not Medicaid. A single adult loses Vermont Medicaid at about $22,026/yr. In that income range, the real cliffs are:
1. **200% FPL.** Above this line the educator loses the Silver 87 cost-sharing plan: $31,300 single for 2026 coverage, $31,920 for 2027. The out-of-pocket maximum jumps from $2,700 to about $10,150.
2. **300% FPL.** Above this line the educator loses Vermont Premium Assistance: $46,950 single for 2026, $47,880 for 2027. This costs about $700–$720/yr at the line.
3. **400% FPL.** Above this line all federal help ends: $62,600 single for 2026, $63,840 for 2027. This costs about $9,350–$9,670/yr.

For a **parent of two (household of 3)**, the "free coverage" cliff is the adult Medicaid limit of **$3,141.85/month (about $37,702/yr)**. The children stay on free Dr. Dynasaur up to **$7,217.85/month (about $86,614/yr)**. For a **household of 4** (for example, a parent with three children, or two parents with two children), adult Medicaid ends at **$3,795.50/month (about $45,546/yr)**.

## 1. Vermont Medicaid for adults (expansion "MCA" adults): dollar limits, MAGI rules, how a mid-year raise is treated

### Takeaway
The adult Medicaid limit is 133% FPL plus a 5% income disregard, effectively 138% FPL. It is tested against current monthly income, using 2026 FPL from April 1, 2026 through March 2027:
- HH1: $1,835.50/mo (about $22,026/yr)
- HH2: $2,489.20/mo (about $29,870/yr)
- HH3: $3,141.85/mo (about $37,702/yr)
- HH4: $3,795.50/mo (about $45,546/yr)

So for single educators at $30K+, Medicaid is already out of reach. For single parents of one or two children earning roughly $30K–$45K, the Medicaid line is the binding cliff.

Income is MAGI, which for most people equals AGI on the federal return. - **DVHA's own rounding.** DVHA describes 138% FPL as "about $22,025/year for one person or $45,540 for a family of four." — [DVHA Federal Medicaid Changes page (updated 8/6/26)](https://dvha.vermont.gov/medicaid-changes)
- **Older and inconsistent figures (flag as 2025 or outdated).**
  - VHC's consumer "Medicaid and Dr. Dynasaur" page still says adults "at or below 133%," children "under 312%," and pregnant people "under 208%." Those are the thresholds before the 5% disregard.
  - The same page shows a children's table "effective April 1, 2025" (2025 FPL).
  - [VHC Medicaid and Dr. Dynasaur page](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/compare-plans/medicaid-and-dr-dynasaur)
- **Reporting income changes.**
  - People with Medicaid "should report major life events to Vermont Health Connect within 10 days." Others have 30 days. — [VHC FAQ / report-a-change (search snippet)](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/report-change)

- **How a termination works in practice.** A Vermont Human Services Board fair-hearing decision shows the typical sequence. The state found excess income, sent a termination notice effective at month's end, and referred the person to marketplace subsidies. — [Human Services Board fh-17-41 (2017)](https://outside.vermont.gov/agency/AHSHSB/Orders/Documents/2017/fh-17-41.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Monthly test.** Medicaid is tested on current monthly income, unlike marketplace subsidies, which use annual income. A raise that pushes monthly gross MAGI above the line is a reportable change and can end adult Medicaid at the end of the month after notice.
  - Example for HH3: about $18.12/hr × 40 hrs × 52 wks / 12 ≈ $3,141 a month. My arithmetic.
  - Educators paid on a 10-month or irregular schedule should be careful, because monthly income in pay months is what gets tested.
- **The Willowell "max pay" claim fits single parents.** It most likely refers to the adult Medicaid line for a staff member with children: about $37,702/yr for a parent of two, or about $29,870/yr for a parent of one. It cannot refer to a single adult with no children earning $30K+, since that person is already over the $22,026 limit.
- **Losing Medicaid opens a marketplace window.** Loss of Medicaid triggers a special enrollment period into VHC (standard ACA rule; not verified on a VT page this session).

### Gaps
- 2027 FPL guidelines are not yet published (expected January 2027). Vermont's Medicaid dollar limits from April 1, 2027 onward are therefore unknown.
- I did not find a current DVHA rule text stating exactly how many days after a reported raise coverage ends, or whether Vermont uses "reasonable compatibility" or projected annual income for people with fluctuating pay.
- I did not confirm whether Vermont adults keep Medicaid until renewal if they don't report a raise. Before 2027, adults get no 12-month continuous eligibility; that guarantee applies only to children.

## 2. Dr. Dynasaur (children and pregnant people): limits, premiums, status

### Takeaway
Dr. Dynasaur covers children under 19 up to 312% FPL plus the 5% disregard, effectively 317%:
- HH2: $5,717.20/mo (about $68,606/yr)
- HH3: $7,217.85/mo (about $86,614/yr)
- HH4: $8,718.50/mo (about $104,622/yr)

It covers pregnant people up to 208% plus 5%, effectively 213%:
- HH2: $3,841.20/mo
- HH3: $4,849.85/mo
- HH4: $5,857.50/mo

Monthly premiums of $15, $20 or $60 per family are **suspended indefinitely**, and VTLawHelp (Aug 2026) describes the program as "no premium" with no copays. Every child of a Willowell educator in the $30K–$60K range is eligible for free Dr. Dynasaur.

  - [VHC Medicaid and Dr. Dynasaur](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/compare-plans/medicaid-and-dr-dynasaur)

### Inferences
- In Willowell's pay band, children are never the problem. An educator household of 3 would need about $86.6K and a household of 4 about $104.6K before the children lose free coverage.
- Because the children are on Dr. Dynasaur, the parent who leaves Medicaid shops for self-only marketplace coverage. That makes the relevant benchmark the single (adult-only) premium.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the premium suspension was codified as a permanent elimination by statute. VHC says "suspended indefinitely."

## 3. Vermont Premium Assistance (VPA) and Vermont/federal cost-sharing reductions (CSR): thresholds and drop-offs

### Takeaway
- **VPA.** VPA cuts the expected contribution by 1.5% of household income, but only up to 300% FPL. Crossing 300% raises the benchmark contribution from 8.46% to 9.96% of income in 2026, and from about 8.72% to 10.22% in 2027.
  - For a single person this is about a **$704/yr cliff in 2026** at $46,950, and about a **$718/yr cliff in 2027** at $47,880.
- **CSR.** Cost-sharing help is effectively available only up to 200% FPL:
  - Silver 94 up to 150%
  - Silver 87 up to 200%
  - Vermont's state Silver 77 and Silver 73 tiers (up to 250% and 300%) are listed but enrollment is "blocked."
- **The 200% CSR cliff.** For a single person it sits at $31,300 (2026) or $31,920 (2027). Crossing it raises the out-of-pocket maximum from $2,700 (Silver 87) to about $10,150 (standard silver).

### Inferences
- **Size of the VPA cliff** (my arithmetic: contribution at the line × 1.5%):
  - 2026: single $46,950 → about $704/yr; HH3 $79,950 → about $1,199/yr.
  - 2027: single $47,880 → about $718/yr.
  - Net-loss zone, starting exactly at 300% FPL: a raise of up to about **$1,100** leaves a single educator worse off. The raise adds about 9.96% of itself in extra premium, plus roughly 27–28% payroll and income tax (7.65% FICA, 12% federal bracket, about 3.35% VT). So net gain ≈ 0.62 × raise − $704, which turns positive only above about $1,130. Approximate; the tax assumptions are mine.
- **Size of the 200% CSR cliff.** Premiums are continuous at 200% because the applicable percentage is 5.10% on both sides in 2026. What jumps is the out-of-pocket exposure: from $2,700 to $10,150 maximum, and from a $1,300 to a $3,750+ deductible. This is a "cliff" only for staff who actually use care. For a single educator it bites between $31,300 and $31,920.
- **Steep phase-out inside the 250–300% band (no cliff, but high marginal cost).** The VPA-adjusted percentage climbs from 6.94% to 8.46% across $39,125–$46,950 (single, 2026). Each extra $1 of income adds about 17¢ to the benchmark premium. Combined with taxes, the effective marginal rate is about 44%.

### Gaps
- I did not find a 2027 VHC applicable-percentage PDF. That VPA stays at 1.5 points in 2027 (giving 0.65%; 1.73–2.80%; 2.80–5.28%; 5.28–7.16%; 7.16–8.72%) is my inference from the 2027 federal table minus 1.5. However, the 2027 thresholds PDF still labels the ≤300% tier "VPA."
- The 2026 legislative session considered a state premium cap at 10% of MAGI (draft language, April 2026). I could not confirm enactment; see Section 4.

## 4. Federal premium tax credits 2026/2027: enhanced subsidies, 400% cliff, extension status, Vermont backfill

### Takeaway
The ARPA/IRA enhanced PTCs **expired December 31, 2025**. The House passed a 3-year extension in January 2026 (230–196), but **the Senate has not acted as of September 2026, and nothing has been signed**. The **400% FPL cliff is back** for 2026 and 2027:
- 2026: $62,600 single / $106,600 HH3 / $128,600 HH4
- 2027: $63,840 single / $109,280 HH3 / $132,000 HH4

Vermont did **not** backfill. VPA remains the pre-existing 1.5-point subsidy up to 300% FPL. A 10%-of-MAGI state cap was drafted in April 2026, but I found no evidence it was enacted.

### Cited Findings
- **Expiration and Senate history.** The Senate rejected the 3-year extension (S. 3385) in December 2025. — [Healio, Dec 2025](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20251211/senate-rejects-legislation-to-extend-affordable-care-act-subsidies-in-2026)
- **House action.** The House passed a 3-year extension 230–196 on Jan 9, 2026, with 17 Republicans in favor. — [AHA News](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-01-09-house-passes-bill-extending-enhanced-premium-tax-credits); [NACo](https://www.naco.org/news/house-passes-three-year-extension-aca-enhanced-premium-tax-credits)
- **Status now.** Secondary sources from September 2026 say the Senate has not acted and nothing has been signed. A bipartisan Senate "CARE Act" (2-year restoration with minimum premiums and income caps) was discussed, but I found no report that it advanced. — [Health Affairs Forefront](https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/extending-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-things-stand); [ASTHO blog 2026](https://www.astho.org/communications/blog/2026/aca-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-legislative-developments-2025-2026/); [benefitchamps (Sept 2026)](https://benefitchamps.com/resources/articles/what-changed-2027-open-enrollment/)

- **Backfill options DVHA costed, but none enacted.**

  - A reinsurance program cutting premiums 10%: $10.3M state plus $27.2M federal pass-through.
  - [DVHA Marketplace Report, Feb 12, 2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Health%20Care/Miscellaneous/W~Adaline%20Strumolo~DVHA%20Marketplace%20Report~2-12-2026.pdf)

## 5. H.R.1 ("One Big Beautiful Bill Act," 2025): Medicaid and marketplace changes affecting low-wage VT ECE workers

### Takeaway
In Vermont:
- **Work ("community engagement") requirements** start **Jan 1, 2027** for new applicants and **Mar 1, 2027** for current members at renewal.
  - The requirement is met with $580/month of earnings or 80 hours/month of work, school or volunteering.
  - Caregivers of a child under 14 are exempt.
- **Six-month renewals** start in 2027.
- **Retroactive coverage** drops to 1 month for adults (2 months for children and pregnant people).
- **New cost-sharing** for certain expansion adults starts in 2028.

On the marketplace side, the **PTC repayment caps are gone starting tax year 2026**. Pre-enrollment verification and the end of passive auto-renewal with APTC begin with plan year 2028.

### Cited Findings
- **DVHA timeline (page updated 8/6/26):**
  - Oct 1, 2026: immigrant eligibility limits.
  - Jan 1, 2027: work requirements for new applicants, 6-month renewals, and the retroactive-coverage cut.
  - Mar 1, 2027: work requirements for current members at renewal.
  - "During 2028": additional Medicaid cost-sharing for certain adults.
  - [DVHA Federal Medicaid Changes](https://dvha.vermont.gov/medicaid-changes)
- **How to meet the requirement.**
  - Monthly income of at least $580, but not above 138% FPL, or
  - at least 80 hours of work, education, work programs, self-employment, volunteering or community service. Hours can be combined.

  - [DVHA Federal Medicaid Changes](https://dvha.vermont.gov/medicaid-changes)
- **Exemptions and who is unaffected.**
  - Exemption examples include caregivers of a child under 14 or of a disabled person, and people with certain health conditions.
  - Unaffected: people under 19, 65 or older, pregnant or up to 12 months postpartum, or on Medicare.
  - [DVHA Federal Medicaid Changes](https://dvha.vermont.gov/medicaid-changes); [Little Rivers Health Care guide](https://www.littlerivers.org/medicaid-is-changing-little-rivers-is-here-to-help-you-stay-covered)
- **Implementation status.**

  - Postcards went to all Medicaid beneficiaries in early August 2026.
  - A medically-frail exemption will rely on self-attestation.
  - The CMS rule was not yet final as of June 2026, so standards may change.

  - Excess-APTC repayment caps are eliminated beginning tax year 2026. Under the 2025 rules, the cap was $375 for a single filer at 200% FPL and $1,575 near 400%. Now enrollees owe the full excess. — [CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/blog/marketplace-enrollees-could-face-surprise-tax-increases-next-year-thanks-to-the-republican); [healthinsurance.org FAQ](https://www.healthinsurance.org/faqs/if-your-income-last-year-was-higher-than-expected-do-you-have-to-pay-back-some-of-the-advance-premium-tax-credits-that-you-received-for-marketplace-coverage/)

## 7. Employer-coverage interaction: affordability %, QSEHRA, ICHRA, Medicaid, family glitch

### Takeaway
If Willowell offered "affordable" minimum-value coverage, employees would lose federal PTC **and therefore Vermont Premium Assistance**, because 33 V.S.A. § 1812 ties VPA to PTC eligibility. "Affordable" means the employee's self-only share is at or below **9.96% of household income in 2026** and **10.22% in 2027**.
- **A QSEHRA** that is "affordable" blocks PTC entirely, even if declined. An unaffordable QSEHRA reduces PTC dollar-for-dollar.
- **An ICHRA** is affordable if the lowest-cost silver self-only premium minus the monthly allowance is at or below 1/12 × 9.96% of household income. For a Vermont educator at $45K that requires an allowance of about **$915/month or more**. A smaller ICHRA is "unaffordable," so the employee can opt out and keep PTC/VPA.
- **Medicaid and Dr. Dynasaur** eligibility is income-based; I did not find a source saying an employer offer bars Medicaid.
- **The family-glitch fix** (2022 rule, effective 2023) appears still in force.

### Cited Findings
- **Affordability percentages.**
  - 2026 is 9.96% (VHC; Covered California lists 9.962%). — [VHC Applicable-Percentages-2026.pdf](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/Applicable-Percentages-2026.pdf); [Covered CA Family Glitch FAQ](https://hbex.coveredca.com/toolkit/pdfs/Family_Glitch_FAQ.pdf)
  - 2027 is 10.22% (Rev. Proc. 2026-26, July 2026). The 2027 FPL safe harbor is $135.92/mo for plan years starting January–June 2027. — [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/insights/law-and-policy/2027-affordability-percentage-for-employer-health-coverage-increases/); [Sequoia](https://www.sequoia.com/2026/07/irs-sets-2027-aca-affordability-percentage-at-10-22/); [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/7/21/revenue-procedure-2026-26-technical-overview-of-2027-indexing-adjustments-for-premium-tax-credits-and-affordability-standards)
- **VPA requires PTC eligibility.** It is available to those "eligible for federal premium tax credits under 26 U.S.C. § 36B with income ≤300% FPL." — [33 V.S.A. § 1812](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/33/018/01812)
- **VHC offers an "Affordable Employer Coverage Tool."** It notes that families with employer offers "may have new opportunities for savings," a reference to the family-glitch fix. — [VHC Financial Help](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/financial-help)
- **QSEHRA rules.**
  - An affordable QSEHRA means no PTC "even if you haven't accepted the offer."
  - If unaffordable, APTC is reduced by the monthly QSEHRA amount.
  - The test is the second-lowest silver self-only premium minus the QSEHRA monthly amount.
  - The 2026 QSEHRA maximum is $6,450/yr ($537.50/mo) self-only and $13,100/yr family.
  - [MNsure QSEHRA worksheet](https://www.mnsure.org/assets/MNsure-QSEHRA-worksheet_tcm34-408781.pdf); [HealthCare.gov QSEHRA worksheet](https://www.HealthCare.gov/downloads/qsehra-worksheet.pdf); [healthinsurance.org QSEHRA glossary](https://healthinsurance.org/glossary/qualified-small-employer-health-reimbursement-arrangements)
- **ICHRA rules.**
  - Affordable if the lowest-cost silver premium minus the monthly ICHRA allowance is at or below 9.96% of monthly household income (2026).
  - If affordable, the employee cannot claim PTC. If unaffordable, the employee may opt out and claim PTC.
  - People enrolled only in Medicaid generally can't use an ICHRA.
  - [PeopleKeep ICHRA affordability 2026](https://www.peoplekeep.com/blog/determining-ichra-affordability-in-2021); [Thatch](https://thatch.com/blog/ichra-plans-and-premium-tax-credits); [PeopleKeep ICHRA vs QSEHRA](https://www.peoplekeep.com/blog/qsehra-vs.-ichra-how-do-they-compare)
- **Family-glitch fix.** The October 2022 final rule took effect for 2023. It measures family-member affordability on the family premium and adds a minimum-value test for family members. The IRS deferred any separate ICHRA family rule. — [PeopleKeep family glitch](https://www.peoplekeep.com/blog/what-is-the-family-glitch-and-is-it-fixed); [Paylocity](https://www.paylocity.com/resources/resource-library/alert/irs-final-rules-fix-aca-family-glitch)
  - I found no repeal or change through 2026, but did not check the Federal Register directly.

### Inferences
- **What an affordable ICHRA would cost Willowell** (my arithmetic, using the lowest-cost single silver of $1,288.59/mo in 2026 with no age rating):

  | Employee household income | Allowance needed for "affordable" |
  |---|---|
  | $35,000 | ≥ $998/mo |
  | $45,000 | ≥ $915/mo |
  | $55,000 | ≥ $832/mo |

  - That is about $10–12K per employee per year. Anything less is "unaffordable," and staff can decline and keep PTC plus VPA. This mirrors the "$45,000 → $373.50 monthly limit" example in the PeopleKeep source.
- **A QSEHRA rarely helps subsidized staff.** In Vermont, at about $1,299 SLCSP, even the 2026 maximum QSEHRA ($537.50/mo) leaves the plan "unaffordable" for anyone under about $91K. So subsidized staff would see their APTC cut dollar-for-dollar by the QSEHRA. The QSEHRA adds little net value for them, though it does help with out-of-pocket costs if structured for those expenses.
  - Possible VPA interaction: VPA pays only if the premium is "not entirely covered by the federal premium tax credit." A QSEHRA reduces PTC; how DVHA treats the QSEHRA for VPA was not found.
- **Small-group coverage would usually be "affordable" and strip subsidies.** If Willowell bought small-group coverage and charged employees about $0–$300/mo for self-only, that would typically be "affordable" (9.96% of $35K = $290/mo). Employees would then lose PTC and VPA, while their children would keep Dr. Dynasaur.
  - For low earners this can make staff worse off if the employer plan has high deductibles.
- **Medicaid is unaffected by an employer offer** (standard MAGI rule). Vermont Medicaid can coexist with employer insurance as secondary coverage. The sources in this session only addressed ICHRA participation, not Medicaid eligibility itself, so confirm with HCA or DVHA.

### Gaps
- I found no Vermont-specific guidance on how VPA is computed when a QSEHRA reduces PTC.
- I found no primary IRS citation for the Medicaid-and-employer-offer question.
