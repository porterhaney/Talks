# Charlotte, VT Wastewater Problem: What the Town of Charlotte's Own Documents Say (as of Oct 10, 2026)

**Important note on the starting URL:** The assigned page, https://charlottevt.org/index.asp?SEC=%7B0EF50649-2F98-473C-92F7-93726C8E195A%7D, now loads as **"2026 DRB Meetings"** (the Development Review Board agenda/packet list). It is not a dedicated wastewater project page. Its only substantive wastewater content is the Old Brick Store dispute (see Q1 and Q6). The town's dedicated page at charlottevt.org/wastewater covers only septic/water-supply permitting. Two older pages, "Expansion of West Village Wastewater System" (SEC={4B3516CA…}) and "Studies" (SEC=BF715565…), still show in search indexes but **return 404** on the current site. The notes below therefore draw on the 2026 Town Plan, its Appendix C (which reproduces the 2011 Wastewater Committee report), the sewer ordinances, the 2024 East & West Villages Project documents, Selectboard minutes, packets and staff reports from 2024–2026, and the FY2025 Town Report.

**Naming convention used by the town:** The "municipal" or "village" wastewater system is the town-owned leach field on the **Burns property**, located in West Charlotte Village. It serves Town Hall, the Library, Fire & Rescue and the Senior Center. The Selectboard sits as the "Board of Sewage Disposal Commissioners" when it acts on this system. Separate town-run systems serve **Thompson's Point**, a seasonal lakeshore camp community on town land, and **Charlotte Central School**.

## Q1. What exactly is the wastewater problem?

### Takeaway
Charlotte has no municipal sewer. Village development depends on individual on-site septic systems and wells. Small historic lots, poor soils and required isolation distances make those systems hard to site, so housing and commercial growth in the two village centers has been effectively blocked. The town's one small shared system (≈6,499 gpd at Burns) is mostly committed to town buildings. Its ordinance largely bars residential hookups, and the town has never built the expansion its 2011 committee recommended. Failing private systems are a secondary issue: the town documents them (2011 inventory; the 2026 Old Brick Store violation), but the ordinance excludes them from allocation.

### Cited Findings
- **Small lots and poor soils:** "Previous studies of the West Charlotte Village demonstrated that on-site wastewater disposal for some properties is challenging due to small lot size and/or inadequate soils." (2011 Wastewater Committee report, Sept 10, 2011) — [2026 Town Plan Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **On-site systems can't support more than a handful of new units:** "any new construction in the villages requires proven water supply and septic disposal capacity… Currently, the only tools available are individual wells and on-site septic systems, which require large lots due to required isolation distances between wells and leach fields. To accommodate more than a handful of new homes and businesses, more centralized water and sewage capacity would be needed." (Nov 2024) — [Villages Project FAQ v4](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Charlotte_Villages_Project_FAQs_v4_-11.24.pdf)
- **Village growth vs. rural growth:**
  - The FAQ says only **15 homes** were built in the Village districts in 20 years, against **231** in rural areas. It attributes this to the 5-acre/150-ft frontage village zoning, the 20%/30% coverage caps, and water/septic constraints (Nov 2024) — [Villages FAQ](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Charlotte_Villages_Project_FAQs_v4_-11.24.pdf)
  - The final report puts it at **29 homes** in the Village districts over 30 years, against **452** in the rest of town (Oct 17, 2024) — [Final East & West Villages Report (Dubois & King)](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/20241017_Final_East_and_West_Villages_Report.pdf)
- **Seen as a precondition to any development:** "Because Charlotte generally now lacks available community wastewater capacity and water supply in the villages, many residents commented on these factors as a major obstacle to more development there." "It is not a question if Charlotte's villages will need additional water and wastewater capacity to grow, but how much, and where." (May 2024) — [Villages Project Update #4](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/VMP_update_4_5.24.pdf)
- **Resident fears:** Residents fear new construction will deplete private wells (especially in the West Village). They worry taxpayers will be "on the hook" for water and wastewater capital costs, and that upgrades "will lead to the State mandating development in Charlotte." (2024) — [Final Villages Report p.27](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/20241017_Final_East_and_West_Villages_Report.pdf)
- **Town Plan acknowledgment:** "Significant concerns of water and septic capacity in the west village have been repeatedly raised." "Additional water supply and wastewater infrastructure is needed in the West Charlotte Village." (adopted Mar 3, 2026) — [2026 Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Failing systems:** The 2011 committee had the Zoning Administrator inventory "challenged systems." Per the Munson study, "the systems serving at least some of the existing development in the village are likely to fail." (2011) — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **Effect on community services:**
  - Charlotte Children's Center: "the main limiting factor for accepting more children had been limited wastewater disposal capacity." — [2026 Town Plan ch.2](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
  - Affordable housing on the Burns parcel: in 2007 the Selectboard dropped a planned vote to convey 5 acres for up to 9 affordable units "because of new information… regarding the use of the wastewater disposal capacity on the parcel." — [2026 Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Central School is at its limit:** The school's "innovative wastewater system" has a design flow of 10,250 GPD and disposal capacity of 6,000 GPD. This "represents the upper limit of school capacity, where expansion would prove to be difficult due to environmental constraints." — [2026 Town Plan ch.2 p.2-53](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Thompson's Point:** "The soils have severe limitations for on-site sewage disposal." A community system was "permitted and constructed in 1994 as a 'best fix' system to serve only the existing camps." Leases allow only seasonal use partly because of soils and wastewater permit conditions — [2026 Town Plan ch.2 p.2-10](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Old Brick Store (2026, West Village):**
  - The Zoning Administrator wrote that ANR "issued a Notice of Alleged Violation April 17, 2026 concerning the Store's wastewater system and water supply" at 290 Ferry Road. ANR did not require the store to close — [ZA Response Letter (on the 2026 DRB page)](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10462/ZAResponse_Letter_to_Request_for_Zoning_Enforcement.pdf)
  - Appellants (neighbors Beal-Kenyon, via counsel) allege "the Store is operating with a failed wastewater system" and needs a Non-Community Public Water System permit. A DRB appeal hearing is set for **Oct 14, 2026** — [Notice of Appeal, Jul–Aug 2026](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Beal-Kenyon_Notice_of_Appeal_of_ZA_August_20_2026_Decision.pdf); [DRB Oct 14, 2026 agenda](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/DRB_agenda_20261014.pdf)
- **Water quality:** In the natural-resources chapter, the Town Plan lists "poorly operating household septic systems" among pollution sources. Its shoreland policy calls the Thompson's Point community wastewater/water infrastructure a possible "model" — [2026 Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)

### Inferences
- In the town's own documents, the core issue is less a public-health crisis from failures and more a structural growth constraint. Without community wastewater, the villages cannot absorb the housing the Town Plan and state targets assign to them.
- The Old Brick Store case is a live 2026 example of the West Village problem. A small commercial lot's septic and water supply cannot easily support expanded use.

### Gaps
- No town document found with a current (post-2011) count or map of failed or failing septic systems in either village.
- The Town Plan's water-quality text is general. No town study links village septics to measured surface or groundwater contamination.

## Q2. Which areas are affected?

### Takeaway
**West Charlotte Village** is the main focus: the Ferry Road corridor between US 7 and Greenbush Road, plus the Greenbush Road intersection. **East Charlotte Village** is covered in the 2024 Villages Project but has no town system or capacity study. Thompson's Point (lakeshore leaseholds) and Charlotte Central School have their own town-run systems with their own constraints.

### Cited Findings
- **Sewer Service Area:** Allocation is limited to the "Sewer Service Area" in Appendix A of the ordinance, "generally comprising a portion of the Village Commercial District and West Charlotte Village District." "Only those properties located within the Village Commercial District are eligible for allocation of capacity in the Disposal System." Satellite facilities may serve only the West Charlotte Village District (approved Jan 6, 2020) — [Sewer Allocation Ordinance](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Approved_Sewer_Alloc_Ordinance-20200106.pdf)
- **2008 Munson study area:** 89 parcels between the west side of Route 7 and the railroad tracks, running north along Greenbush Road (Clemmons and Braun properties) and south (Burns and Gaujac properties). Estimated existing flow was 42,385 gpd, with 16,935 gpd (40%) "likely" or "questionable" candidates for a community system — [2011 report in Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **East Village:** The 2024 options table rates a municipal indirect-discharge system as "Exists in West Village and feasible to expand • Feasible in East Village" — [Final Villages Report p.27](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/20241017_Final_East_and_West_Villages_Report.pdf)
- **Town Plan policy:** It directs "new residential and commercial development primarily into the West Charlotte Village and East Charlotte Village." It also calls for education on "nonmunicipal wastewater and infrastructure improvements in the villages to allow them to accommodate the substantial majority of Charlotte's future housing needs." CCRPC-derived housing targets are 100 new units by 2030 and 353 by 2050 (mid scenario), with "the substantial majority" expected in Village Centers/Areas — [2026 Town Plan ch.1](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Three town-run systems:** "The town is responsible for the maintenance and operation of three wastewater systems… Thompson's Point, West Charlotte Village, and the Charlotte Central School." — [2026 Town Plan p.2-53](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Thompson's Point today:** The system serves lakeshore camps on town-owned leased land. A Thompson's Point Wastewater Advisory Committee exists under the TP Wastewater Disposal System Ordinance (amended Oct 8, 2018). Civil Engineering Associates' Chris Galipeau is consulting engineer and Lance Perlee is system operator — [TP Wastewater Advisory Committee page](https://charlottevt.org/index.asp?SEC=36CEA8EE-1CAE-4B12-88E7-2E31C0EA7C3E); [Ordinances page](https://charlottevt.org/index.asp?SEC=D12051E3-8E19-4619-AE45-308D09BECC25)

### Inferences
- In practice the "village wastewater" debate concerns a few dozen parcels on Ferry Road and Greenbush Road in West Charlotte. East Charlotte has had planning attention but no engineering work in the town record.

### Gaps
- The Sewer Service Area map (Appendix A; also posted as Charlotte_Sewer_Service_Area_Map.pdf in 2026 SB materials) is graphic only. Parcel-level boundaries were not extracted.
- No East Village wastewater study or soils evaluation was found on the town site.

## Q3. What studies were done, by whom, when, and what did they find?

### Takeaway
The engineering base is mostly old: Otter Creek Engineering soils work (2004), a Civil Engineering Associates refinement (2007), and the Munson demand study (2008). These were synthesized by the citizen Charlotte Wastewater Committee (chair Dave Marshall, P.E.) in its Sept 2011 report. Follow-up committee reports came in 2016 and 2017. The 2024 Dubois & King/CCRPC Villages Project only summarized options and recommended a future Wastewater Feasibility Study. The record shows no evidence that study had begun as of October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **2004, Otter Creek Engineering (OCE):** Retained to do soil investigations on the town's Burns and Barber Hill properties. Report dated Aug 2, 2004 "identified nine different potential wastewater disposal sites on the two properties with a total potential wastewater disposal capacity of 34,460 gallons per day." — [2011 report, Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **Jan 2007, Civil Engineering Associates (CEA):** Retained to refine the OCE work. CEA mapped disposal footprints, accounted for well isolation distances and refined site capacities — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
  - Burns-property capacities in gpd, reported at 50% of total to reserve state-required replacement area: SW 500; North Triangle 480; Town WW North 2,620; Town WW East 7,300; Town WW South 100; Flea Market 1,130. Total 12,130 gpd.
  - Barber Hill: Old Lantern-West 4,200 gpd and Old Lantern-South 2,880 gpd (mounds only, due to shallow seasonal high groundwater).
- **Mar 2008, Michael Munson, PhD (land use planner):** Assembled demand data for 89 West Village parcels. Total estimated existing flow 42,385 gpd; likely connections 8,675 gpd plus questionable 8,260 gpd, for 16,935 gpd (40%) — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **2009 Town Meeting advisory question:** Voters approved asking the Selectboard to "explore the construction of a public or community facility or facilities for… wastewater disposal for residential and commercial use in West Charlotte Village." The Selectboard appointed a task force Aug 23, 2010 — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **Sept 10, 2011, Charlotte Wastewater Committee report:** Title: "Final Report on Potential Community Wastewater Service to the West Charlotte Village." Authors: Dave Marshall P.E., Vince Crockenberg, Dana Farley, Winslow Ladue. Work ran Aug 2010–May 2011. Key findings — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf):
  - **Existing system:** Built 2002 for ≈$125,000. Conventional in-ground field of ≈5,000 sf of trench; 296 lf gravity main, duplex pump station, 3,160 lf of 4" force main. Permitted for 4,999 gpd with 3,102 gpd committed (Town Office 320, Library 195, Fire 350, Senior Center 2,000, infiltration 237), leaving 1,897 gpd unused.
  - **Expansion limits:** The system can go to 6,499 gpd under then-current rules "with no physical modifications." At ≥6,500 gpd an **Indirect Discharge Permit** is required, with "much more stringent" standards and significant groundwater testing.
  - **Habitat for Humanity:** Separately uses 1,260 gpd on the Burns property, which does not count against the municipal system.
  - **Lavalette easement:** West of Greenbush Rd near the railroad; permitted but unbuilt. State permit WW-4-1150 allows 900 gpd for the town (expandable to 1,200) and 1,080 gpd held by the Charlotte Land Trust.
  - **Barber Hill:** Expansion potential "limited, at best." Only 25% of capacity is usable for municipal purposes under the contract.
  - **Total capacity:** Existing permitted 5,899 gpd, plus 14,370 gpd potential additional primary capacity using new state rules.
  - **Resident survey:** At a then-estimated $35,000–$40,000 connection cost plus ≈$500/yr, West Village residents "generally expressed little interest… primarily because of the cost." Ferry Road businesses showed "considerable interest."
  - **Town Meeting 2011 survey (85 responses):** Voted 53–26 to make unused capacity available for private use. Voted 43–22 against charging new users less than full pro-rated cost. Top priority was adaptive reuse (7.5/10); new residential was lowest (4.2).
- **2015–16 and 2017 committees:** The Town Plan cites a second report, "Final Report on Expanding Community Wastewater Service in the West Charlotte Village" (Charlotte Wastewater Committee, July 16, 2016). It also says a 2017 committee "provided recommendations for the adoption of municipal sewer allocation and use ordinances" — [2026 Town Plan ch.1 p.1-38](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **"2012 committee" in the Town Plan:** The plan says a "study Committee created in 2012 recommended an additional 435 GPD be retained" for municipal uses, leaving "1,462 GPD of excess capacity as currently permitted." It says another 1,500 GPD could be permitted (6,499 total) for "almost 3,000 GPD of additional capacity." The plan adds: "Questions remain as to if and how this excess capacity might be allocated." — [2026 Town Plan p.2-53](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **2023–2024 East & West Villages Project (Dubois & King, consultant; CCRPC project management):**
  - **Funding:** Started from a $17,000 state Bylaw Modernization Grant (80/20 match). CCRPC added $68,800 plus a separate $12,000 transportation study, for a total of $97,800 — [Villages FAQ](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Charlotte_Villages_Project_FAQs_v4_-11.24.pdf)
  - **Acceptance:** The PC voted to accept the revised report on Nov 7, 2024. The report is "not a formal planning document" — [Villages FAQ](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Charlotte_Villages_Project_FAQs_v4_-11.24.pdf)
  - **Wastewater findings:** The report says the town "has not fully explored the options to expand the treatment capacity of the existing system as outlined in the Committee's report." It says solutions "can best be explored in a Feasibility Study, which is planned by CCRPC as a follow-up to this Villages Project," and that a future "Wastewater Feasibility Study" should address village needs — [Final Villages Report, Oct 17, 2024](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/20241017_Final_East_and_West_Villages_Report.pdf)
- **Related studies:** The Town Plan also lists a "Report on the Geology and Hydrogeology of Charlotte (2010)" and the 2010 East Charlotte Village Planning Project — [2026 Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Thompson's Point engineering:** David S. Marshall, "Thompson's Point Wastewater Disposal System: 2016 Annual Inspection – System ID-9-0244," Civil Engineering Associates, Aug 5, 2016 (cited in Town Plan) — [2026 Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)

### Inferences
- Dave Marshall, P.E. (of CEA per the 2016 TP inspection citation) chaired the 2011 committee and was still chairing the town Wastewater Committee in Sept 2024. One engineer has shaped the town's wastewater thinking for 15+ years.
- No new engineering capacity study (for example, Indirect Discharge testing at Burns, or East Village soils) appears in town records since 2007.

### Gaps
- The 2016 and 2017 committee reports, the 2004 OCE report and the 2007 CEA report are cited but were not found online. The town "Studies" page that likely hosted them now returns 404.
- The 2026 Town Plan's "2012 committee" may be a misdating of the 2011 report, whose reservations (435 gpd and so on) match. Unconfirmed.
- No evidence was found that the CCRPC-planned Wastewater Feasibility Study had been funded or started by Oct 2026.

## Q4. What solutions and options have been proposed (with gpd capacities)?

### Takeaway
The town's recommended path since 2011 has three parts:
1. Open the existing Burns municipal system to private users (done by ordinance in 2020).
2. Raise its permit from 4,999 to 6,499 gpd (done ≈2019).
3. Extend gravity sewer along Ferry and Greenbush Roads, with potential growth to ~15,000 gpd under a state Indirect Discharge Permit, plus satellite fields at Lavalette (900–1,200 gpd) and Barber Hill (2,000–4,000 gpd est.).

The collection-system extension and Indirect Discharge expansion have not been carried out. The 2024 Villages report lays out a menu of options: individual septic, private shared indirect discharge, municipal indirect discharge, or a municipal treatment plant (judged "likely infeasible").

### Cited Findings
- **2011 recommendations:** Expand use of the existing system to new users. Adopt a master plan (service area, phases, capacity increase from 4,999 to 6,499 gpd, reserved allocations, metering, O&M, budget, fees). Adopt an allocation/fee/O&M ordinance. Put the private-connection question to voters at Town Meeting 2012 — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **2011 reserved allocations within 6,499 gpd:**

  | Use | gpd |
  |---|---|
  | Town offices, library, fire & rescue (865 existing + 260 future) | 1,125 |
  | Senior Center | 2,025 |
  | Infiltration | 387 |
  | Failed-system / public-health reserve | 900 |
  | Future private allocations | ≈2,062 |

  — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **2011 collection-system master plan:**
  - Phase I: extend gravity sewer 300 ft east along Ferry Road and 560 ft west to the Greenbush Road intersection, then 300 ft south to the Village Commercial district limit. Estimated cost $125,000.
  - Phase II: a low-pressure force-main system for outlying users.
  - The committee also recommended metering actual flows to free up capacity — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **2011 financing options:** (1) new users build extensions incrementally, paid through connection fees; or (2) a municipal bond repaid by users in a service district — [Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)
- **Adopted capacity framework (Sewer Allocation Ordinance, Jan 6, 2020):**
  - The Burns system was "permitted… 4,999 gpd with an expected increase to 6,499 gpd by summer, 2019." The site "has the capacity to be expanded in the future beyond its permitted 6,499 GPD capacity to up to 15,000 GPD through the State of Vermont's Indirect Discharge Permit program."
  - Other Burns areas have more capacity, also subject to an Indirect Discharge Permit.
  - Satellite systems: Lavalette at 900 gpd permitted, expandable to 1,200 gpd; Barber Hill easements at an estimated 2,000–4,000 gpd with no permits.
  - Satellite capacity is prioritized for (1) senior housing, (2) home conversions/adaptive reuse, (3) affordable housing, (4) mixed use.
  - [Sewer Allocation Ordinance](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Approved_Sewer_Alloc_Ordinance-20200106.pdf)
- **Who can use the Burns system:** Allocations are limited to "(a) Municipal; (b) Commercial uses… adaptive re-use… new commercial uses; and (c) Elderly housing and affordable housing." "The correction of failed private systems is not eligible for allocation consideration." The municipal reservation totals 3,587 gpd (3,102 existing + 435 future) — [Sewer Allocation Ordinance](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Approved_Sewer_Alloc_Ordinance-20200106.pdf)
- **Permit status in 2024:** "The system is currently permitted for 6,499 gallons per day." (Dave Marshall, Wastewater Committee chair, Sept 23, 2024) — [SB minutes Sept 23, 2024](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/September_23_2024(2).pdf)
- **2024 options menu (Dubois & King):**
  - Individual septic: "If it fails, high cost to user… Site capacity limits development."
  - Private indirect discharge (shared): "More development options, still limited by site/soils."
  - Municipal indirect discharge: "Exists in West Village and feasible to expand • Feasible in East Village • Spreads costs over more users • Town maintains control."
  - Municipal sewer treatment plant: "Not limited by soils • High capital and administrative cost • Most potential for growth • Likely infeasible due to costs/permits."
  - Water options include shared or public drilled wells and Champlain Water District service ("Likely Feasible for West Charlotte Village").
  - [Final Villages Report p.27](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/20241017_Final_East_and_West_Villages_Report.pdf)
- **2026 Town Plan policy:**
  - The Town "will develop a framework for moving forward with the work completed by the Charlotte Wastewater Committee in 2010-2011, and 2016."
  - This includes "establishment / reestablishment of a Wastewater Advisory Committee" (Selectboard, PC, DRB, village residents and at least one non-village resident).
  - If that committee proceeds, a "town-wide education and outreach campaign" covering "alternatives to a public system including private, community systems," with a consultant evaluation that "could be funded through a grant."
  - The plan supports "nonmunicipal community based water and wastewater systems in West Charlotte Village."
  - [2026 Town Plan ch.1](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)
- **Thompson's Point:** Seasonal system with a design flow of 20,000 GPD. Peak use is historically ≈80% of capacity (July 4 week). The 2016 highest average daily demand was 8,356 GPD and the May–Aug 2016 average 5,608 GPD to the mounds. "As of 2016, the Town intends to expand the system to include at least seven residences along Lane's Lane… Adding greater capacity… may be challenging due to environmental constraints." — [2026 Town Plan p.2-53](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)

### Inferences
- The town has chosen to serve a small number of commercial, affordable and senior users from existing capacity, not to build a village-wide system. The 2026 Town Plan's emphasis on "nonmunicipal" and "private, community systems" signals continued reluctance to create a municipal sewer utility.
- In the March 2, 2026 hearing, at least one resident argued "Charlotte doesn't want public wastewater, public water facilities," framing natural constraints as a welcome check on growth — [SB minutes Mar 2, 2026](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/4-March_2_2026_DRAFT_SELECTBOARD__MEETING_MINUTES_.pdf). This is public comment, not town policy.
