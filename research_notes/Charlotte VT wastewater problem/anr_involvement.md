## Old Brick Store (290 Ferry Rd, West Charlotte Village): how the wastewater and water supply failed, and ANR's Notice of Alleged Violation

### Takeaway
The Old Brick Store's off-site mound disposal system on a neighbor's land was about 30 years old (installed around 1993) and failed hydraulically. On Mar 26, 2026, the DEC Regional Engineer (Frederic "Freddy" Larsen) saw effluent "immediately rise to the surface and spill over" when the pump station kicked on. The tankage also had no required grease tank, even though the store serves food. Neighbors complained on Apr 1, 2026. Their complaint also argued that the store's design flow was undercounted (440 gpd claimed vs about 880 gpd by their calculation) and that the store was operating an unpermitted transient non-community public water system.

On Apr 17, 2026, DEC's Environmental Compliance Division issued a Notice of Alleged Violation (NOAV, case 26EC00247). It cited two things: operating a failed wastewater system (Wastewater System and Potable Water Supply Rules §1-301(a)(12)), and operating a public water system without a permit (Water Supply Rule, EPR Ch. 21 §3.0.1). It set deadlines of May 1 (contact staff), June 1, 2026 (file applications) and Nov 1, 2026 (replacement system built). ANR issued replacement permit WW-4-0624-3 on Sept 18, 2026: a new mound in the same footprint at 440 gpd, with a grease tank, a second 1,500-gal settling tank and HighStrength-FAST 3.0 pretreatment. ANR did not order the store to close.

### Cited Findings

**How and when the failure was found (chronology)**
- **Mar 11, 2026:** The store's engineer, John Pitrowiski (Chief Engineer, Bowman, Williston), wrote to DEC Regional Engineer Frederic Larsen. He said WW-4-0624-1 was issued 3/19/99 and a "WW amendment for a change in use and no increase in flows" was dated 9/29/23. He said the store "is considering hooking to municipal sewer which isn't too far away or upgrading there existing septic system." The site is "silty sandy loam… No evidence of a groundwater table to about 40 inches." The project "is approved for a 1000-gallon septic tank and 1000-gallon pump station on the neighbor's land… The primary mound septic area is also on a neighboring parcel along with an approved mound replacement site." He proposed replacing the 1,000-gal septic tank with "a 2-chamber grease trap / septic tank" and rebuilding the mound or building a new one in the replacement area. He asked DEC to confirm that pretreatment would not be required, "given the flows are 440 gpd and the project has operated without pre-treatment since 1999" — [Bowman to DEC email, Mar 11, 2026](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_RE_%2023-111%20Brick%20Store.pdf)
- **Mar 12, 2026:** Larsen replied: "I won't 'require' pretreatment. I will require 1-805 (wastewater strength) to be addressed… FOG and TSS need to be checked too. Might be an issue currently given the lack of a grease tank." Replacing the tank with a combination grease/septic tank could be a "minor repair or replacement," but rebuilding the mound needs at least one soil excavation under §1-910(f)(3) — [same thread](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_RE_%2023-111%20Brick%20Store.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Mode of failure:** The documents support a hydraulic and biological failure of the ~30-year-old off-site disposal field. Effluent surfaced on pumping, and the SHWT was only 16–24" below grade. The lack of a grease tank in a food-service operation probably contributed: Larsen's stated concern was "hot grease… prematurely failing the new mound." No document shows ANR finding that flow above 440 gpd caused the failure. That theory comes from the neighbors.
- **Pumping data:** ANR's design reviewer had the store's water-meter data (supplied to the drinking-water staff) and insisted on annual meter reporting. This suggests ANR wants to verify actual use against the 440 gpd permit.
- **Description inconsistency:** The application form says "existing trenches will be removed and replaced with a mound bed," while other documents describe the existing system as a mound. Either the existing "mound" was trench-in-mound or the form is imprecise.
- **Delegation link:** WW-4-0624-2 (Sept 29, 2023) was issued during Charlotte's delegated-permitting era, about two months before the town voted (Nov 27, 2023) to give up delegation. ## Permits covering Charlotte's town and school systems (Thompson's Point, West Village, Charlotte Central School)

### Takeaway
Thompson's Point and Charlotte Central School each hold state Indirect Discharge (ID) permits from DEC's Indirect Discharge/UIC program, which treats them as discharges to groundwater and indirectly to Lake Champlain or a LaPlatte tributary:

## Why Charlotte returned permitting delegation to the State, and what changed

### Takeaway
On Nov 27, 2023, the Selectboard authorized the Zoning Administrator and Wastewater Control Officer, Aaron Brown, to give ANR 90 days' notice ending the town's 2007 delegation. The Dec 11, 2023 letter to ANR Secretary Julie Moore gave cost as the only stated reason: "Maintaining delegation… involves ongoing costs that are unsustainable." ANR took over permits, compliance and enforcement on Mar 1, 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Dec 11, 2023 letter from Aaron Brown to Secretary Julie Moore (cc Bruce Douglas, Wastewater Program Manager): "On November 27, 2023, the Charlotte Selectboard voted to authorize… Aaron Brown to send such written notice… We are proposing to terminate our delegated authority effective April 1, 2024. Brown sent notice to Bruce Douglas on November 28 and has since met with ANR staff… Maintaining delegation, however, involves ongoing costs that are unsustainable." The delegation agreement required 90 days' notice — [Delegation termination letter](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Delegation_Termination_Letter_for_SB_Approval.pdf)
- Since Mar 1, 2024, DWGWPD has handled wastewater permits, compliance and enforcement. Town records cover 2007 – Feb 2024 (at Town Hall); pre-2007 permits are at DEC Rutland. The town page lists Frederic Larsen at the Rutland North office and also refers applicants to the "Essex Regional Office" (this is inconsistent) — [Town wastewater page](https://charlottevt.org/wastewater). Larsen's 2026 emails are signed from 271 North Main St, Rutland ([DEC email](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_Old%20Brick%20Store%2C%20Charlotte.pdf)).
- Under delegation the town issued "WW-138-" numbered permits; after 2024 the state uses "WW-4-" numbers (e.g., WW-4-6215, WW-4-0624-3) — [ANR WWDocs search](https://anrweb.vt.gov/DEC/WWDocs/Default.aspx)
- Under delegation, the town hired a contract engineer (Eli/Elias Erwin, Landmark Engineering) for independent review. Neighbors say the town said the cost of independent review was high — [neighbor complaint, Apr 1, 2026](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_FW_%20urgent%20need%20to%20review%20permit%20impacting%20ground%20water%20and%20health%283%29.pdf) (allegation)
- Statewide context, already in the other notes file: DEC's Jan 2025 Act 47 report says Charlotte and Colchester both returned delegation, citing administrative and technical review problems and cost, and that ANR found the records handed back incomplete — see vermont_context.md.

### Inferences
- The Old Brick Store case is the first visible test of the post-delegation regime. A permit amended under town delegation in Sept 2023 was later handled by state staff: a regional engineer's site visit, a referral to the central enforcement office, a formal NOAV, and multi-program review across wastewater and drinking water. Neighbors explicitly argued that "State would have caught these issues immediately."
