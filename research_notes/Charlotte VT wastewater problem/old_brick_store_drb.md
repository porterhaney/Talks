## PRIORITY: How the Old Brick Store wastewater and water-supply system failed (technical story from town documents)

### Takeaway
The town record confirms the failure and the fix. It does not explain how the system failed. No town document names a failed component (tank, pump, mound or leach field), and none mentions surfacing effluent, backups, a water test or a contaminated well. ANR's Notice of Alleged Violation dated Apr 17, 2026 says only that the store was operating "with a failed wastewater system" and "a Non-Community Water System without a valid Permit."

The replacement permit WW-4-0624-3, issued Sept 18, 2026 and recorded Sept 28, 2026, approves "the replacement of the failed wastewater disposal system to serve the existing food market with a bakery and deli." The new system is designed by John Pitrowiski, P.E. (Trudell Consulting Engineers). It adds an Innovative/Alternative high-strength pretreatment unit (BioMicrobics HighStrengthFAST model 3.0) and sets design flow at 440 gpd, with seats cut from 20 to 16. Construction must be finished by Nov 1, 2026. The water supply is to become a permitted public water system, WSID VT0021841.

The old system was an off-site mound system with an off-site well, permitted in 1993. The store's deed references 1993 well, water-line and sewer-line easements granted by George and Marilyn Richardson (and the Horsfords). The appellant David Kenyon bought his house from the Marilyn K. Richardson Family Trust in 2015. This suggests (unconfirmed) that the store's well and/or sewer infrastructure sits on or crosses the appellants' land.

### Inferences
- The fix points to the cause. The replacement adds a *high-strength* aerobic pretreatment unit with BOD, TSS and FOG limits, and reclassifies the use as "food market with a bakery and deli." This strongly suggests ANR and the engineer treat the store's effluent as high-strength food-service wastewater. The 1993-era mound system was designed for a general store plus apartment. Organic and grease loading from expanded food service is a plausible cause of failure, but no document states it.
- Seats were cut from 20 (2023 permit) to 16 (2026 permit), and deli and bakery flows were added while the total stayed at 440 gpd. The apartment bedroom no longer appears in the flow table. This suggests flows were reallocated from the apartment and four seats to cover kitchen and bakery uses not separately counted in 2023.
- The water-supply violation appears to be a classification issue: serving ≥25 people/day for ≥60 days/year makes the well a transient non-community public water system. It does not appear to be a contamination finding. The 2023 permit authorized only a "private" supply. The neighbors' reading supports this: "failed to account for the potable water flows the Store would require as a restaurant."
- Who owns the land under the store's well and septic is likely central to the neighbor conflict, but no document says so. The store's well and sewer rights come from 1993 easements over the Richardson (and Horsford) land, and appellant Kenyon bought from the Richardson trust in 2015. If the off-site mound or well is on the Beal/Kenyon parcel, a failed system on their land would explain the neighbors' strong interest. This needs land-record confirmation, because the Richardsons may have owned more than one parcel.
- The deadline is Nov 1, 2026, and the permit was recorded Sept 28. Construction was presumably expected in October 2026. No document confirms construction has started.

### Gaps
- No town document describes which component failed, when or how the failure was discovered (neighbor complaint vs. inspection vs. sale/test), or what the engineer said caused it. The Pitrowiski/Trudell application and plans (C3-01 etc.) for WW-4-0624-3 are not in the town packet; they would be in ANR's WW permit file.
- I found no evidence in town documents of well contamination, water-test failures, or isolation-distance conflicts with neighbors' wells.
- There is no mention anywhere of connecting to the Town's West Village municipal wastewater system. The remedy chosen is an on-site/off-site replacement with I/A pretreatment. The Town system is a ~4,999–6,499 gpd system serving the town hall, library, senior center and fire station per [The Citizen, Dec 8, 2016](https://www.vtcng.com/thecitizenvt/community/town-pursues-increase-for-wastewater-system/article_8f950f3f-d613-5c47-8e35-eb9e5153c89d.html), which is background only.
- The location of the new disposal field (on-lot vs. the off-site easement area) is unknown.

## Neighbors' appeal: who, what, arguments, and the store's response

### Takeaway
Adjoining owners Sarah Beal and David Kenyon (2789 Greenbush Rd), represented by Peter G. Raymond of Sheehey Furlong & Behm, appeal the Zoning Administrator's Aug 20, 2026 refusal to bring enforcement (DRB 26-103-AZA). They argue the store violates its 2024 site plan approval and the zoning regulations. Their points are unmet parking conditions, no zoning permit, seating beyond 130 sq ft, de facto conversion to a restaurant, no loading area, performance standards, and the failed wastewater and unpermitted water system under LUR §3.16. The store (Liam L. Murphy, MSK Attorneys) answers that the 2024 decision is final and cannot be collaterally attacked, that food service is a pre-existing use, that seating is about 98 sq ft, and that wastewater is ANR's domain.

### Inferences
- The neighbors need a way to bring wastewater into a forum that lacks wastewater jurisdiction. Their route is Condition 1 (binding representations) plus LUR §3.16. The ZA and the store both say that is ANR's job, and DRB chair Russell signaled on Sept 30 that discussion would be "limited to items within the DRB's purview." The wastewater argument therefore looks weaker at the DRB than the parking and zoning-permit conditions, which the ZA herself conceded were unmet (Condition 4).
- The 2026 replacement permit cuts dine-in seats from 20 to 16. The neighbors may use this to argue seating should be capped, and the store may argue it confirms compliance with state limits.

## Remedies under discussion

### Takeaway
The wastewater remedy is fixed by the state: a replacement system with I/A high-strength pretreatment, 440 gpd, 16 seats plus deli and bakery, to be built by Nov 1, 2026, and a public water system permit (WSID VT0021841) for the store's water supply. Locally, the remedies are recording the CVFRS parking agreement, obtaining the Condition 5 zoning permit, re-striping the sixth space, and off-site staff parking. The neighbors want enforcement and, implicitly, limits on seating and use. Town-level parking and traffic fixes (a senior-center parking lot, striping, traffic calming) were debated in 2025 without resolution.
