## 1. How have local and regional outlets covered Charlotte's wastewater issue?

### Takeaway
Almost all of the coverage is hyper-local: The Charlotte News, a nonprofit biweekly, and The Citizen (Vermont Community Newspaper Group; Shelburne News is a sister paper). Neither covers wastewater as a standalone beat. It appears inside stories about the Selectboard, village planning and zoning, where it is treated as the hidden constraint on village growth. I found no Charlotte-specific wastewater stories in VTDigger, Seven Days or the Burlington Free Press. VTDigger has covered the same problem statewide.

## 2. Key events and dates (studies, Selectboard decisions, votes, grants, land and leach fields)

### Takeaway
Charlotte has three small town-run wastewater systems: Thompson's Point (1994), the West Village municipal complex system, and Charlotte Central School. The West Village system serves only town buildings. Three wastewater committees (2010–11, 2015–16, 2017–19) produced the Dec 16, 2019 sewer use and allocation ordinances (passed 4-1). No fee schedule followed until Apr 13, 2026 (passed 5-0). The 2023–2025 East & West Villages Project concluded that the villages need more water and wastewater capacity, but no new system, grant, bond, or land or leach-field purchase has been reported.

**System capacities (Town Plan, adopted Mar 6, 2018, amended Nov 5, 2019)**

The 2012 committee recommended keeping 435 GPD in reserve for these uses, leaving 1,462 GPD of permitted excess. It said another 1,500 GPD could be permitted, for a total of 6,499 GPD and "almost 3,000 GPD of additional capacity." ## 3. Stakeholders and arguments for and against

### Takeaway
On one side are planners and pro-village voices: former PC chair Peter Joslin, town planners Larry Lewack and later Lindsay Kahn, the wastewater committee's Dave Marshall, and CCRPC. They argue that shared water and wastewater is the precondition for any village housing and can be paid for by users. On the other side are residents and some Selectboard members (Frank Tenney most consistently) worried about cost to taxpayers, wells running dry, overdevelopment, rural or village character, and process. Voters have rejected expansion-flavored ballot items (2021) and approved modest density changes (2026).

### Cited Findings
**Arguments for more capacity and village growth**
- Joslin (Apr 21, 2022): without municipal water and wastewater, village growth will be "extremely limited." Richmond's experience suggests public systems do not cause runaway development. — [Charlotte News](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2022/04/21/opinion-where-do-we-go-from-here/)

## 4. Links to housing, Act 250/Act 181, village designation, school, library and town hall, Senior Center, businesses

### Takeaway
Wastewater is the hinge for every village-housing policy. The HOME Act (Act 47, 2023) gives density bonuses only where public water AND sewer exist, which Charlotte lacks. Act 181 (2024) temporarily exempts the village centers from Act 250, but development is still capped by septic and well rules. The existing West Village system serves the civic core (town hall, library, Fire & Rescue, Senior Center). The school has its own system, which is at its limit.

### Cited Findings
- HOME Act (Act 47 of 2023): in areas served by municipal water and wastewater, owners can build at least 5 units per acre. The town still controls allocation of public capacity through its Sewer Ordinance. — [FAQ, Nov 2024](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Charlotte_Villages_Project_FAQs_v4_-11.24.pdf)
- HOME Act duplexes are allowed townwide (except Thompson's Point, the conservation district and flood zones) "assuming adequate septic capacity to serve the total number of bedrooms." — [2025 Town Report (PC report)](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2025_WEBSITE_Charlotte_TOWN_REPORT.pdf)
- Act 181 (2024) suspended Act 250 review for certain developments in the East and West Charlotte village centers until Jan 1, 2027. The permanent Tier 1B opt-in (tax credits, state investment priority, up to 50 units on 10 acres or fewer without Act 250) was deferred by the Selectboard on May 27, 2025. In CCRPC's optimistic scenario, the six rural towns, including Charlotte, would absorb 3.8% of more than 10,500 county units by 2030. — [Charlotte News, May 29, 2025](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2025/05/29/selectboard-chooses-deferring-to-voters-on-act-250-exemption/)
- The 2022 CCRPC village-regulations audit found that most Chittenden County zoning allows about 2 units per acre unsewered and 12 units per acre sewered, and that wastewater is "a limiting factor for village density" (search snippet). — [CCRPC memo, Aug 29, 2022](https://studiesandreports.ccrpcvt.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Memo_VillageRegsAudit_Charlotte_20220829.pdf)
- Civic buildings: the West Village system serves town offices, the library, Fire & Rescue and the Senior Center, about 3,100 GPD. — [Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/COMBINED_-_Charlotte_Town_Plan_Amended_20191105(1).pdf)
- Charlotte Central School has its own system (10,250 GPD design, 6,000 GPD disposal) at its upper limit. The Town Plan also ties village planning to keeping CCS as an educational and community facility amid falling enrollment. — [Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/COMBINED_-_Charlotte_Town_Plan_Amended_20191105(1).pdf). A Mar 3, 2026 CVSD bond of up to $13M including CCS renovations passed 4,478–1,781; I found no wastewater component in the coverage. — [Charlotte News](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2026/03/04/voting-results-from-tuesdays-voting/)
- Businesses and mixed use: Scott Burns's 89 Ferry Rd mixed-use project, next to the library, was blocked because the ordinance allows residential allocation only for affordable or elderly housing (Mar 9, 2026). The 2021 LUR amendment allowing sewer lines from rural into East Village commercial districts was defeated. — [Charlotte News, Mar 12, 2026](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2026/03/12/selectboard-choses-natalie-kanner-as-new-chair/); [The Citizen, Mar 2021](https://www.vtcng.com/thecitizenvt/news/local_news_charlotte/voters-shoot-down-charlotte-s-two-development-proposals/article_4f702f4e-7d08-11eb-a49e-37491469b5b7.html)
- The Town Plan directs the town to "investigate a municipal water supply and wastewater system for the East and West villages" through a volunteer committee, and to "support the development of community based water and wastewater systems in West Charlotte Village." — [Town Plan 2018/2019](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/COMBINED_-_Charlotte_Town_Plan_Amended_20191105(1).pdf)
- Water is part of the same picture: neither village has a public water system (Joslin, 2022). Some East-side or northern properties (Tamarack Rd) can connect to the Shelburne Water District under a 1996 settlement capped at 12 hookups (Jul 2026). — [Charlotte News, Apr 21, 2022](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2022/04/21/opinion-where-do-we-go-from-here/); [Selectboard minutes, Jul 27, 2026](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/July_27_2026_DRAFT_SELECTBOARD__MEETING_MINUTES.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because the HOME Act's 5-units-per-acre floor needs both public water and sewer, and the town's own consultant in Feb 2025 called that "unlikely at this juncture," HOME Act density gains in Charlotte's villages are mostly theoretical. Remaining growth paths are small shared or private systems and allocation of the existing West Village spare capacity.
- The Act 181 interim exemption expires Jan 1, 2027. Whether Charlotte opted into Tier 1B is a near-term open question.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Charlotte's formal state "village center designation" status or Tier 1B outcome as of Oct 2026.
- I found no reporting tying the Senior Center or library expansions to wastewater capacity limits.
