# Willowell creative health options

*Addendum to "Willowell staff health coverage options." Prepared Oct 9, 2026. Sources are in `research_notes/Willowell staff health coverage options/creative_tax_verification.md` and `creative_models.md`. Nothing here is legal or tax advice. A benefits attorney or CPA should sign off before Willowell adopts any of it.*

## The reframe

Every option in the main report was a version of "Willowell pays premiums." For Willowell's staff, those options collide with Medicaid and the subsidies they already receive. Public money already covers most of a subsidized educator's premium. An employer premium plan either replaces that money (QSEHRA) or switches it off (an "affordable" group plan or ICHRA).

The better question is: **how does Willowell make staff materially better off without moving the one number the cliffs measure?** That number is countable income. Medicaid tests it monthly, and the federal premium tax credit and Vermont Premium Assistance test it annually.

There are two answers. Most of the money can come from a revenue source Willowell probably isn't fully using.

1. **Pay people in forms that don't count as income.** Federal tax law gives Willowell a set of compensation tools that are excluded from MAGI, the income measure Medicaid and the subsidies use. They raise staff's standard of living and leave their public coverage intact.
2. **Fund it from CCFAP, not just donors.** Act 76 turned the state child care subsidy into a revenue engine. It pays more than Addison County private tuition, and its eligibility now reaches far into the middle class.

## 1. A pay package that doesn't touch the cliffs

Each component below has been checked against its controlling authority. All of them are excluded from the income Medicaid and the premium tax credit use, unless noted otherwise.

| Component | What it is | Annual value | Who it fits | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Free or discounted pre-K for staff kids** (§129 dependent care) | Wren's Nest tuition waived as an in-kind benefit | Up to **$7,500 per household** (new 2026 limit, set in the 2025 federal budget law and not indexed) | Staff with children of preschool age | Pre-K only; kindergarten and up counts as education, not care. Both parents must work or study. Needs a written plan and nondiscrimination testing, which is easy at Willowell's pay levels. |
| **Retirement: SIMPLE IRA or 403(b)** | Employer match or contribution, plus pre-tax deferrals by staff | 3% match ≈ **$1,200/person** at $40K | Everyone, and especially anyone near a line | Must stay pre-tax; Roth contributions count as income. A SIMPLE IRA requires a 3% match or a 2% contribution for all eligible staff. |
| **HSA deposits** | Employer deposits into the HSA of staff who buy a marketplace bronze plan (all bronze plans qualify for an HSA from 2026) | **$2,000–$4,000/person** suggested | Subsidized staff above ~200% FPL, for whom bronze nets to ~$0 premium | Comparable amounts are required for all eligible full-time staff, or Willowell owes a 35% excise tax. Not for staff on Medicaid, because Medicaid disqualifies them from an HSA. Below ~200% FPL, the reduced-cost silver plans are worth more than bronze plus an HSA. |
| **Direct primary care, paid from the HSA** | Fiddlehead Family Health Care in Bristol: about $75–115/mo per adult, unlimited visits | Covered by the HSA deposit | HSA holders | **Willowell must not pay for direct primary care directly.** It would likely count as an employer group health plan, which could block staff subsidies and expose Willowell to a $100/day penalty. Fiddlehead reports it is near capacity, with a waitlist. |
| **Education and student-loan payments** (§127) | Tax-free payments on staff student loans or tuition | Up to **$5,250/person** | Staff with loans | Stacks with the state's $4K/yr loan repayment program. Covers the employee only, and can't be offered as a choice instead of salary. |
| **Dental and vision** | Through the chamber's VACEplus program (Northeast Delta Dental / DeltaVision) | ~$500–700/person (estimate; get a quote) | Everyone at 20+ hrs/wk | Doesn't affect subsidies. Any hospital indemnity coverage should be bought by staff after tax. |
| **Coverage navigation** | Free one-on-one help for staff through the Office of the Health Care Advocate (1-800-917-7787) | Staff time | Everyone, especially from 2027 | Medicaid renewals move to every 6 months and work requirements begin Jan/Mar 2027. Paperwork, not eligibility, is now the most likely way someone loses coverage. |

**Do not use:** cash "health stipends" tied to buying insurance, employer payment of individual premiums, or a premium fund that Willowell controls. Each of these counts as an employer health plan under ACA rules and carries penalties.

**Bonus timing has limited value.** Vermont counts "reasonably predictable" income changes, so a routine annual bonus may be spread across the year. Only a genuinely one-time, discretionary payment gets lump-sum treatment, and only for Medicaid.

### By staff situation

These are archetypes, not real staff. Willowell would model each actual person privately, with their consent.

- **A single parent near the adult Medicaid line** (~$37.7K for a household of 3). Free pre-K for an eligible child, a pre-tax SIMPLE IRA deferral plus the match, student-loan help, and dental/vision. Together that is potentially **$10K+ a year of added value with zero change to Medicaid eligibility**. Give a cash raise only when it's large enough to clear the line by a margin; small raises leave the family worse off. No HSA, because they are on Medicaid.
- **A single educator on subsidized marketplace coverage, $35–47K.** Above ~200% FPL: a bronze plan plus a Willowell HSA deposit, with direct primary care paid from the HSA. That turns a ~$0-premium, high-deductible plan into usable coverage. Pre-tax retirement deferrals keep them under the 300% line, where Vermont Premium Assistance stops. Below 200%: stay on reduced-cost silver and use the other components.
- **Above 300% FPL** (~$47K single). Cash raises are fine, apart from the small Vermont Premium Assistance step. The 400% line (~$62.6K) is well above Willowell's pay scale.

## 2. CCFAP: the revenue lever peers actually used

- **Eligibility is wide.** Families qualify up to **575% FPL** (about $190K for a family of 4). Families at or below 175% FPL pay nothing, and the weekly family share runs from $50 at 200% FPL to $425 at 575%. (DCF tables, effective Mar 22, 2026.)
- **The state rate beats Addison County tuition.** For a licensed-center preschooler the state pays **$439/wk full-time** (26–50 hrs) and **$240/wk part-time**, "regardless of the provider's established rates." Addison County's median private preschool tuition is **$300/wk** full-time and $178 part-time (DCF 2024 market rate survey).
- **Most enrolled families are worth more to the program.** A family of 4 at 300% FPL pays $150/wk and the state adds $289/wk. Many CCFAP-eligible families pay *less* than private tuition while the program receives *more*.
- **The UPK trap.** The 10 state-funded pre-K hours come off the CCFAP certificate. A 32.5-hour 8–2:30 day drops to 22.5 CCFAP hours, which is the part-time rate ($240) plus UPK (~$117/wk). **Extended care to 4:30 puts a child back over 26 hours, at the full-time rate plus UPK.** How Wren's Nest schedules and bills its day decides how much of this it captures.
- **Size of the prize:** about **$2K–5K more per converted child per school year**. Across ten families that is roughly $20K–50K/yr. This needs Wren's Nest's actual tuition, days per week and CCFAP mix to confirm.
- **Precedent:** Turtle Island walks every family through the CCFAP application. The Growing Tree in Addison County reports most of its families qualify. ABC & LOL in Cabot added health insurance after Act 76 rates took effect.
- **Staff families may qualify too.** That can pair with the pre-K benefit. Whether Willowell may waive a CCFAP family share as an employee benefit is unconfirmed; ask the Child Development Division before doing it.

*Privacy:* report the CCFAP uplift as one aggregate revenue line. Never publish how many families use CCFAP or their income bands. This is the same discipline as the Pond Brook aid rule.

## 3. Compliance item due now: Vermont Saves

Vermont requires employers with **2+ W-2 employees and no retirement plan** to register with Vermont Saves, the state auto-IRA. The penalty is **$75 per employee per year from Oct 1, 2026**. Vermont Saves defaults staff to a **Roth IRA**, which does *not* lower countable income.

The better move is to **set up a SIMPLE IRA (or 403(b)) instead**. That exempts Willowell from Vermont Saves, lowers staff MAGI, and makes low earners eligible for the new federal Saver's Match (up to $1,000/yr, based on contributions from 2027). A SIMPLE IRA starting Jan 1, 2027 needs the staff election notice out about 60 days before, so **in early November**. Until the new plan is in place, certify the exemption or register with Vermont Saves to avoid the penalty.

## 4. Bigger plays, each owned by someone else

- **Push Vermont to copy Connecticut.** From 2027, Connecticut pays **$1,000–1,200/yr per early educator** toward coverage of any kind (individual, small group or ICHRA), funded from a $10M slice of its early education endowment. Vermont's equivalent source would be the Act 76 payroll tax. No 2027 Vermont bill exists yet. Coalitions to join are VTAEYC with the Let's Grow Kids Action Network, the Vermont Early Childhood Advocacy Alliance, and the Building Bright Futures Addison Regional Council. Willowell's role is to supply a credible story and the numbers; it shouldn't run the campaign.
- **An Addison County early educator health fund.** This is legally viable only if independent: hosted by the Vermont Community Foundation or United Way, need-based, and open to educators at every Addison program. If Willowell funds, controls or earmarks it, it becomes an employer premium plan. Insurers also aren't required to accept charity premium payments, so get BCBSVT's and MVP's position in writing. First Children's Finance Vermont is the natural convener. Michigan's Nurture Benefits pool is the closest working model. This is a pitch to make, not a project for a board member's 8 hours a month.
- **A PEO for group coverage.** PEOs are legal and licensed in Vermont. Whether one can put Vermont small employers on an out-of-state large-group plan is unresolved, so send a written inquiry to the Department of Financial Regulation and 2–3 PEOs. This is a long shot that only matters once Willowell wants a group plan.
- **Reclassify Willowell as a school (public charity).** This doesn't unlock tax-free tuition for staff families at Pond Brook directly. It does make it easier for foundations to give and removes the tax on investment income, which matters once a benefits endowment exists. CPA review.

## 5. How this fits the campaign

"Every teacher can stay" becomes concrete in two stages:

1. **Now, without new donors:** the cliff-proof package plus navigation help, funded by the loan payoff and CCFAP. The board can adopt it in the 2027 budget.
2. **Campaign-scale:** a fully employer-paid group plan, from the main report. It is still the gold standard, but it's a choice about cost rather than the only way to help staff.

The package also tells donors a better story. Instead of "we can't afford insurance," it is "we redesigned how we pay our teachers so no raise ever costs them their coverage, and your gift turns that into full coverage."
