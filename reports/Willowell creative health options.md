# Willowell creative health options

*Addendum to "Willowell staff health coverage options." Revised Oct 9, 2026 to reflect the actual staff profile: single adults, roughly 25–38, almost none with children. Sources are in `research_notes/Willowell staff health coverage options/` (`cliff_mechanics.md`, `creative_tax_verification.md`, `creative_models.md`). This is not legal or tax advice. A benefits attorney or CPA should sign off before Willowell adopts any of it.*

## What changes when the staff are single adults

1. **Only one line means "free" healthcare for a single adult: adult Medicaid, at $1,835.50 a month of gross pay (about $22,026 a year).** That works out to roughly 30 hours a week at about $14 an hour. The staff "up against the max" are almost certainly the 75%-time roles at the bottom of the pay scale. Full-time staff earning $30K or more are already on the marketplace and pay something. *Have Tasha confirm whether "free" means Medicaid or a $0-premium bronze plan. If it's the bronze plan, there is no cliff at all, just a slope.*
2. **For a single adult the Medicaid cliff is a step, not a wall.** Just above the line, Vermont's subsidies are generous. At $23–26K the benchmark silver plan costs about **$48–75 a month**, with reduced cost-sharing:
   - **Silver 94** up to $23,475: $350 deductible, $1,100 out-of-pocket maximum.
   - **Silver 87** up to $31,300: $1,300 deductible, $2,700 out-of-pocket maximum.
3. **Every family lever drops out.** The free pre-K benefit, staff CCFAP, and the Dr. Dynasaur math no longer apply. The levers that remain are cash, student loans, retirement, HSAs, and dental/vision. The *CCFAP revenue* lever is unaffected, because it comes from enrolled families, not staff.

## The single-adult cliff, in numbers (2026, approximate)

These figures are my arithmetic from VHC's 2026 tables, not verified to the dollar. Assumptions: about 21% combined payroll and income tax on a raise (FICA 7.65%, federal 10%, Vermont about 3.35%), and the benchmark silver plan after federal plus Vermont subsidy.

| Annual pay | Coverage | Premium/yr | Worst-case medical bills | Take-home gain vs. $22K, after premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $22,000 | Medicaid | $0 | ~$0 | — |
| $24,000 | Silver 87 | ~$680 | $2,700 | **+$900** (healthy year) / **–$1,800** (bad year) |
| $26,000 | Silver 87 | ~$900 | $2,700 | **+$2,260** / –$440 |
| $30,000 | Silver 87 | ~$1,400 | $2,700 | **+$4,900** / +$2,200 |
| $32,000+ | Standard silver *or* bronze + HSA | ~$1,600+ | $10,150 on silver | 200% FPL line: out-of-pocket exposure jumps here, not premium |

**The rule this produces:** never move someone off Medicaid with a small raise. Either the raise clears the line by **about $4K or more**, or the value comes in forms that don't count as income (below). Either way, it's the staffer's choice. Vermont Medicaid is excellent coverage for a healthy 30-year-old, and some will rationally prefer to keep it.

## The package for single adults: two paths, each person chooses

### Path A: "Stay," keeping Medicaid and adding value that doesn't count as income

| Lever | Value | Why it fits this staff |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-tax retirement deferrals as a "cliff dial"** (SIMPLE IRA or 403(b)) | Staff decide how much of a raise to put in pre-tax to stay under $1,835.50 a month | Pre-tax deferrals are excluded from Medicaid and subsidy income (verified). Caveat: some Vermont caseworkers read gross pay from pay stubs, so staff should flag their deferrals. |
| **Federal Saver's Match** (from 2027) | Up to **$1,000/yr** deposited by the federal government into the staffer's retirement account, on $2,000 of contributions | It targets low single earners, phasing out at roughly $20.5K–35.5K AGI *(verify 2027 thresholds)*. Deferrals lower AGI, which raises the match. |
| **Employer retirement contribution** (SIMPLE 2% non-elective, or a 403(b) contribution) | ~$450–800/person | Excluded from income. It's long-term money, but for a 25–38-year-old with nothing saved it compounds for 30+ years. |
| **Student-loan payments, §127** | Up to **$5,250/yr**, tax-free and excluded from income | Probably the single highest-value lever for degreed educators in their late 20s and 30s. Stacks with the state's early-educator loan repayment (up to $4K/yr, salary cap $60K). |
| **Dental and vision** (VACEplus, through the Addison County Chamber) | ~$500–700/person | Vermont's adult Medicaid dental benefit is capped, so this adds real value even for Medicaid enrollees. |
| **Job-required outdoor gear, tax-free** | A few hundred dollars per person | A working-condition fringe benefit: for an all-weather forest program, gear is a genuine job requirement. Small money, real morale. |
| **Navigation help** (Office of the Health Care Advocate) | Staff time | Six-month Medicaid renewals and work requirements start in Jan/Mar 2027. Lost paperwork is now the biggest risk to a staffer on Medicaid. |

**Worked example (archetype, not a real person):** a staffer at $21K is offered a $3K raise. They defer about $2.1K of it pre-tax, so their countable income stays under the Medicaid line. They receive about $900 in cash, $2.1K into retirement, about $1K of Saver's Match, plus loan help and dental. They keep Medicaid, and their total comp rises about $4K or more.

### Path B: "Leap," a raise big enough to clear the line, with the risk covered

- **Raise by at least ~$4K**, so the staffer lands at $26K or more on Silver 87, paying about $75/month for a plan with a $2,700 out-of-pocket maximum. Converting a 75% role to full-time is the cleanest way to do this, if the work exists.
- **Once a staffer is above $31,300 (the 200% line):** they buy a marketplace **bronze plan, which costs about $0 net** after subsidy. Willowell deposits **$2,000–3,000 a year into their HSA**. That doesn't affect their subsidy (verified), it's free of payroll tax for both sides, and Willowell must give comparable amounts to all eligible full-time staff. The HSA can pay for **Fiddlehead Family Health Care** in Bristol, a direct primary care practice charging about $75–115 a month for unlimited visits. Fiddlehead reports it is near capacity, so check the waitlist. **Willowell must not pay Fiddlehead directly.**
- Path A levers apply here too: student loans, retirement, dental/vision, gear.

The loan payoff frees $29,685 a year, and the CCFAP lever below adds an estimated $20–50K. Together they fund this package, plus Path B raises for the lowest-paid roles, **without waiting for a benefits endowment.**

## CCFAP: the revenue lever (unchanged)

This applies to enrolled families, not staff, so the single-staff profile doesn't affect it.

- CCFAP now reaches families up to **575% FPL** (about $190K for a family of 4).
- The state pays **$439/wk full-time and $240/wk part-time** for a licensed-center preschooler, "regardless of the provider's established rates."
- Addison County's median private preschool tuition is **$300/wk**.
- **The UPK trap:** the 10 state pre-K hours come off the CCFAP certificate. An 8–2:30 day drops to the part-time rate, while extended care to 4:30 restores full-time.
- **Size:** roughly **$2K–5K more per converted child per year**, about $20K–50K across ten families. This needs Wren's Nest's actual tuition, schedule and CCFAP mix to confirm.
- Report it only as an aggregate revenue line, never as a count of families on CCFAP.

## Compliance due now: Vermont Saves

Employers with 2+ W-2 employees and no retirement plan must register with Vermont Saves or certify an exemption. The penalty is **$75 per employee per year from Oct 1, 2026**. Vermont Saves defaults staff to a **Roth IRA**, which *doesn't* lower countable income, so it is the wrong tool for this staff.

**Set up a SIMPLE IRA instead.** It is cheap, satisfies the requirement, and powers the cliff dial and the Saver's Match. For a Jan 1, 2027 start, the staff election notice goes out about 60 days ahead, so **in early November**.

## Two more ideas worth a conversation

- **Housing beats health insurance for this demographic.** For single 25–38-year-olds in Addison County, rent is likely their largest expense. Lodging an employer provides on its own premises, as a condition of the job and for the employer's convenience, is excluded from income under §119 and so from Medicaid and subsidy income. A caretaker or residence role on the Monkton land would qualify. *Caveats: strict IRS tests, Cameron owns the land, and it cuts against "we are not looking to expand." This is a long-horizon idea, not a 2027 one.*
- **AmeriCorps for entry-level roles.** Willowell's posted co-teacher role looks like an AmeriCorps position. Full-time AmeriCorps members get health coverage and a Segal award (~$7,395) usable on student loans. Confirm what that position actually includes and whether more slots are possible; federal AmeriCorps funding has been unstable since 2025.

## Where the group plan fits now

For single adults, a group plan is simpler than for families: employee-only coverage, with no family glitch. A **100%-employer-paid, employee-only BCBSVT plan** runs about **$10K (bronze) to $14K (gold) per person per year**. The §45R small-business credit refunds part of it for two years.

Staff on Medicaid could still choose to keep Medicaid; an employer offer doesn't change Medicaid eligibility. **This remains the campaign-scale "every teacher can stay" goal.** The package above is what Willowell does in 2027 while the campaign builds toward it.
