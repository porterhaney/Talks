# Frame OIA's Sale in Ranges, Not Points

Public information supports a range-based valuation of OIA Global, but not a point estimate anyone could defend. There is a usable gross-revenue series from Transport Topics, a headcount history, an acquisition timeline and good 2026 inputs for discount rates and multiples. There are no public figures for EBITDA, net debt, capex, working capital, segment mix, deal prices or LDI's ownership percentage, and these are the numbers a DCF actually depends on. Using public anchors, the hypothetical framework below puts normalized EBITDA at roughly **$21–41M (base about $30M)**. A cost-of-capital DCF at a 14–17% WACC values that at about **$74–228M EV (base about $129M)**. The 2025–26 transaction multiples imply more, about **$195–270M for the base case at 6.5–9x**. Where the business lands between those two numbers depends on how many buyers compete and how the sale is run. The bar for the sale is high. LDI most likely closed on **August 31, 2010**, and from then to October 8, 2026 the S&P 500 compounded at **15.3% a year with dividends reinvested ($1 became $9.94)**. From the August 31, 2011 date the user remembers, the figures are **15.1% and $8.39**. Matching the S&P on exit price alone would require roughly a 10x return on the original equity check. The fair test, though, counts every dividend and every add-on equity injection on its actual date (a public market equivalent, or PME). On that basis, $1M a year of distributions on a $10M check lowers the exit price needed to tie the index from **$99M to $54M**. Selling in late 2026 adds four problems: freight earnings at a Hormuz-driven peak, a tariff regime rewritten three times this year, a 10-year Treasury near 5.3%, and a CFO who left in May.

## Public data can frame OIA's value but cannot support a point estimate

The best public revenue source is Transport Topics' Top 100 Logistics ranking. The figures are submitted by the company and are unaudited. They show gross revenue of **$876M (2020 data), $1,156M (2021), $1,191M (2022), $732M (2023), about $770M (2024, a TT estimate) and $779M on the latest list**, which is labeled "data through Dec 31, 2024" ([TT 2026 rankings](https://www.ttnews.com/logistics/rankings/2026); [TT OIA profile](https://www.ttnews.com/logistics/companies/oia/2026); [TT 2023 rankings](https://www.ttnews.com/logistics/rankings/2023)). The 2021–22 peak and the 39% drop in 2023 track the freight-rate cycle. Revenue before and after the spike sits in a narrow band, so **normalized gross revenue of about $750–850M** is the most defensible starting point. The company's own figures are less reliable. A May 2026 release called OIA "a $1.4 billion revenue" company ([Yahoo Finance/PR Newswire](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/icat-logistics-appoints-youssef-annali-170000824.html)), but LDI says its *entire* portfolio, including minority stakes, generates "over $1.2 billion" ([LDI Two-Page Summary, Apr 2026](https://lacydiversified.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LDI-Two-Page-Summary-2026.04.pdf)). OIA therefore cannot be $1.4B on a like-for-like basis. TT's net revenue series is less useful than its gross series. Net revenue as a share of gross swings from 15% to 50% across years, which suggests the definition changed. Only the latest **$202M (26% of gross)** is plausible as a modeling input.

The rest of what a DCF needs is not public. The researchers found no disclosed credit facility, lender or covenant. The 2010 deal was a "recapitalization" that PitchBook classifies as a buyout/LBO, which suggests there was acquisition debt ([LDI release](https://lacydiversified.com/acquisition-and-recapitalization-of-oia-global-logistics/); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/58211-29)). None of the roughly 11 add-on acquisitions disclosed a price, including the six since July 2024 (Sandford Freight Ireland, BTi, Airmark, JF Moran, About Freight Worldwide and Cargo Services) ([OIA timeline](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/); [OIA: Cargo Services](https://www.oiaglobal.com/company-news/acquisition-cargo-services/)). PPP loan data gives a rough size for two of the targets. JF Moran's $951,660 first-draw loan implies about $4.6M of annual payroll, and Cargo Services' $416,700 loan implies about $2.0M. Both are small tuck-ins next to OIA's roughly 1,200 employees ([SBA PPP FOIA](https://data.sba.gov/dataset/ppp-foia)). Also, OIA is not mainly a packaging company. It is an asset-light freight forwarder and customs broker with a packaging and sourcing segment attached. Every acquisition since 2013 has been a forwarder or broker ([EIN Presswire, Oct 2025](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/858153394/oia-global-names-brandon-ruggles-chief-operating-officer)). That mix sets the multiple, the cyclicality and the working-capital profile.

**Verdict:** the public record is enough to bound the answer and set up the model. It is not enough to give a price. Each uncertain input moves value by tens of millions of dollars: EBITDA margin, net debt, the share of value held by minority owners, and how much of 2026 is peak earnings. The owners need to supply the items below before any number is shown to a buyer or to the family.

| Data the owners must supply | Why it moves the answer |

## A hypothetical base case lands near $130M, below the $195–270M comps band

**Everything in this section is illustrative and built only from public benchmarks. None of it is an OIA figure.** The valuation date is assumed to be December 31, 2026. The forecast covers 2027–2031, uses end-of-year discounting, and models on net revenue because freight cost is passed through. Net revenue is 23% of gross. The EBITDA margin on net revenue is anchored on Radiant Logistics, the closest public comparable by size, at **14.9% in FY2026** ([Finviz/Radiant release](https://finviz.com/news/391788/radiant-logistics-announces-results-for-the-fourth-fiscal-quarter-and-year-ended-june-30-2026)). Expeditors and C.H. Robinson, at about 25–30%, are treated as a ceiling. Capex and D&A are each set at 0.5% of gross revenue, consistent with Expeditors' FY2025 figures. Net working capital is 7.5% of gross revenue. That figure is unsourced and should be replaced with OIA's ledger.

The cash tax rate is federal 21% plus Oregon's 7.6% excise rate net of the federal benefit: 0.21 + 0.076 × (1 − 0.21) = **27.0%** ([26 U.S.C. §11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/11); [ZenBusiness](https://www.zenbusiness.com/oregon-small-business-taxes/)). Oregon's **Corporate Activity Tax** is modeled separately as an operating cost. It is $250 plus 0.57% of Oregon commercial activity above $1M, after a 35% subtraction ([Bizee](https://bizee.com/llc/oregon/business-taxes)). The model assumes the subtraction is taken on cost inputs, which are about 77% of a forwarder's gross. Taxable activity is then about 73% of Oregon receipts. On $800M of gross revenue, the CAT comes to about **$0.33M if 10% of receipts are Oregon-sourced, $0.83M at 25% and $3.3M at 100%**. At 100% it would absorb about 11% of base EBITDA. The base case assumes 25%. Apportionment is one of the data requests above.

The discount rate uses a build-up method, and every input is cited in the industry research notes:

- Risk-free rate of 5.3% ([Damodaran, Oct 1 2026](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/home.htm)).
- Kroll's 5.0% equity risk premium ([BVResources](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/bvwire/kroll-reaffirms-50-us-erp-sticks-with-treasury-risk-free-rate)).
- Damodaran unlevered betas of 0.71–0.87, relevered at a 25% debt-to-equity ratio to **0.84–1.03** ([Damodaran betas](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/Betas.html)).
- A decile-10 size premium of 4.47% ([SD PUC exhibit citing Kroll](https://puc.sd.gov/commission/dockets/electric/2025/EL25-022/Volume2/ExhAEB-1Schedule9.pdf)).
- A company-specific premium of 2–5%. This is a practitioner convention, not a sourced figure.
- Pre-tax debt cost of 8–10%.

These give a cost of equity of 16.0–19.9% and a **WACC of 13.9–17.4%, with a base of 15.5%**. Substituting Damodaran's 4.2% implied ERP in the low case brings WACC down to about 13.4%. Terminal growth is 2.0–3.0%, with a base of 2.5%. That sits below the Fed's roughly 4% long-run nominal GDP (2.0% real plus the 2% inflation target) ([Fed SEP, Sep 16 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcprojtabl20260916.pdf)).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $160M | 2%/yr | 13% | $20.8M | 3.0% | 17.0% | 2.0% | **$74M** | 3.6x |
| Base | $185M | 3.5%/yr | 16% | $29.6M | 3.7% | 15.5% | 2.5% | **$129M** | 4.4x |
| High | $205M | 5%/yr | 20% | $41.0M | 4.6% | 14.0% | 3.0% | **$228M** | 5.6x |

In every case EBITDA falls between 3.0% and 4.6% of gross revenue. That fits the 3–5% benchmark range, and it brackets the $25–55M sanity band in the financial-data notes. Here is the base-case walk for 2027, in $M:

1. Net revenue: 185.0. Gross revenue: 185 / 0.23 = 804.3.
2. EBITDA: 185 × 16% = 29.6.
3. CAT: 0.0057 × (201.1 × 0.7305 − 1.0) = 0.83, where 201.1 is the 25% of gross that is Oregon-sourced.
4. D&A, equal to capex: 0.5% × 804.3 = 4.02.
5. EBIT: 29.6 − 0.83 − 4.02 = 24.75.
6. Tax at 27.0%: 6.68.
7. Change in NWC: 7.5% × (185.0 − 178.7) / 0.23 = 2.04.
8. Unlevered free cash flow: 24.75 − 6.68 + 4.02 − 4.02 − 2.04 = **16.0**, discounted at 1/1.155 = 0.866 to 13.9.

Free cash flow grows to $18.4M by 2031. The five years sum to **$56.4M of present value**. The 2032 free cash flow of $19.5M capitalized at (15.5% − 2.5%) gives a terminal value of $150.1M. Discounted at 1.155^5 = 2.056, that is **$73.0M**, so EV is about **$129M**. The terminal value is 56% of the total. Mid-year discounting would add about 7%, to roughly $139M.

The comps give a higher answer. The comps researcher's 2026 framework puts a mid-market asset-light forwarder-and-packaging business at **about 6.5–9x normalized EBITDA in the base case, 5–6.5x in the downside and 9–11x+ with a synergistic strategic buyer**. The evidence for those ranges is:

- GF Data's private-equity averages of 6.8x for $25–50M TEV deals and 8.3x for $50–100M ([GF Data](https://gfdata.com/wp-content/uploads/Q3-25_GFData_ESOP_Report.pdf)).
- The H2 2025 logistics split of 13.7x for strategic buyers and 8.1x for sponsors ([Proventis](https://proventis.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-02-02_MA-Facts_Logistics_H2-final.pdf)).
- Veritiv's purchase of Orora Packaging Solutions at about 9.9x cash EBITDA ([Orora](https://ororagroup.com/system/downloads/files/000/000/982/original/2771985.pdf?1725403361=)).

On base EBITDA of $30M, 6.5–9x gives **$195–270M**. That is 0.24–0.34x gross revenue, inside the 0.2–0.5x revenue cross-check, and about 1.05–1.46x net revenue. The DCF only reaches the bottom of that band at a WACC near 11%. A WACC that low implies buyers are not charging the full size premium and company-specific premium. That is realistic for a strategic buyer that can strip out overhead, for a sponsor using leverage, and for anyone who credits OIA's roll-up engine. The DCF deliberately leaves out future acquisitions. Read the gap this way: **about $130M is roughly what the business is worth to a standalone owner bearing its full risk, and about $195–270M is what a competitive process could plausibly get**. The difference is synergy and competition between buyers, and the seller can only capture it by running a real auction. The equity bridge then subtracts net debt, add-on earn-outs, transaction costs, any working-capital shortfall against the peg, and the share held by minority holders. None of those amounts is public.

## The S&P 500 sets a roughly 10x hurdle on the original equity check

The date matters first. OIA's web post announcing the LDI recapitalization is dated August 31, 2011 ([OIA](https://www.oiaglobal.com/?p=394)). Four other sources point to 2010. LDI's own release is datelined August 31, 2010 ([LDI](https://lacydiversified.com/acquisition-and-recapitalization-of-oia-global-logistics/)). The Indianapolis Business Journal covered the deal on September 16, 2010 ([IBJ](https://ibj.com/?p=20502)). OIA's own timeline and LDI's portfolio page both say 2010 ([OIA timeline](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/); [LDI portfolio](https://lacydiversified.com/portfolio/oia-global/)). The most likely close is therefore **August 31, 2010**, and the 2011 post is probably a later repost. Both dates are shown below. S&P 500 levels come from Yahoo Finance: the total-return index stood at 1,752.55 on August 31, 2010, 2,076.78 on August 31, 2011 and 17,419.63 on October 8, 2026. The price index stood at 1,049.33, 1,218.89 and 7,765.36 on the same dates ([Yahoo ^SP500TR](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5ESP500TR/history/); [Yahoo ^GSPC](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EGSPC/history/)).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aug 31, 2010 (likely close)** | 16.10 | **15.33%/yr; 9.94x** | 13.23%/yr; 7.40x | 15.14%/yr; 8.69x |
| Aug 31, 2011 (OIA web post) | 15.11 | **15.12%/yr; 8.39x** | 13.04%/yr; 6.37x | 14.91%/yr; 7.33x |

The CAGR check for 2010 is 17,419.63 / 1,752.55 = 9.94, and 9.94^(1/16.10) − 1 = 15.33%. Benchmarks that better match OIA's risk set lower bars. From August 31, 2011, the Russell 2000 returned **10.8% a year (4.70x)**, Dow Jones Transports 10.5% (4.52x), Expeditors 11.5% (5.18x) and S&P 500 Industrials 13.6% (6.88x). From September 30, 2010, the Russell 2000 returned 10.7% a year (5.11x) ([Yahoo IWM](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/IWM/history/); [Yahoo IYT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/IYT/history/); [Yahoo EXPD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EXPD/history/)). On private equity, the Cambridge Associates US PE Index returned **15.71% net over 15 years**, beating its Russell 2000 mPME of 11.18% by about 450 bps ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/WEB-2025-Q3-USPE-Benchmark-Book.pdf)). Top-quartile 2011-vintage funds returned about 19.2% ([Dakota](https://www.dakota.com/resources/blog/top-quartile-private-equity-irr-benchmarks-by-vintage-year)). The customary 8% preferred return compounds to only **3.45x from 2010**. That is a floor, not a benchmark. The honest opportunity cost for a family office is the S&P 500 at about 15% a year. The like-for-like risk bar is small caps and transports at about 10.5–11%.

The single-check multiples overstate the hurdle, because OIA has thrown off EBITDA for 16 years and LDI may have put more money in. The right test treats LDI as an investor with dated cash flows. **Contributions (negative)** are the original equity check, every LDI equity injection for add-ons, and any rescue capital. **Distributions (positive)** are dividends, true economic fees, and net exit proceeds after net debt, earn-outs, escrows, costs and minority holders' share. The test then compounds each flow by the index to the exit date. OIA beat the S&P if and only if the Kaplan–Schoar PME is above 1, or equivalently Direct Alpha is above 0 ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/blogs/enterprising-investor/2014/evaluating-private-equity-performance-pme-vs-direct-alpha)). The exit price needed to tie the index is **Σ Contribution_t × (I_exit / I_t) − Σ Distribution_t × (I_exit / I_t)**, where I is the index level. Two rules prevent double counting. First, EBITDA that OIA kept and reinvested in add-ons, capex or debt paydown is *not* a distribution. It is already in the exit value, through higher EBITDA or lower net debt. Second, add-ons funded with OIA-level debt create no LDI cash flow; their debt is subtracted at exit. EBITDA is also not cash. Expeditors' receivables consumed $1.87B in 2021 and released $1.59B in 2022 as freight rates rose and fell ([Expeditors 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746515/000095017023005412/expd-20221231.htm)). OIA's distributable cash in the spike years was probably much thinner than its EBITDA.

The worked example below uses hypothetical cash flows and actual S&P 500 total-return year-end levels. LDI invests $10M at entry. Dividends are paid each December 31 from 2012 to 2025. The exit is October 8, 2026. A 2012 dividend dollar compounds 6.96x to the exit date. A dollar injected at end-2024 needs only 1.35x.

| Scenario (hypothetical) | Interim distributions | Index value of distributions at exit | **Exit needed to tie S&P, 2010 entry** | **Exit needed to tie S&P, 2011 entry** | MOIC at hurdle (2010 / 2011) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. No distributions | $0 | $0 | **$99.4M** | **$83.9M** | 9.94x / 8.39x |
| B. $0.5M/yr | $7.0M | $22.6M | **$76.8M** | **$61.3M** | 8.38x / 6.83x |
| C. $1.0M/yr | $14.0M | $45.3M | **$54.1M** | **$38.6M** | 6.81x / 5.26x |
| D. C plus $5M LDI add-on equity on Dec 31, 2024 | $14.0M | $45.3M (injection grows to $6.75M) | **$60.9M** | **$45.4M** | 4.99x / 3.96x on $15M |

At every hurdle the deal IRR is about 15.1–15.5%, which is the index's own return. That is how it should be. In scenario C with the 2010 entry, a $60M exit produces a 16.0% IRR, a PME of 1.06 and Direct Alpha of +0.5% a year. A $40M exit lags the index, with a PME of 0.86 and a 13.9% IRR, even though it returns 5.4x. The same $60M exit with a 2011 entry gives a PME of 1.26 and +2.1% a year. The deal date alone moves the verdict. Scenario D shows why raw MOIC misleads: late add-on money lowers the required multiple. The test can also run in reverse. If LDI's net proceeds after the equity bridge come to $150M or $200M, a single 2010 check, with no dividends, must have been below **$15.1M or $20.1M** for the sale to beat the S&P. For a 2011 check the thresholds are $17.9M or $23.8M. Every dollar of dividends raises those thresholds.

## Selling into late 2026 means peak freight, unstable tariffs, 5.3% Treasuries and an empty CFO chair

The biggest valuation issue is that 2026 earnings look better than they will stay. After the Strait of Hormuz disruption that began around February 28, 2026, Xeneta put Far East–US West Coast spot rates at **$8,346/FEU on October 1, up 344%**. The East Coast rate was $11,523, up 335%. Xeneta calls this the peak and expects USWC to fall to $4,500–5,500 and USEC to $6,000–7,000 within three months: "a sizeable correction, not a collapse" ([Xeneta via AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/xeneta-weekly-ocean-container-shipping-market-update-october-2-2026)). The way down also hurts. In 2025, a soft-rate year, C.H. Robinson's ocean adjusted gross profit fell 16.8% ([CHRW 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm)). Buyers will strip peak 2026 forwarding margin out of the QoE and use earn-outs to bridge the gap, as Radiant routinely does ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/radiant-logistics-acquires-california-based-forwarder)). Working capital pulls the other way. A close at peak receivables lets the seller argue for a lower NWC peg, or keep the cash released as rates normalize. That negotiation is worth real money.

Tariffs cut both ways. The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026 ([Plante Moran](https://plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2026/02/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs)). A 10% Section 122 surcharge followed, and from July 24 it was replaced by Section 301 duties of 10–12.5% on about 60 economies, 12.5% for China, with no sunset ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/trump-administration-imposes-new-section-301-tariffs-on-60-trading-partners)). The de minimis suspension was codified on June 24 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other)). Complexity is good for brokers: C.H. Robinson's customs gross profit rose **23.6% in 2025** ([CHRW 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000009/chrw-20251231.htm)). But revenue from IEEPA refund filings through CBP's CAPE system is a one-off that diligence will remove ([Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/update-on-ieepa-tariff-refunds)). China-plus-one sourcing shifts also threaten OIA's Asia-centric sourcing book, unless its supplier network can show it has followed customers to Vietnam, India or Mexico.

Rates raise the bar on both sides. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% on September 16 ([Federal Reserve](https://federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)), and the 10-year hit **5.31% on October 1, the highest since 2002** ([InsiderFinance](https://www.insiderfinance.io/news/10-year-treasury-yield-hits-24-year-high)). That pushes up WACC and cuts how much leveraged buyers can pay. Packaging-sector multiples fell from 10.3x in FY2025 to 6.5x in 2026 year to date ([Packaging Dive](https://www.packagingdive.com/news/capstone-partners-packaging-acquisitions-muted-2026/826759/)). Transports fell 11% from their July high ([Yahoo IYT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/IYT/history/)). Strategic buyers still have money. C.H. Robinson's pending $5.8B bid for RXO is priced at 13.2x synergized EBITDA ([CHRW 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001043277/000119312526413203/d115124dex992.htm)), and Radiant has a $100M acquisition accordion. The sale process should be built to bring them in rather than defaulting to sponsors at about 8x.

Two governance gaps need fixing before launch. CFO Youssef Annali left for ICAT Logistics in May 2026 after four years in a role that covered finance, corporate development, legal and real estate ([Yahoo Finance/PR Newswire](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/icat-logistics-appoints-youssef-annali-170000824.html)), and the current leadership page lists no CFO ([OIA leadership](https://www.oiaglobal.com/who-we-are/leadership/)). Going to market without a seasoned finance lead and a completed QoE invites retrades on price. Separately, LDI acquired "majority control" through a recapitalization ([LDI](https://lacydiversified.com/acquisition-and-recapitalization-of-oia-global-logistics/)). That leaves open whether the founder or management still hold rollover or incentive equity. Drag/tag rights and waterfall terms need to be settled first, because they set LDI's actual share of the exit. A process launched in Q4 2026 realistically closes in 2027. That means LTM figures carrying the Hormuz spike, and an S&P hurdle that grows about 1.2% for every month of delay at a 15% index pace. The researchers found no public sign of a sale process. LDI's stated posture is to grow its portfolio ([IBJ, Feb 2025](https://www.ibj.com/articles/mitch-daniels-joins-indianapolis-based-ldi-ltd-as-executive-partner)), so the owners control the timing.

## Conclusion

The two valuation methods disagree, and the gap between them is useful. A full-risk DCF says roughly $130M; market multiples say roughly $195–270M. The difference is what buyers will pay for synergies, leverage and OIA's roll-up engine, so the sale process will move value as much as operating performance. On the benchmark, the result turns on the ledger. A deal that looks like a weak 5x against a 10x S&P hurdle can actually beat the index once 16 years of dividends are credited on their dates. Conversely, an add-on spree funded with fresh LDI equity in 2024–26 can make a healthy-looking MOIC lag. The S&P is the right opportunity-cost bar. Small caps and transports at about 10.5–11% are the right risk-matched bar. The family should decide in advance which question it is answering.

On timing, the strongest move is to finish the QoE, hire or name a finance lead, and settle the cap table first. Then go to market with normalized, not peak, EBITDA and an NWC peg negotiated to capture the 2027 receivables release. Rushing a sale while a CFO seat is empty and rates sit at a 24-year high gives buyers the excuses they need to discount.
