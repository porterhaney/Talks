# Surfacing effluent exposes Charlotte's village wastewater bind

The wastewater problem on the Town of Charlotte's [2026 Development Review Board page](https://charlottevt.org/index.asp?SEC=%7B0EF50649-2F98-473C-92F7-93726C8E195A%7D) is the Old Brick Store at 290 Ferry Road. The store is a café and general store at the West Village's main corner, and its roughly 30-year-old off-site mound system failed. When a state engineer visited on **March 26, 2026**, effluent "immediately rose to the surface and spilled over" as the pump station kicked on. The store's tankage, which serves a busy dine-in café, had **no grease tank**, and test pits found the seasonal high water table only **16–24 inches** below grade. On **April 17, 2026**, Vermont's Agency of Natural Resources (ANR) issued a Notice of Alleged Violation for two things: operating a failed wastewater system, and running an unpermitted public water system. It set a **November 1, 2026** deadline to build a replacement. ANR did not close the store. On **September 18** it issued replacement permit **WW-4-0624-3**, which rebuilds the mound in the same footprint, adds grease separation and high-strength pretreatment, holds the design flow at **440 gpd**, and requires annual water-meter reporting. Adjoining neighbors are now using a zoning appeal to press the matter (DRB 26-103-AZA, hearing **October 14, 2026**). The town's position is that wastewater is outside the board's jurisdiction, so the hearing turns on that question. Behind the dispute sits a long-standing structural problem: Charlotte has no municipal sewer and only one small town leach field, its village lots are small with wet soils, and since March 2024 the town has handed all wastewater permitting back to the state. That leaves on-site fixes like this one as essentially the only option for village businesses.

The mechanism appears only in ANR's own regional permit files, which are public through the agency's wastewater document database. Most of the technical account below comes from those files.

## A 30-year-old off-site mound failed under a food-service load

### What the system was

The store sits on a **0.06-acre** lot in the Village Commercial district, and its building footprint is about 2,800 sq ft. That leaves essentially no room on the lot for a septic system. Its wastewater has therefore gone off-site since 1993. A 1,000-gallon septic tank and a 1,000-gallon pump station send effluent to a mound on a neighboring parcel, and a separate off-site well supplies water ([OBS Response, Exhibit C](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10465/OBSResponseLetterwithExhibitsFINALas_sent_8.19.2615060096.1.pdf)).

In March 2026 the store's engineer, John Pitrowiski, confirmed this arrangement to the state. The project "is approved for a 1000-gallon septic tank and 1000-gallon pump station on the neighbor's land… The primary mound septic area is also on a neighboring parcel along with an approved mound replacement site" ([Bowman to DEC, Mar 11, 2026](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_RE_%2023-111%20Brick%20Store.pdf)). The store's deed carries **June 16, 1993** well, water-line and sewer-line easements from George and Marilyn Richardson and from the Horsfords ([OBS Response, Exhibit A](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10465/OBSResponseLetterwithExhibitsFINALas_sent_8.19.2615060096.1.pdf)).

### How the permitted use changed while the system stayed the same

The system's permitted load stayed nearly flat while the building's use changed underneath it:

- **1999.** WW-4-0624-1 permitted a two-bedroom apartment over the store with "offsite sewage disposal 450 gpd" ([ANR WWDocs](https://anrweb.vt.gov/DEC/WWDocs/Default.aspx)).
- **2023.** Jolene Kao bought the store on July 19, 2023. Her company, KaoHaus LLC, then obtained WW-4-0624-2 on **September 29, 2023**. It cut the apartment to one bedroom and allowed "a maximum of twenty (20) public seats," for a total of **440 gpd** (20 seats × 15 gpd plus 140 gpd for the bedroom). It made "no proposed modifications to the previously permitted off-site potable water supply well and off-site mound-type wastewater disposal system." It also authorized the well only as a **private** supply ([OBS Response, Exhibit C](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10465/OBSResponseLetterwithExhibitsFINALas_sent_8.19.2615060096.1.pdf); [2024 DRB decision](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10463/DRB_23178SPA_KaoOld_Brick_Store_decision_APPROVED.pdf)).

The 2023 amendment therefore moved the existing gallon budget from residential use to café seating. It did not upgrade the mound or add grease handling.

### How the failure was discovered

The failure came to light during the store's own planning, not through a complaint:

- **March 11.** Pitrowiski wrote to DEC Regional Engineer Frederic Larsen that the store "is considering hooking to municipal sewer which isn't too far away or upgrading there existing septic system." He asked DEC to confirm that pretreatment would not be needed, since "the project has operated without pre-treatment since 1999."
- **March 12.** Larsen replied: "I won't 'require' pretreatment. I will require 1-805 (wastewater strength) to be addressed… FOG and TSS need to be checked too. Might be an issue currently given the lack of a grease tank" ([same thread](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_RE_%2023-111%20Brick%20Store.pdf)).
- **March 26.** Designer Amanda Raab invited Larsen to the site. His account, later sent to the enforcement officer, is the core evidence: "It is visibly failed, as when the pump station kicked on the effluent immediately rose to the surface and spilled over… We then went over to the store and checked out the tankage, which notably does not include a grease tank" ([Larsen to Nagy, Apr 17, 2026](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_Old%20Brick%20Store%2C%20Charlotte.pdf)).
- **March 26, test pits.** Hand-dug pits that day found a seasonal high water table at **16, 19 and 24 inches**, each with seeps and redox features ([Test Pit Log](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_4%20-%20Test%20Pits-v1.pdf)). Two weeks earlier, prior logs had been cited as showing "no evidence of a groundwater table to about 40 inches." Vermont's rules require **36 inches** of unsaturated natural soil below a leachfield, or a mound in its place ([DEC, Mar 2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Environment/Water%20Quality/W~Ben%20Montross~Water%20and%20Wells%20Presentation%20~3-25-2026.pdf)). A water table at under two feet leaves very little margin even with a mound.
- **Age.** The designer later called the mound "approximately 30 years old" and said it had "reached the end of its useful life" ([Raab to BioMicrobics, Sept 14, 2026](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_HSFAST%20Email.pdf)).

### The neighbors' complaint and DEC's response

On **April 1**, a group calling itself "Greenbush Neighbors," claiming to represent "the 5 neighbors impacted," emailed ANR enforcement, DEC staff and the Attorney General's consumer office. They alleged that the 2023 flow math ignored the 2,600 sq ft retail space and all food preparation. By their own calculation the true design flow was **880 gpd**, double the permit. They also alleged that the original system was installed without a permit and could handle only about 450 gpd; that the store's 575-ft well sits within 60 feet of another well and was never drawdown-tested; that the store lacked a transient non-community water permit; and that the amendment "was filed at the tail end of the delegation period" ([complaint as forwarded within DEC](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_FW_%20urgent%20need%20to%20review%20permit%20impacting%20ground%20water%20and%20health%283%29.pdf)).

Larsen told his managers the same day: "the existing mound is failed. There are clearly going to be a lot of eyes on this project." Enforcement Officer Dana Nagy took the lead "as this appears to be multi-jurisdictional" ([DEC emails, Apr 1, 2026](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_RE_%20urgent%20need%20to%20review%20permit%20impacting%20ground%20water%20and%20health.pdf)).

## ANR's April 17 notice set deadlines for May 1, June 1 and November 1

The Notice of Alleged Violation was issued under 10 V.S.A. §8006(b) by DEC's Environmental Compliance Division (case **26EC00247**) to KaoHaus LLC. It cites two rules:

- Wastewater System and Potable Water Supply Rules **§1-301(a)(12)**, which requires a permit for "the use or operation of a failed supply or failed system."
- Water Supply Rule (Environmental Protection Rules Ch. 21) **§3.0.1**, which bars operating "a Public water system without first obtaining the appropriate permit."

Its compliance schedule ran in three steps:

1. By **May 1**, contact Meredith Maskell about a public water system permit and Frederic Larsen "to discuss a plan for replacing the failed wastewater system."
2. By **June 1**, file a public water system permit-to-operate application and a wastewater and potable water supply application.
3. By **November 1**, "construct a fully compliant replacement wastewater system."

The notice requested a written response within 10 days. It warned of possible escalation to an Administrative Order (10 V.S.A. §8008) or a civil penalty complaint (§8019) ([NOAV, Att. 3 to Notice of Appeal](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Beal-Kenyon_Notice_of_Appeal_of_ZA_August_20_2026_Decision.pdf)). Delivery was slow. The letter was "stuck at the Vergennes post office," so on April 30 Nagy said she would deliver it by hand, after an earlier in-person visit to discuss "your mound system and the need for the system to be permitted as a public water system" ([Nagy email, Apr 30, 2026](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-2/Compliance/WW-4-0624-2_26EC00247%20KaoHaus%20LLC_5.pdf)).

**The notice has an address error.** It places the violation "at 57 One Mile Road (Span #138-043-11306)." That SPAN is the 290 Ferry Road store parcel, and KaoHaus's mailing address on ANR correspondence is **577** One Mile Road ([WW-4-0624-3 narrative](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_1%20-Project%20Narrative.pdf); [ANR cover letter](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_Permit%20Cover%20Letter.pdf)). The error is clerical. The parcel identifier is unambiguous and the store's counsel does not contest that the notice applies.

The water-supply citation is best read as a classification problem, not a contamination finding. The 2023 permit authorized a *private* well. A busy café serving the public all year falls into Vermont's public-water-system category. The neighbors framed it the same way: the store "failed to account for the potable water flows the Store would require as a restaurant" ([Notice of Appeal, Ex. A](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Beal-Kenyon_Notice_of_Appeal_of_ZA_August_20_2026_Decision.pdf)). A precedent sits a mile up the road. In 2023, Catamount at 823 Ferry Road cut its staff from 60 to 24 specifically so it could convert its public non-transient water system back to a private well ([ANR WWDocs](https://anrweb.vt.gov/DEC/WWDocs/Default.aspx)).

## Permit WW-4-0624-3 rebuilds the mound, adds grease control and meters the store

### The review

ANR received the replacement application on **June 29, 2026**, labeled "FAILED WW | Kao – Old Brick Store." It proposed that "existing trenches will be removed and replaced with a mound bed system in the same location" ([ANROnline submission](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_HQP-KQWX-VRV63-v1-SubmissionDownload.pdf)). ANR's July 6 receipt letter warned that "until a new system is installed and working, you are responsible for making sure wastewater does not come to the surface" ([ANR letter, July 6](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_Application%20Received%20for%20Failed%20WW%20Letter.pdf)).

Larsen's July 13 comments show what ANR actually demanded. He asked for wastewater strength to be addressed under §1-805. He said that "both a grease tank and septic tank are required per 1-909 (a) & (b)," because of the risk of "hot grease making its way out and into the pump station and prematurely failing the new mound." And he held that the 440 gpd total "can't be increased" ([Larsen, July 13](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Emails/WW-4-0624-3%20_%20Kao%20-%20Old%20Brick%20Store.pdf)).

The designer's June narrative had asked for two variances, both because of the cramped easement:

- basal area of 1,680 sq ft against 1,875 required;
- a curtain drain 16 ft from the mound against 35 ft required

([June narrative](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_1%20-%20Project%20Narrative%20and%20Variance%20Request-v1.pdf)). By September the design was called "fully compliant." A Darcy's-law basal analysis showed 12.53 inches of unsaturated native soil at the mound toe, and the curtain-drain setback was handled through a Reduction in Technical Standards under §1-912(e). The curtain drain is meant to lower the roughly 19-inch water table by at least 6 inches ([Sept narrative](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_1%20-Project%20Narrative.pdf)).

The designer explained why the old tank stays in place: replacing the tank in the neighbor's easement "would likely be strongly opposed." The existing 1,000-gallon tank was instead converted to a baffled grease trap. A new 1,500-gallon settling tank, a BioMicrobics high-strength aerobic unit and a new dosing station were added near the mound ([Response to State Comments](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_0%20-%20Response%20to%20State%20Comments.pdf)).

On **September 10**, Larsen settled the flow question. Without metered data he could not go below the table rate of 15 gpd per seat, and he would include "a condition in the permit for annual water meter reporting regardless." He noted he had "seen the rough water meter data that was supplied to the drinking water folks." The designer objected to the metering condition ([Sept 2026 email chain](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Emails/Re_%20WW-4-0624-3%20_%20Kao%20-%20Old%20Brick%20Store.pdf)).

### The issued permit

The permit was issued **September 18** and recorded in Charlotte's land records on **September 28** (Vol. 273, pp. 137–141). It approves "the replacement of the failed wastewater disposal system to serve the existing food market with a bakery and deli." Its main terms:

- **Treatment.** It names the BioMicrobics **HighStrengthFAST model 3.0**, an Innovative/Alternative "submerged fixed-film aerobic bioreactor" approved under letter #P-2004-01-R9C. Its design capacity is 6.6 lb BOD/day.
- **Effluent limits.** BOD₅ ≤ 300 mg/L, TSS ≤ 150 mg/L and FOG ≤ 50 mg/L. Missing these for a year makes the system "a failed system."
- **Inspections and reporting.** Monthly I/A inspections for six months, then twice a year. An annual Class 1 designer inspection that reports daily average and maximum **water-meter usage** by August 1.
- **Interim duty.** The owner must prevent wastewater surfacing until the new system is built.
- **Construction deadline.** **November 1, 2026**, matching the NOAV.
- **Water supply.** Connection to a public water system under WSID **VT0021841**, "when issued."

([WW-4-0624-3, recorded](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/WW-4-0624-3.pdf)). The permit therefore went out before the public-water construction permit existed.

### Seats: 16 or 20?

The recorded permit's flow table reads "Food Market; 16-Seats – 15 GPD/Seat; Deli – 100 GPD; Bakery – 100 GPD," which totals 440 gpd with no bedroom ([WW-4-0624-3](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/WW-4-0624-3.pdf)). Two other documents say 20 seats: ANR's database summary describes "no change in use to the existing 20-seat food market" ([ANR WWDocs](https://anrweb.vt.gov/DEC/WWDocs/Default.aspx)), and the September design calculations say "Food Market with Deli + 20 seats" at 440 gpd ([Design Calculations](https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WWDocs/Regional-Imaging/WW/WW-4/WW-4-0601_thru_WW-4-0800/WW-4-0624-3/Documents/WW-4-0624-3_5%20-%20Design%20Calculations%200901%202026.pdf)).

The arithmetic favors 16. Twenty seats at 15 gpd plus 200 gpd for deli and bakery would be 500 gpd, above the cap Larsen enforced. Two caveats apply. The recorded permit is an image-only scan that one research pass transcribed and another could not read. And the designer's correspondence calls the unit a "MicroFAST 3.0" rather than HighStrengthFAST. The seat count matters beyond ANR. If the state's operating permit now allows 16 seats, the neighbors' count of "19-plus seats" becomes a state-permit question, not just a zoning one.

### Construction status

As of October 10, ANR's "Installation Certificates" folder for the permit was empty, and no document confirms that construction has begun. Neither does any document show an Administrative Order or penalty. ANR's handling so far has been a cooperative compliance schedule, not punishment.

## Neighbors are pressing a state wastewater violation through a zoning forum

### The appeal

The appellants are Sarah Beal and David Kenyon of 2789 Greenbush Road, represented by Peter Raymond of Sheehey Furlong & Behm. On July 28 they asked the Zoning Administrator to enforce against the store. She refused on August 20, and they appealed on September 4 (DRB 26-103-AZA), seeking to "reverse and find store in violation" ([Appeal Application](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10323/2026AppealApplication.pdf)). Their appeal stacks five grounds:

1. Unmet parking conditions: five on-site spaces instead of six, and no recorded agreement for the two spaces at the fire station.
2. No zoning permit under Condition 5 of the January 2024 approval.
3. A failed wastewater system and unpermitted water system, in breach of land-use rule §3.16(A).
4. Seating beyond the 130 sq ft approved, amounting to a de facto restaurant.
5. Delivery trucks blocking the right-of-way, with no on-site loading area.

([Notice of Appeal](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Beal-Kenyon_Notice_of_Appeal_of_ZA_August_20_2026_Decision.pdf)).

### The jurisdictional problem

The wastewater ground has to get around a jurisdictional limit, and the appellants concede the point. "It is not within the DRB's jurisdiction to determine whether various State permits should be issued." They argue instead that the board "can and did rightly condition its approval" on the applicant's representations. Those representations include permit WW-4-0624-2, made binding through Condition 1. They also rely on §3.16(A), which bars converting a building to another use "unless adequate potable water and wastewater disposal systems are provided in compliance with" state rules ([Notice of Appeal](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Beal-Kenyon_Notice_of_Appeal_of_ZA_August_20_2026_Decision.pdf)).

The store and the town reject this route:

- **The store's counsel**, Liam Murphy of MSK, concedes the NOAV but says "the appropriate forum for the wastewater issue is the ANR process already underway." He argues the 2024 approval is final and immune from collateral attack (24 V.S.A. §4472; *In re Hopkins*, 2020 VT 47). He says food service is a pre-existing use whose intensification Charlotte's own rules do not restrict (*In re Old Lantern*, 2017), and that seating occupies about 98 sq ft ([OBS Response](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10465/OBSResponseLetterwithExhibitsFINALas_sent_8.19.2615060096.1.pdf)).
- **Zoning Administrator Rebecca Kaplan** agreed that "ANR did not require the Store to cease operations" and that "it is not in the DRB's jurisdiction to oversee wastewater permits, therefore, a failed wastewater system would not cause a DRB decision to be violated." She did concede that the parking-agreement condition "has not yet been met" ([ZA Decision](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10462/ZAResponse_Letter_to_Request_for_Zoning_Enforcement.pdf)).
- **The DRB chair** pointed the same way in a September 30 training session. Asked whether wastewater should be discussed in the appeal, Chair Charles Russell said "the discussion is limited to items within the DRB's purview" ([DRB minutes, Sept 30](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/09-DRB_September_30_2026_minutes_-_draft.pdf)).

### Likely outcome

The record points to a split outcome on October 14. The wastewater ground looks weak before this board. The administrative conditions are where the appellants have the most traction, because the store has promised to record the fire-station agreement and obtain the zoning permit ([OBS Response](https://charlottevt.org/uploads/dm/10465/OBSResponseLetterwithExhibitsFINALas_sent_8.19.2615060096.1.pdf)). Any DRB decision can be appealed to the Environmental Division within 30 days. No court docket exists yet.

Timing creates a further complication. The Planning Commission holds a hearing on **October 15**, the day after the appeal, on amendments that rewrite §3.16 and replace local septic standards with "refer to state guidelines" ([Bylaw Revisions Memo, Sept 4, 2026](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Charlotte_Bylaw_Revisions_Report_Memo_09042026.pdf)). That would further narrow the local hook for the appellants' argument.

### Public reaction

The dispute has produced almost no press. Local coverage of the store has focused on traffic. In 2025 Beal told the Selectboard that conditions "got more dangerous… when the Old Brick Store changed from just a store to a store with a seated café" ([Charlotte News, Aug 21, 2025](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2025/08/21/talk-continues-on-ferry-road-traffic/)). No Charlotte News or Citizen story on the failure or the NOAV was found through October 10. The one supporter letter in the packet urges the board to let the store "continue operating as it does today" ([Lesser-Goldsmith letter](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/20261007_Lesser-Goldsmith_Letter_re_OBS.pdf)).

## ANR has held every Charlotte wastewater permit since the 2024 handback

The Old Brick Store case is the first visible test of a regime Charlotte chose. In 2007 the town became one of only two Vermont municipalities, with Colchester, to take delegated authority over state wastewater and water-supply permits. On **November 27, 2023**, the Selectboard voted to give that authority back. The December 11 notice to ANR Secretary Julie Moore gave cost as the only reason: "Maintaining delegation… involves ongoing costs that are unsustainable" ([Delegation termination letter](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Delegation_Termination_Letter_for_SB_Approval.pdf)). The letter proposed April 1, 2024, but the town says ANR took over permits, compliance and enforcement on **March 1, 2024** ([Town wastewater page](https://charlottevt.org/wastewater)). DEC later reported that both towns returned delegation over review problems and cost, and that the records handed back were incomplete ([DEC Act 47 report, Jan 2025](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Environment/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Department%20of%20Environmental%20Conservation~Report%20on%20Municipal%20Water%20and%20Wastewater%20Connections%20Permitting%20and%20Housing~1-30-2025.pdf)).

**Who issued the 2023 store amendment is unresolved.** WW-4-0624-2 was dated two months before the handback vote, and the neighbors insist the state "would have caught these issues immediately." ANR's file stores its plans under a "Charlotte Approved Plans" folder. Against that, it carries a state-style "WW-4" number, while delegated town permits used "WW-138" numbers ([ANR WWDocs](https://anrweb.vt.gov/DEC/WWDocs/Default.aspx)). No document read names its signatory.

## A small town leach field cannot rescue a failing village business

The store's March 2026 idea of "hooking to municipal sewer" ran into Charlotte's core problem. The villages have no sewer. The only shared system is the Burns leach field. It was built in 2002 for about **$125,000** and serves town buildings, and the 2011 committee counted about **3,102 gpd** of committed municipal use ([2011 Wastewater Committee report, Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)). In June 2024 the town's reviewing engineer reported **1,310 gpd** of uncommitted reserve ([SB minutes, June 10, 2024](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/June_10_2024(2).pdf)). The store's 440 gpd would consume a third of it.

The 2020 allocation ordinance states flatly that "the correction of failed private systems is not eligible for allocation consideration" ([Sewer Allocation Ordinance](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Approved_Sewer_Alloc_Ordinance-20200106.pdf)). That is so even though the 2011 committee had proposed a **900 gpd** failed-system reserve ([Appendix C](https://rebuild-charlottevt.govoffice2.com/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/AppendixC_OtherPlans_reduced.pdf)). The 2011 Phase I plan would have run gravity sewer 560 ft west along Ferry Road "to the Greenbush Road intersection," which is the store's corner, for an estimated $125,000. It was never built. Under the fee basis presented when the Selectboard adopted its first village fee schedule on April 13, 2026, connection costs **$48.85/gpd**, about **$21,500** for 440 gpd, plus about $1,221 a year per 100-gpd user ([Fee computation](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Sample_Wastewater_Quarterly_Fee_Computation.pdf); [SB minutes, Apr 13, 2026](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/April_13_2026_DRAFT_SELECTBOARD__MEETING_MINUTES_.pdf)). The rules even block a healthy mixed-use applicant. In March 2026 a 280 gpd request for 89 Ferry Road was tabled because residential hookups are allowed only for affordable or senior housing ([SB minutes, Mar 9, 2026](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/3-March_9_2026_DRAFT_SELECTBOARD__MEETING_MINUTES_.pdf)).

The town's own documents frame the larger issue as a growth constraint. "To accommodate more than a handful of new homes and businesses, more centralized water and sewage capacity would be needed" ([Villages FAQ, Nov 2024](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Charlotte_Villages_Project_FAQs_v4_-11.24.pdf)). The FAQ counts **15 homes** built in village districts over 20 years against 231 elsewhere. The final consultant report counts **29 over 30 years** against 452 ([Final Villages Report](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/20241017_Final_East_and_West_Villages_Report.pdf)). The Burns site could reach about **15,000 gpd**, but only under a state Indirect Discharge permit, which applies at 6,500 gpd and above ([Sewer Allocation Ordinance](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/Sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/Approved_Sewer_Alloc_Ordinance-20200106.pdf); [DEC rules, Subchapter 5](https://dec.vermont.gov/sites/dec/files/dwgwp/rorulesarchive/pdf/subchap5-6.08.16.02.pdf)). No design, cost estimate or feasibility study for that expansion exists. The 2026 Town Plan leans toward "nonmunicipal community based" systems ([2026 Town Plan](https://charlottevt.org/vertical/sites/%7B797C5C63-8973-48DF-A5F9-82DC6FAE7EDF%7D/uploads/2026_Town_Plan.pdf)).

Vermont's rules and funding explain why the problem is so common:

- **Scale.** Almost two-thirds of the state's historic villages (**219 of 335** in 2015) lack public wastewater, and direct discharge in the Lake Champlain basin is constrained by phosphorus allocations ([VTDigger, 2019](https://vtdigger.org/2019/12/12/lack-of-public-wastewater-systems-inhibits-rural-development/)).
- **Siting rules.** The 36-inch separation rule and a **100-ft** well-to-septic isolation distance, in place since 1949, consume small lots. Isolation zones also "overshadow" neighbors' land ([DEC, Mar 2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Environment/Water%20Quality/W~Ben%20Montross~Water%20and%20Wells%20Presentation%20~3-25-2026.pdf)). H.746, which would cut that distance to 75 ft, has sat in committee since January 2026 ([H.746 status](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/H.746)).
- **Cost of on-site fixes.** DEC puts the annualized cost of an innovative/alternative system like the store's at about **$3,000 a year**, against $1,933 for a plain mound ([DEC case studies](https://www.westminstervt.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Case-Studies-in-Community-Wastewater-Disposal-Systems.pdf)).
- **Funding.** The ARPA Village Water and Wastewater Initiative (about $30M) is closed, and Charlotte is absent from its project list ([Kamman, May 2025](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Appropriations/Other%20Topics/ARPA/W~Neil%20Kamman~DEC%20ARPA%20Overview~5-20-2025.pdf)). DEC concedes that existing programs "are not designed to accommodate growth" ([DEC Act 47 report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Environment/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Department%20of%20Environmental%20Conservation~Report%20on%20Municipal%20Water%20and%20Wastewater%20Connections%20Permitting%20and%20Housing~1-30-2025.pdf)).
- **Local politics.** The nearest analog is Westford, another affluent Chittenden County town. It held ARPA money and still lost its 2023 bond vote 488–532 ([WCAX](https://www.wcax.com/2023/11/08/westford-residents-vote-down-new-wastewater-treatment-system)), which shows that local votes, not engineering, usually decide these projects ([VTDigger, 2025](https://vtdigger.org/2025/02/25/to-add-housing-in-much-of-vermont-you-need-wastewater-infrastructure-local-opposition-can-kill-it/)).

## Conclusion

The Old Brick Store failure is less a story about one bad septic system than a demonstration of how Charlotte's village model works under stress. A historic commercial building on a lot smaller than its own footprint depends on easements across a neighbor's wet ground. Its gallon budget was rewritten on paper from apartment to café without any physical upgrade. When the mound gave out, there was no sewer to connect to: the town's ordinance bars failed systems from allocation, and the 2011 extension to that very corner was never built. The state has taken a pragmatic line. It cited the store, kept it open, rebuilt it under a tighter permit, and installed a water meter as the arbiter of the neighbors' 880-gpd theory. That metering condition is the most consequential detail in the file. From 2027 onward, annual meter reports will show whether 440 gpd is real, and a future amendment fight will turn on data rather than competing flow tables.

The October 14 hearing will probably not resolve the wastewater question. The board has signaled it lacks jurisdiction, and the next day's bylaw hearing would hand §3.16 standards to the state outright. The neighbors' strongest leverage is the store's unmet parking conditions and the seat count, which the new state permit may have quietly lowered to 16. For Charlotte, the case shows that after returning delegation, the town controls almost none of the levers that determine whether village businesses can grow. Those levers are state permits, state rules and a 6,499-gpd town field reserved for others. Until the town funds the long-deferred feasibility study and decides whether failing village systems deserve a share of its capacity, the next West Village failure will play out the same way.
