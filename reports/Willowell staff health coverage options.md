# Spend Benefit Dollars Where Subsidies Stop

The "max pay" cliff Willowell's teachers describe is almost certainly **Vermont's adult Medicaid line, 138% of the federal poverty level, tested on monthly income**. That works out to **$2,489/month (about $29,870/yr) for a parent with one child, $3,142/month (about $37,702/yr) for a parent with two, and $3,796/month (about $45,546/yr) for a household of four**. It has no spend-down, so a raise that tips monthly pay over the line ends free adult coverage. Children stay on free Dr. Dynasaur up to about $86,600 for a household of three ([DVHA 2026 Medicaid chart](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-MCA-PIL-FPL-Disregard-Chart.pdf)). Single educators lose free Medicaid at $22,026, so for them the lines are softer: **$31,300** (loss of low-deductible Silver 87), **$46,950** (loss of Vermont Premium Assistance, about $704/yr) and **$62,600** (loss of all federal help, about $9,350/yr) for 2026 coverage, each about 2% higher for 2027 ([VHC 2026 thresholds](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf); [VHC 2027 thresholds](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2027-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf)).

Several of the hoped-for answers turn out to be no:

- **No state option.** Vermont has no health plan, buy-in or subsidy for educators outside public schools.
- **No chamber medical plan.** Vermont shut down association health plans in 2020, and the 2026 bill to revive them died.
- **No grant program for health insurance.** No Vermont funder pays early-educator premiums by name.
- **No public evidence of "90% employer-paid."** No Burlington-area peer publishes that figure. Peers that do offer coverage have a sponsoring institution, a federal grant that pays fringe benefits, scale, or large new Act 76 subsidy revenue.

The most important number for the board is this: **public subsidies already pay about $10,000–$14,000 a year toward the premium of a subsidized single educator**. That is about the board's own $14K-per-seat estimate. So any employer dollar that merely replaces that subsidy buys almost nothing. The QSEHRA the board floated ($64,500/yr for 10) would mostly do exactly that.

The do-no-harm path has two stages:

- **Now:** use the roughly $29,685 a year freed by retiring the loan for cliff-aware raises and for compensation that does not raise countable income (MAGI). Add dental and vision, free navigation help for staff, and revenue levers Willowell may be under-using.
- **Later:** if the board wants real employer coverage, pay 100% of a small-group plan, about **$99K–$144K a year for 10 staff** at 2026 prices, with a two-year federal tax credit (Section 45R). That needs a benefits endowment or pledge stream of roughly **$1.4M–$2.9M** on top of the loan savings.

## The "max pay" cliff is the parent Medicaid line, roughly $18 an hour full-time

"Free" adult coverage in Vermont means Medicaid. The adult limit is 133% FPL plus a 5% disregard, measured on **current monthly income**, not annual income. Adults who only qualify under the expansion category are "NOT allowed to 'spend down'" once they exceed it ([DVHA 2026 chart](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-MCA-PIL-FPL-Disregard-Chart.pdf)). The 2026 FPL figures govern through March 2027 ([VHC 2027 eligibility tables](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/compare-plans/eligibility-tables/2027-eligibility-tables)). Willowell's posted pay puts many staff right at the line:

- **Posted pay.** A full-time co-teacher gets a $30,000 living allowance plus a $7,395 education award, and an hourly role pays $17–$20 ([Willowell jobs](https://www.willowell.org/jobs)). That means roughly $26,500 to $41,600 a year at 30–40 hours a week.
- **Parent of two.** The adult Medicaid line is **about $18.13/hr at 40 hours, or $24.17/hr at 30 hours**.
- **Parent of one.** About $14.36/hr full-time.
- **Household of four.** About $21.90/hr full-time.

These hourly figures are my arithmetic from the DVHA chart. The research cannot say which staff sit in which band, and the board should not try to find out individually. Still, the "up against the max pay" description fits this line far better than any other. A single adult at 75%+ FTE is already above the $22,026 single-adult limit, so for singles there is no "free" coverage left to lose.

Crossing the line is a real net loss at small raise sizes. A parent of two at $37,700 pays $0 for coverage. At $37,703 the same parent moves to Vermont Health Connect (VHC) and pays about $68/month ($816/yr) for the benchmark silver plan, with Silver 94 cost-sharing (out-of-pocket maximum $1,100). A $1,000 raise nets only about $800–$900 after taxes, so it leaves the family behind. **A raise needs to be comfortably above about $1,000, and preferably $2,000 or more, to leave a parent clearly ahead** once cost-sharing and EITC/Child Tax Credit phase-outs (not modeled here) are counted. This is my calculation from the [DVHA chart](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-MCA-PIL-FPL-Disregard-Chart.pdf) and [VHC 2026 applicable percentages](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/Applicable-Percentages-2026.pdf). Because the test is monthly, Willowell's 11-month and 4-day schedules matter: pay concentrated in fewer months raises the monthly figure that gets tested. Children are not the issue. **Dr. Dynasaur covers children with no premium to $7,218/month (about $86,600) for a household of three**, and premiums are "suspended indefinitely" ([VTLawHelp Dr. Dynasaur](https://vtlawhelp.org/dr-dynasaur); [VHC Medicaid page](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/compare-plans/medicaid-and-dr-dynasaur)).

For single educators, and for parents already above the Medicaid line, the lines below are subsidy steps rather than "free versus paid":

| Line (single adult) | 2026 coverage | 2027 coverage | What is lost | Approx. size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adult Medicaid, 138% FPL | $22,026/yr | same through Mar 2027 | Free coverage | Entire premium |
| 200% FPL (Silver 87 cost-sharing) | $31,300 | $31,920 | Out-of-pocket max rises from $2,700 to $10,150; deductible rises from $1,300 to $3,750+ | Matters only for people who use care |
| 300% FPL (Vermont Premium Assistance) | $46,950 | $47,880 | 1.5% of income in state help | ~$704/yr (2026), ~$718 (2027) |
| 400% FPL (all federal premium credit) | $62,600 | $63,840 | Entire premium tax credit | ~$9,350/yr (2026), ~$9,670 (2027) |

Sources: [VHC 2026 thresholds](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf), [VHC 2027 thresholds](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2027-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf), [VHC 2026 CSR plan designs](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026_CSR_Plan_Designs.pdf), [33 V.S.A. § 1812](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/33/018/01812). The cliff sizes are my arithmetic.

Between these lines, the slope is steep but continuous. In the 250–300% band, every extra dollar of income adds about 17 cents of premium, which combined with taxes is an effective marginal rate near 44%. Some third-party guides say Vermont's cost-sharing help reaches 250% FPL. That is misleading, because Vermont's state Silver 77 and Silver 73 tiers are listed but "blocked" ([VHC 2026 thresholds](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026-Eligibility-Income-Thresholds-for-QHP-Subsidies.pdf)).

Five federal changes make raises riskier for staff in 2026 and 2027:

- **Enhanced premium tax credits expired December 31, 2025.** The House passed a three-year extension 230–196 on January 9, 2026 ([AHA News](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-01-09-house-passes-bill-extending-enhanced-premium-tax-credits)). Secondary sources say the Senate had not acted as of September 2026 ([Health Affairs Forefront](https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/extending-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-things-stand)). That is why the 400% cliff is back.
- **No repayment caps.** Starting with tax year 2026, H.R.1 removed the caps on repaying excess advance credits. A raise not reported to VHC is repaid in full at tax time ([CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/blog/marketplace-enrollees-could-face-surprise-tax-increases-next-year-thanks-to-the-republican)).
- **Medicaid work requirements.** They start January 1, 2027 for new applicants and March 1, 2027 at renewal. Caregivers of a child under 14 are exempt, and anyone earning $580 a month meets the requirement ([DVHA Medicaid changes](https://dvha.vermont.gov/medicaid-changes)). The real risk for Medicaid-enrolled staff is therefore **procedural loss** of coverage, through six-month renewals and paperwork, rather than substantive ineligibility.
- **Reporting deadlines.** Medicaid enrollees must report changes within 10 days and others within 30 ([VHC report a change](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/report-change)). Willowell should pair every raise with a reminder and a navigator contact.
- **No state backfill.** Vermont did not replace the lost federal help. DVHA estimated Vermonters lose about $65M in federal credits in 2026, and that premiums above 400% FPL "could more than double" ([DVHA Marketplace Report, Feb 2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Health%20Care/Miscellaneous/W~Adaline%20Strumolo~DVHA%20Marketplace%20Report~2-12-2026.pdf)).

## Public subsidies worth $10K–$14K per educator decide which employer options help

For 2026, the benchmark silver premium for a single adult is about $1,299/month, and Vermont does not vary premiums by age ([VHC 2026 rates](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/sites/vhc/files/documents/2026_PlanDesigns_FinalRates.pdf)). After federal credits and Vermont Premium Assistance, a single educator pays a net benchmark premium of about:

- **$117/month at $30,000**
- **$174/month at $35,000**
- **$303/month at $45,000**
- **$456/month at $55,000**

So **public money is already paying about $14,200, $13,500, $12,000 and $10,100 a year** toward those educators' premiums (my arithmetic from VHC tables). For singles below roughly $52K–$57K, the subsidy also exceeds the price of MVP's bronze plan (about $824–$862), so **a $0-premium bronze plan is already available**, at the cost of a high deductible.

This is the hidden reason simple employer benefits underperform here. Three rules link employer money to the subsidy:

- **Affordability.** An employer offer counts as "affordable" if the employee's self-only share is at most **9.96% of household income in 2026, or 10.22% in 2027**. An affordable offer cancels the federal credit. Because Vermont Premium Assistance requires federal eligibility, it cancels the state help too ([Mercer on 2027 percentage](https://www.mercer.com/insights/law-and-policy/2027-affordability-percentage-for-employer-health-coverage-increases/); [33 V.S.A. § 1812](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/33/018/01812)).
- **QSEHRA (Qualified Small Employer HRA).** It reduces the credit dollar-for-dollar ([PeopleKeep](https://www.peoplekeep.com/blog/offering-a-qsehra-and-individual-premium-tax-credits)).
- **VHC's stricter stance.** VHC goes further than federal guidance: "An employee cannot have an HRA and also receive financial help from Vermont Health Connect" ([VHC HRA guidance](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/node/147)).
- **Medicaid is different.** It depends on income alone. An employer offer appears not to cost anyone their Medicaid, though that is an inference to confirm with DVHA.

The workforce stakes are large. Building Bright Futures reports that **about 67% of Vermont early educators rely on Medicaid**, against 20% of Vermont adults aged 19–64 ([BBF Act 76 Monitoring Report, Jan 2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)).

The "do no harm" test sorts staff into four archetypes. The board can estimate how many staff fall in each without collecting anyone's private data, for example through an anonymous self-sort or a confidential session run by a navigator or broker:

- **A. Adult Medicaid:** parents below the household-size lines above.
- **B. Cost-sharing zone:** singles up to $31,300, or parents above the Medicaid line but under 200% FPL. These staff have low-deductible Silver 87 or Silver 94 plans.
- **C. Subsidized, 200–400% FPL:** paying about $174–$520 a month.
- **D. Unsubsidized:** household income above 400% FPL, or covered through a spouse.

| Employer option | A. Adult Medicaid | B. Cost-sharing zone | C. Subsidized 200–400% | D. Unsubsidized |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small raise that crosses a line | **Net loss** under ~$1K–$2K | Loses Silver 87 protection | Net loss if under ~$1.1K across 300%; very costly across 400% | Taxable gain |
| Raise of ~$2,500+ | Net gain even after leaving Medicaid | Gain; more out-of-pocket risk | Gain | Gain |
| QSEHRA (up to $6,450) | Small value (out-of-pocket costs only) | **Wash**: credit cut dollar for dollar | **Wash** | Full value |
| ICHRA, modest allowance | Can't use it; no harm | Opts out, keeps credits; no gain | Opts out; no gain | Full value |
| Group plan, 100% employer-paid | Can decline and keep Medicaid | $0 premium, but out-of-pocket max may exceed Silver 87's $2,700; check | Clear gain ($2K–$5.5K/yr premium plus richer plan) | Large gain |
| Group plan, partial share that counts as "affordable" for this employee | Declines | **Harm**: loses credit *and* cost-sharing help | Neutral to harmful | Gain |
| Employer HSA deposit for bronze/HDHP enrollees | Not eligible | Gain, but bronze gives up Silver 87 | Gain; no credit reduction (inferred) | Gain |
| Dental/vision (VACEplus) | Gain | Gain | Gain | Gain |

Cell judgments are my synthesis of the [VHC](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/node/147), [IRS/Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/insights/law-and-policy/2027-affordability-percentage-for-employer-health-coverage-increases/) and [PeopleKeep](https://www.peoplekeep.com/blog/offering-a-qsehra-and-individual-premium-tax-credits) rules.

Two patterns emerge:

- **Only a 100% employer-paid plan reliably beats public subsidies for everyone.** Even it carries one wrinkle: a low-income heavy user of care might prefer Silver 87's $2,700 out-of-pocket ceiling to a $0-premium gold or bronze group plan with a higher ceiling.
- **Partial contributions are the danger zone.** A group plan where the employee pays, say, $200/month counts as "affordable" for a $30K–$35K earner. That earner then loses both the federal credit and the cost-sharing help, with nothing to fall back on.

Premium figures come from the [BCBSVT 2026 small-group chart](https://www.bluecrossvt.org/documents/2026-small-group-premiums-chart). The 45R figures are from the [IRS 2026 Form 8941 instructions (draft)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8941--dft.pdf). The prohibition is explained by [Bricker Graydon on Notice 2013-54](https://www.brickergraydon.com/benefits-insights/the-clock-runs-out-on-employers-reimbursing-individual-insurance-premiums).

**Raises, done cliff-aware, are the right first use of the loan savings.** Unconditional raises are the alternative the IRS itself points to for small employers that cannot run a premium-reimbursement plan ([Church Law & Tax on Notice 2015-17](https://www.churchlawandtax.com/irs-developments/10-key-points-on-the-new-health-care-payments-guidance/)). The board's figure of about $2,970 per person clears the Medicaid and 300% FPL break-evens for every archetype. The one exception is someone sitting just below the 400% line, where a raise could cost more than $9,000 in lost credits. Two rules follow from the table above: avoid raises of a few hundred dollars that tip a parent off Medicaid, and time raises with a navigator conversation.

Willowell can also shift some compensation into forms that do not count toward MAGI, the income figure Medicaid and the marketplace use:

- employer-paid dental and vision premiums
- employer HSA deposits, which the research infers are pre-tax and do not reduce the credit ([NATP on OBBBA HSA changes](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/new-rules-for-hsa-eligibility-and-tax-benefits/))
- retirement contributions. Peers such as Otter Creek and North Branch Nature Center added retirement plans before health insurance ([VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2025/09/29/otter-creek-child-center-to-expand-amid-overwhelming-child-care-demand-in-addison-county/); [Conservation Job Board](https://www.conservationjobboard.com/job-listing-forest-preschool-teacher-montpelier-vermont/8230055187)). Under standard tax treatment, employer retirement contributions and employee pre-tax deferrals are left out of MAGI. That would let a parent near the Medicaid line take a raise without losing coverage. The research notes did not cover this, so confirm it with a CPA and the Health Care Advocate before relying on it.

Free, individualized "what happens if I get a $X raise" analysis is available from the **Office of the Health Care Advocate (1-800-917-7787)**. The Open Door Clinic in Middlebury lists an insurance assister ([VTLawHelp](https://vtlawhelp.org/insurance-costs-subsidies-0); [Vermont 211](https://search.vermont211.org/search/b874d6a4-3a02-5d0b-ada9-3364a7c853af)).

**The floated QSEHRA is the weakest option.** At Vermont's roughly $1,299 benchmark, even the $537.50/month maximum leaves the QSEHRA "unaffordable" for anyone under about $91,700 of household income. For those staff, it cuts their federal credit by the full monthly amount. Willowell would spend $6,450 per head while most subsidized staff end up roughly where they started ([HealthCare.gov QSEHRA worksheet](https://www.HealthCare.gov/downloads/qsehra-worksheet.pdf); [Take Command](https://www.takecommandhealth.com/blog/qsehra-rules)). Three more problems:

- **Notice timing.** Written notice is due 90 days before the plan year, so a January 1, 2027 start needed notice by October 3, 2026. One secondary source reads a late notice as triggering a $50-per-employee penalty (capped at $2,500) rather than voiding the plan ([myprivatehealthinsurance](https://myprivatehealthinsurance.com/qsehra-vs-private-individual-plans-for-micro-businesses-in-2027/)).
- **2027 limits** are not yet published.
- **VHC's stance** that an HRA and financial help cannot be combined adds friction.

**An ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA) is the do-no-harm version of an HRA, but for subsidized staff it also does little good.** An ICHRA has no cap and can differ by class (full-time versus part-time). If it does not count as "affordable" for an employee, that employee can opt out and keep the full federal credit and Vermont Premium Assistance ([VHC](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/node/147); [Thatch](https://thatch.com/blog/ichra-affordability-guide)). Because Willowell would offer no group plan, the minimum class-size rules do not apply ([Foley on HRA final rules](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2019/06/regulations-employers-reimburse-health-insurance/)). The catch is that an employee at $45,000 would need an allowance of about **$915/month** before the ICHRA beats their subsidy. Below that, subsidized staff rationally opt out, so only Group D staff benefit. ICHRA money also buys individual-market coverage, which since Vermont split its individual and small-group markets in January 2026 costs about **11% more than group coverage for gold and about 36% more for silver** ([Act 2 summary](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT002/ACT002%20Act%20Summary.pdf); [BCBSVT individual chart](https://www.bluecrossvt.org/documents/2026-individual-family-premiums-chart)).

**A 100% employer-paid small-group plan is the only option that gives every educator something real, and it costs five times the loan savings.** Willowell would buy directly from BCBSVT or MVP; small group now covers employers with up to 100 employees ([VHC Small Business FAQ](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/small-business-resources/small-business-faq)). Employee-only premiums in 2026 run:

| Tier | Monthly | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze | $827–$876 | ~$9.9K–$10.5K |
| Silver | $960–$1,022 | ~$11.5K–$12.3K |
| Gold | ~$1,190–$1,198 | ~$14.3K |

The board's $14K-per-person estimate is therefore a **gold** seat. The earlier figure of $63K for six people ($10.5K each) matches 100% of a bronze seat, or about 73% of gold. The Section 45R credit applies because Willowell is an organization described in 501(c). The terms:

- **Eligibility:** fewer than 25 full-time equivalents, average wages under about $68,200, a uniform employer contribution of at least 50%, and a VHC-certified plan backed by VHC's **205ER-TAX** eligibility letter.
- **Size:** up to 35% of the employer's premiums, refundable through Form 990-T, for **two consecutive years only**.
- **Cap:** limited to federal income tax withheld plus Medicare taxes.

The notes' worked example: 8 FTEs averaging $40,000 in wages, with Willowell paying 100% of silver, yields a credit of about **$27,300 a year**. The payroll-tax ceiling could bind, so model it from Willowell's quarterly payroll returns (Form 941) ([IRS i8941 draft](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8941--dft.pdf); [VHC tax credit page](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/learn-more/businesses-and-employees/business-tax-credit)).

A stand-alone small-group dental plan also counts for 45R. Do not claim the credit on dental first if a medical plan is coming: the two-year clock would likely start on the dental claim. That is my inference from the consecutive-year rule.

Two caveats on the group plan:

- **Real enrollment may be lower than 10.** Medicaid-enrolled staff and staff on a spouse's plan would likely decline, so the true cost could be closer to 6–8 seats.
- **Minimum participation and contribution rules are not published.** Carriers' Vermont rules need to be confirmed by a broker.

Level-funded alternatives, such as Trustmark's Vermont product, are not certified plans, so they don't earn 45R. With 6–10 people, one serious claim can drive a large renewal increase ([Trustmark](https://trustmarkbenefits.com/newsroom/press-release/innovative-level-funded-plans-vermont)).

**The hybrid HDHP-plus-HSA design is how a small employer can advertise a high percentage at a moderate cost.** Burlington Children's Space now lists "BCBSVT/MVP Health Insurance" with an "HSA account (accumulates monthly)" ([Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit-job/7685b3cc83d04768a7885d22b129aa60-preschool-teacher-500-sign-on-bonus-burlington-childrens-space-burlington)). Since 2026, federal law (OBBBA) treats individual-market bronze and catastrophic plans as HSA-eligible. It also allows direct primary care fees up to $150/month as HSA expenses ([NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/new-rules-for-hsa-eligibility-and-tax-benefits/)). That opens two paths:

- **Group version:** 100% of a bronze HSA-compatible plan (about $10.1K) plus an HSA deposit, costing roughly $2K–$4K per person less than gold.
- **No-group-plan version:** a subsidized single educator takes a $0-premium marketplace bronze plan, and Willowell deposits into their HSA to cover much of the deductible, without touching the credit.

Either version needs a benefits attorney's review of comparability rules, and of whether employer-paid direct primary care itself counts as a group health plan.

**Check AmeriCorps and the long shots.** The co-teacher posting's "$7,395 education award" has the structure of an AmeriCorps Segal award ([Willowell jobs](https://www.willowell.org/jobs)). If these are AmeriCorps member positions, full-time members are typically eligible for program-provided health coverage. That is unverified in this research. It could be the cheapest coverage Willowell can offer, or an obligation already attached to the grant. The long shots:

- **VEHI** insures "several private educational institutions" ([VEHI testimony, 2024](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/House%20Education/Teacher%20Support/Benefits/W~Mark%20Hage,%20Bobby-Jo%20Salls~Vermont%20Education%20Health%20Initiative%20Written%20Testimony~1-5-2024.pdf)). But 2018 testimony says members must be municipalities, which leaves its openness to a private nonprofit unresolved ([2018 VEHI testimony](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2018/WorkGroups/House%20Education/Bills/H.858/H.858~Laura%20Soares~About%20VEHI~4-25-2018.pdf)).
- **PEOs** such as Justworks advertise large-group master policies, but say nothing specific about Vermont ([Justworks](https://help.justworks.com/hc/en-us/articles/16971298765467-Large-Group-vs-Open-Market-Health-Insurance)).

Ask both in writing.

## Peers that pay 90% have a sponsor, a federal grant or scale, not a trick

No public posting, website, news story or tax filing shows any Vermont early childhood center paying 90% of staff premiums. Many Chittenden County employers advertise health insurance, but none publishes the employer share: Burlington Children's Space, Heartworks, Robin's Nest, Champlain Valley Head Start, the YMCA and First Roots–Wild Roots ([Heartworks careers](https://heartworksearlyed.com/careers); [CVHS](https://champlainvalleyheadstart.org/); [BetterTeam, First Roots](https://bewilder-vt.betterteam.com/nature-based-preschool-co-teacher)). A 2022 Burlington Children's Space posting reportedly offered "free MVP Health Insurance," but the link is dead and could not be verified. The "up to 90%" figure is plausible, but it comes from word of mouth. It almost certainly refers to **employee-only** coverage, possibly on a high-deductible plan.

The peers that do offer coverage share structural advantages Willowell lacks:

- **Federal fringe benefits.** Head Start funds fringe benefits through its federal grant, inside CVOEO's agency-wide plan. A 2024 federal rule would require Head Start programs to "provide or facilitate access" to affordable coverage by 2028. A May 2026 proposal would remove that requirement ([Federal Register 2024-18279](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-21/pdf/2024-18279.pdf); [Federal Register 2026-09383](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09383/restoring-flexibility-to-support-head-start-program-access)).
- **State grants with fringe built in.** Parent Child Centers funded by a state grant averaged **$8,528 per employee** toward health insurance in 2023, compared with $11,738 that the state contributes for its own employees. Two of 15 PCCs contributed nothing ([PCC Network funding gap](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/House%20Human%20Services/Parent%20Child%20Centers/W~Amy%20Johnson~Parent%20Child%20Center%20Network%20Funding%20Gap~2-9-2023.pdf)).
- **Scale and Act 76 revenue.** ABC & LOL in Cabot, with 65 staff, began offering health insurance in March 2024 using Act 76 rate increases ([Vermont Business Magazine](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2024/march/18/cabot-celebrates-new-child-care-program-made-possible-act-76)). Burlington Children's Space's program revenue roughly **tripled, from $357K in FY2021 to $1.14M in FY2024** ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/222533646)).

Stand-alone programs closer to Willowell's size mostly still go without:

- **Otter Creek Child Center.** Middlebury College routes more than $360K a year to it through the United Way, and its wage floor rose to $20/hr, yet it still treated health insurance as a future goal in September 2025 ([VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2025/09/29/otter-creek-child-center-to-expand-amid-overwhelming-child-care-demand-in-addison-county/); [Middlebury Campus](https://www.middleburycampus.com/article/2025/10/otter-creek-child-care-center-to-reopen-after-months-of-renovation-aiding-child-care-crisis)).
- **Bristol Family Center.** It has 28 employees and ran an $81K deficit, with no public benefits information ([CauseIQ](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/bristol-family-center,223172076/)).
- **Crockett Academy.** A Williston director called an MVP quote of about $800/month per employee "not financially feasible." He added that "health insurance is a huge gap and more of a reason than pay that people don't take jobs" ([Seven Days, Nov 2025](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/education-news/vermonts-bold-investment-in-childcare-is-largely-paying-off/)).

The closest nature-based analogue, North Branch Nature Center, insures its permanent full-time forest teacher (at $40,179) but not its part-time co-teacher ([Conservation Job Board](https://www.conservationjobboard.com/job-listing-forest-preschool-teacher-montpelier-vermont/8230055187)).

What Willowell appears to be missing falls into three groups.

**Revenue the state already offers**

- **CCFAP subsidy revenue (Act 76).** Families earning up to **575% FPL** qualify. The state pays its rate regardless of a program's posted tuition, while private-pay tuition increases are capped at **5.4% for 2026–27** ([DCF Act 76 FAQs](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/DCF/Shared%20Documents/CDD/Act76/ACT-76-FAQs.pdf); [CDD tuition cap](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd-blog/child-care-tuition-increase-limit-0)). An audit of how many Wren's Nest families are eligible but not enrolled could be Willowell's largest single benefits lever.
- **Universal pre-K tuition.** It is $4,106 per child for 2026–27 ([AOE](https://education.vermont.gov/student-support/early-education/universal-prekindergarten-act-166/funding)).
- **QCIP.** This annual quality-and-capacity payment is roughly $2,800–$3,400 for a 5-STARS center of about 25 children. The 2026 window closed September 14 ([DCF QCIP](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/QCIP)).

**Supports that go directly to staff, at no cost to Willowell**

- student loan repayment up to $4,000/yr, now with a $60K salary cap
- T.E.A.C.H. scholarships for staff at 30+ hours
- professional recognition bonuses
- the early-educator apprenticeship

None of these pays for insurance, but they add to total compensation ([CDD staff funding](https://dcf.vermont.gov/cdd/providers/funding/staff); [BBF 2026 report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)). Whether these payments count toward MAGI should be checked against the cliffs.

**Tools that don't depend on scale**

- the 45R credit
- VACEplus dental and vision
- the Health Care Advocate's free navigation
- a benefits consultant funded by a capacity grant
- hard peer data, collected through the director survey in the checklist below

## Grants won't carry premiums; the loan payoff and an endowment must

The Early Educator Investment Collaborative's $9M in grants went to Colorado, Louisiana and DC ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/early-educator-investment-collaborative-makes-9-million-in-grants-to-address-unjust-compensation-for-early-childhood-education-workforce-301988571.html)). A realistic grant stack of several $5K–$25K awards covers one to three seats, not ten. Recurring premiums need recurring money. The endowment arithmetic:

| Goal | Endowment at a 5% draw | Endowment at a 4% draw |
|---|---|---|
| One $14K gold seat | ~$280,000 | ~$350,000 |
| The floated $64.5K | ~$1.29M | ~$1.6M |
| 10 staff at 100% gold (~$143.8K/yr) | ~$2.88M | ~$3.6M |
| Gap after the $29,685 loan savings, 10 gold seats | ~$2.28M | ~$2.85M |
| Gap after the loan savings, 10 bronze seats (~$99.2K/yr) | ~$1.39M | ~$1.74M |

This is my arithmetic, following the method in the grants research.

There is a useful reframe for the "Every teacher can stay" message. **Retiring the loan is itself the equivalent of a roughly $594K benefits endowment** at a 5% draw ($742K at 4%), because it frees $29,685 a year permanently. That buys about two 100%-paid gold seats or three bronze seats, or the cliff-aware raises described above. It does not buy coverage for everyone. If the campaign promise implies health coverage for all, the case statement needs a separately labeled "educator well-being" component. Small-group rates rose 4.4% (BCBSVT, 2026) and 1.2%–6.2% (2027) ([GMCB 2025 release](https://gmcboard.vermont.gov/sites/gmcb/files/documents/Press%20Release%20-%20Rate%20Review%20Decision%20-%2008.22.2025(1).pdf)), so a fixed 4–5% draw will lag premium growth. A hybrid works better: endow the base, and cover growth with three-year bridge pledges (for example, 5–10 donors at $5K–$15K a year) plus subsidy revenue.

Willowell's status as a private operating foundation shapes who can give. Individuals deduct gifts at public-charity limits. Donor-advised funds can grant without extra paperwork. **Other private foundations must exercise "expenditure responsibility,"** and some family foundations will simply decline ([26 USC 4945](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4945); [IRS deductibility codes](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search-deductibility-status-codes); [Council on Foundations](https://www.cof.org/content/taxable-distributions-donor-advised-funds)). With FY2025 revenue of $723K, contributions of $220K (30%) and the rest largely earned ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/030366363)), Willowell may qualify as a public charity, either as a school (Pond Brook, Walden Year) or under the 509(a)(2) support test, via Form 8940 ([IRS termination of PF status](https://www.irs.gov/node/801)). A CPA review is warranted before a benefits endowment starts producing investment income, which private foundations pay excise tax on. Grant applications should state Willowell's status up front so foundation funders can plan for it.

## No state plan exists for private educators, but 2027 opens policy doors

Vermont offers non-public-school educators only the generic options: Medicaid, Dr. Dynasaur, and VHC with income-based federal credits and Vermont Premium Assistance. There is no occupational pathway ([VHC eligibility tables](https://info.healthconnect.vermont.gov/compare-plans/eligibility-tables)). Public school staff get VEHI, the self-insured intermunicipal pool. The history of proposals:

- **2023 proposal.** Let's Grow Kids proposed making early educators "categorically eligible for a no- or low-cost silver-level health insurance plan through Vermont Health Connect," modeled on Washington ([LGK S.56 suggestions](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/Senate%20Health%20and%20Welfare/Bills/S.56/Witness%20Documents/S.56~Sarah%20Kenney~Suggested%20Changes%20from%20Let's%20Grow%20Kids~2-24-2023.pdf)).
- **Not enacted.** Act 76 did not include it, and the 2026 session passed nothing on educator benefits ([BBF 2026 session recap](https://www.buildingbrightfutures.org/what-the-2026-session-means-for-early-childhood-in-vermont/)).
- **The models elsewhere.** Washington's program ended December 31, 2023. DC's HealthCare4ChildCare, funded through a $70M-a-year Pay Equity Fund, is the live model ([WA DCYF wind-down](https://webdevpoc.dcyf.wa.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/WindDown-Guidance.pdf); [Zaentz Navigator](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/healthcare4childcare/)).
- **No pending revival.** Vermont's 2026 attempt to reopen association health plans (H.585, effective 2028 at the earliest) died in Senate Finance ([VTDigger, June 2026](https://vtdigger.org/2026/06/22/gov-scott-vetoed-the-legislatures-latest-attempt-to-lower-health-insurance-costs-whats-next/)).
- **What the chamber offers.** Today's chamber benefit is VACEplus dental, vision and worksite products, through the Addison County Chamber ([Addison County Chamber](https://www.addisoncounty.com/insurance); [VACE Insurance](https://vaceinsurance.com/)).

The advocacy hooks are concrete, and Willowell is a credible small-program voice for them:

1. **Cost-of-care model.** The First Children's Finance model already contrasts a current lead-teacher wage of $47,237 with an "aspirational" $76,372. Ask that the 2026 update add an employer health-contribution line to rate setting ([BBF 2026 report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)).
2. **Pre-K funding decisions.** H.955 defers key pre-K decisions to 2027. A BBF/AOE/DCF monitoring report is due December 1, 2026, and a cost-of-care analysis is due before the session. Push for a pre-K rate that includes benefits, and watch the risk that preschool enrollment shifts to school-based settings.
3. **Vermont Premium Assistance.** DVHA estimated a larger VPA for incomes up to 300% FPL would cost the state about $3.8M ([DVHA Feb 2026](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/House%20Health%20Care/Miscellaneous/W~Adaline%20Strumolo~DVHA%20Marketplace%20Report~2-12-2026.pdf)).
4. **Revive the 2023 educator-silver proposal.** The likely champion, Let's Grow Kids, reports it sunset in 2025 (verify), which leaves a gap that VTAEYC or BBF could fill.
5. **A pooled-insurance shared-services model.** None exists in Vermont. Raise it with VTAEYC and First Children's Finance.
6. **Background constraints.** The child care payroll tax is running about $11M a year below projections, with CCFAP about 99% spent, so new money needs a new source ([BBF 2026 report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Education/Reports%20and%20Resources/W~Building%20Bright%20Futures~Report%20on%20Act%2076%20Monitoring~1-20-2026.pdf)). The Governor's July 2026 executive order asks DFR for a plan by December 31, 2026 to allow age rating of up to 20%, which could reprice coverage for younger and older staff ([Vermont Public](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2026-07-08/after-vetoing-lawmakers-plans-scott-pitches-his-healthcare-ideas-again)).

## Conclusion

The board's question assumed the problem was finding someone to pay for a $14K seat. The research reframes it. For most of Willowell's likely staff, the government is already paying about that amount, so the employer's job is to **add value on top of public coverage without knocking anyone off it**. That makes the order of operations clear.

**Start with work that costs little or nothing:**

- stop raises that land just over a line, and make raises big enough to clear it
- route part of compensation into forms that don't count toward the income tests, after confirmation
- add dental and vision
- recover CCFAP and UPK revenue Willowell may be leaving on the table
- set aside the QSEHRA, which would mostly substitute employer money for federal money

**Treat a 100% employer-paid plan as a deliberate campaign-scale goal.** It buys stability against volatile federal policy and parity with sponsored peers, at roughly $100K–$145K a year, with the 45R credit as a two-year bridge. The loan payoff gets about a quarter of the way there.

The cheapest money of all is legislative. A Washington- or DC-style state subsidy for early educators would reach every Willowell teacher at once. Willowell's experience, a small private program whose staff earnings sit right at the Medicaid line, is exactly the testimony Vermont's 2027 pre-K and cost-of-care debates need.
